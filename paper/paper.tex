\begin{abstract}
We prove that, for every sufficiently small constant \(d>0\), one language in \(\ENP\)
disagrees on more than a \(d\) fraction of inputs with every
\(\SYM\circ\THR\) circuit having at most
\(c\,n^3/((\log n)^{21/4}(\log\log n)^7)\) top wires, where \(c=c(d)>0\),
at every sufficiently large input length. The symmetric
output gate is arbitrary and the threshold gates have arbitrary real
weights; with one top wire per bottom gate this is a gate lower bound.
Compared with Chen--Tal--Wang's \(n^{5/2-\varepsilon}\) bound,
this gives almost-everywhere constant-error hardness at a near-cubic
gate count for symmetric tops; the author's earlier preprints state
almost-everywhere gate bounds at \(n^{5/2}/\operatorname{polylog}n\), and
their proofs need the short-PCP repair made here.
It does not give a near-cubic gate bound for
general \(\THR\circ\THR\).

For periodic symmetric tops, agreement is below
\(1/2+1/\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\), still at \(n^3/\operatorname{polylog}n\)
top wires; one language covers all periods \(m\le n^{1/6}\) and all
prime periods \(p\le n^{2/11}\). Refinements trade size for smaller
advantages, down to \(n^{-\delta/4+o(1)}\) against \(An^{3-\delta}\)
top wires, for each fixed rational \(0<\delta<3\) and \(A>0\).

Like Chen--Tal--Wang's algorithm, our counter combines Kane--Williams
restrictions with Williams' batch evaluation, but it never
approximates the symmetric top: it tabulates exact residues of the top
score modulo primes near \(n/\operatorname{polylog}n\). Nested
integer-rate sampling and the discrete tomography of Hajdu--Tijdeman
recover short score distributions, and even-moment tests keep the count
sound for every guessed sample. The constant-error transfer weights the
Garey--Johnson--Stockmeyer gadget over Chen--Williams' three-query
PCPP; the periodic transfer uses finite Fourier analysis within
Chen--Lyu--Williams' almost-everywhere framework.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Main result.}
For one language in \(\ENP\), every \(\SYM\circ\THR\) circuit with
\(n^3/\polylog n\) bottom gates errs on a constant fraction of inputs, at
every sufficiently large input length (\cref{thm:main}). This raises the
gate exponent for depth-two threshold circuits with a symmetric top from
the \(5/2-\varepsilon\) of Chen, Tal, and Wang~\cite{CTW26} to \(3\), up
to polylogarithmic factors. A second result forces agreement close to one
half when the output gate's lookup has a short period (\cref{thm:periodic}).

Lower bounds for depth-two threshold circuits are a long-standing
frontier of circuit complexity. Chen, Tal, and Wang~\cite{CTW26}
recently proved the first superquadratic gate lower bounds:
\(n^{5/2-\varepsilon}\) for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), for both
\(\THR\circ\THR\) and \(\SYM\circ\THR\), against languages in \(\ENP\).
We study the symmetric-top class. Its bottom layer consists of linear
threshold gates
\[
 g(x)=\ind\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n a_i x_i\ge t\right],
 \qquad a_i,t\in\R,
\]
and its output applies an arbitrary Boolean lookup to the number of
bottom gates that evaluate to one.

This class is where the classical argument stops. For a majority
output gate, exponential lower bounds follow from the communication
complexity of inner product~\cite{HMPST93,Nis94}. But inner product
on inputs \((u,v)\) is the parity of the \(n\) threshold gates
\(u_i\wedge v_i\), so an arbitrary symmetric top computes it with
\(n\) gates. Thus the inner-product argument for majority tops does
not extend to arbitrary symmetric tops; the results here come instead
from a faster circuit-analysis procedure. Moreover,
Chen, Tal, and Wang build their \(\THR\circ\THR\) algorithm on their
\(\SYM\circ\THR\) algorithm~\cite[Section~2]{CTW26}.

\subsection{The circuit model and the headline theorem}\label{sec:main-results}

It is useful to allow integer coefficients in the top sum, charging
their magnitudes as repeated connections.

\begin{definition}\label{def:class}
A weighted symmetric threshold circuit has the form
\[
 C(x)=f(X(x)),\qquad X(x)=\sum_{i=1}^G w_i g_i(x),
 \qquad w_i\in\Z\setminus\{0\},
\]
where each \(g_i\) is a threshold gate and \(f:\Z\to\{0,1\}\)
is arbitrary. Its top weight is \(W(C)=\sum_i|w_i|\).
Write \(\cW\) for this class.
We say that \(C\) has period \(m\) if its lookup on the full frame
\([-W(C),W(C)]\cap\Z\) is the restriction of a Boolean function
on \(\Z/m\Z\). Both \(W(C)\) and the period refer to this one
representation.
\end{definition}

We call \(X(x)\) the \emph{score} and \(f\) the \emph{lookup}.
The resource \(W\) counts wires \emph{entering the output gate},
including multiplicity, not all wires in both layers. A negative
contribution \(-a g_i\) can be replaced by \(a(1-g_i)\), with a
constant shift absorbed into \(f\); complements of threshold gates
are threshold gates. Thus this is the ordinary symmetric-top model
with repeated connections counted, as in Chen--Tal--Wang
\cite[Section~5.3]{CTW26}. With one top wire per bottom gate, \(W\)
is the bottom-gate count; counting the single output gate adds one.
Bottom fan-in and real bottom weights remain unrestricted.

For \(n\ge4\), write
\[
 \lambda_n=\ceil{\lgx n},\qquad
 \mu_n=\ceil{\lgx\lambda_n}.
\]
For an \(n\)-input circuit, \(\operatorname{agr}(C,F)\) means
\(\Pr_{x\sim\{0,1\}^n}[C(x)=F(x)]\).
\emph{Almost everywhere} means at every sufficiently large \(n\),
with the language fixed before choosing \(n\) or the candidate circuit.

\begin{theorem}[Almost-everywhere near-cubic constant-error hardness]
\label{thm:main}\label{thm:gadget-fixed-error}
There is a rational constant \(0<\gamma<1\) such that, for every
fixed \(0<d<\gamma/5\), there are a positive integer \(K_d\)
and a language \(F_d\in\ENP\) with the following property.
For every sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(C\in\cW\),
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 W(C)\le\floor{\frac{n^3}{K_d\lambda_n^{21/4}\mu_n^7}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{agr}(C,F_d)<1-d .
 \quad}
\]
\end{theorem}

In words: below a near-cubic budget, a circuit with an arbitrary
symmetric lookup must be wrong on more than a \(d\) fraction of all
inputs, at every large length. The same \(F_d\) works, eventually,
against every fixed size \(A n^{3-\delta}\), with \(A,\delta>0\),
because logarithmic losses are smaller than every positive power of \(n\).

The constant \(\gamma\) is a rational lower bound on the rejection gap
of a published PCP of proximity, a PCP whose verifier also reads a few
bits of the input and rejects, with probability at least \(\gamma\), every
input far from the accepted set (\cref{sec:gadget-transfer}); it is
small and not optimized.

A Lean~4 proof of \cref{thm:main} is at
\url{https://github.com/devnag/near-cubic-gates-lean}. It assumes exactly
nine published theorems, the ``published inputs'' of \cref{sec:imports},
and proves everything else that \cref{thm:main} needs from them: the
counting algorithm and its analysis (\crefrange{sec:prelim}{sec:parameters})
and the constant-error transfer of \cref{sec:gadget-transfer}. The
periodic results of \cref{sec:per-overview,sec:per-transfer} are not
formalized. Its trusted statement is one file, and \cref{app:sources}
maps each input to its source.

\paragraph{The idea in one paragraph.}
A symmetric gate inspects only an integer sum. An algorithm that
recovers the distribution of that sum evaluates every symmetric
lookup at once, without approximating the lookup. The obstacle is
that the full score range is far longer than the window our modular
tabulation can reconstruct; we therefore confine the score to short
intervals whose validity we can certify. Williams' method then turns the
resulting fast circuit-analysis algorithm into a lower bound. Our algorithm
estimates the acceptance probability of one or
two circuits from the class. It fixes all but
\(k=\Theta(\log n\log\log n)\) input coordinates, so that in most of
the resulting cells only \(O(Wk/\sqrt n)\) top wires are live, and it
tabulates, across all cells at once, the exact residues of the integer
top score modulo every prime in \((P/2,P]\), where
\(P=n/\operatorname{polylog}n\). Residues determine the score
distribution on a window of about \(P^2\) consecutive values for one
score, and on a box of side about \(P^{3/2}\) for two scores, by a
discrete-tomography argument of Hajdu and Tijdeman. A random subsample
of a cell's live wires estimates the score up to a fluctuation far
smaller than its range, so the score lies in a short interval around a
rescaled subsample sum; nested subsamples at integer ratios make the
successive corrections, the \emph{digits}, small integers. The
algorithm does not trust a guessed subsample: it computes even moments
of the digits over each cell and uses a cell only when they certify
that the digits are short. A guessed sample thus places each score
inside such a window, and the count is correct for every guess and
accurate for an honest one. Chen, Tal, and Wang instead approximate the symmetric top
by a low-degree polynomial, whose degree budget is exhausted once the
live weight passes \(n^2\). Together, residue reconstruction and
conditioning on certified digits raise the permitted live-weight
exponent from \(2\) to \(5/2\), supplying the extra \(\sqrt n\).

The rest is transfer: a weighted analysis of the
Garey--Johnson--Stockmeyer gadget gives constant error for arbitrary
tops (\cref{sec:gadget-transfer}), and a Fourier reduction of
four-output products to scalar counts gives vanishing correlation for
periodic tops (\cref{sec:per-overview,sec:per-transfer}).
\Cref{sec:overview} expands this paragraph.

\subsection{The second result: periodic tops and vanishing correlation}
\label{sec:periodic-results}

A period-\(m\) lookup depends only on the count modulo \(m\).
It may accept any subset of residues, not just residue zero.
Parity has period two. An ordinary majority lookup on a long
interval generally has no short period. The periods below belong
to the \emph{original output lookup}; they are not the auxiliary
primes used later by the counting algorithm.

\begin{theorem}[Near-cubic vanishing correlation for periodic tops]
\label{thm:periodic}\label{thm:per-endpoint}
There are a positive integer \(C_{\rm per}\) and one language
\(F_{\rm per}\in\ENP\) such that, at every sufficiently large \(n\),
every \(C\in\cW\) with either
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item an arbitrary integer period \(m\le n^{1/6}\), or
\item a prime period \(p\le n^{2/11}\),
\end{enumerate}
satisfies
\[
 W(C)\le
 \floor{\frac{n^3}{C_{\rm per}\lambda_n^{10}\mu_n^{23/2}}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{agr}(C,F_{\rm per})<\frac12+\frac1{\lambda_n}.
\]
The same language works for both period ranges, all eligible periods,
and all eligible lookups.
\end{theorem}

The permitted periods grow with input length. The first range
includes composites, and the second improves the bound for primes.
Since output complementation preserves the class and period, the
theorem also bounds the absolute signed correlation by \(2/\lambda_n\):
\[
 \left|\E_x(-1)^{C(x)+F_{\rm per}(x)}\right|<\frac2{\lambda_n}.
\]
The hard language here need not be the language in \cref{thm:main}.

\subsection{Quantitative refinements and their scope}\label{sec:tradeoffs}

The constructions also give quantitative refinements, all almost
everywhere and all with \(\ENP\) languages. One language approaches
the error boundary \(\gamma/5\) at the cost of one more logarithm
(\cref{cor:main-boundary}). For periodic tops, size can be traded for
advantage: every fixed inverse-polylogarithmic advantage at weight
\(n^3/\operatorname{polylog}n\), subpolynomial advantages of regular
growth (\cref{thm:per-regular}) at weight \(n^{3-o(1)}\), and advantage \(n^{-\delta/4+o(1)}\) against
weight \(An^{3-\delta}\) for each fixed rational \(0<\delta<3\) and
every fixed \(A>0\) (\cref{thm:per-log-powers,thm:per-regular,cor:per-rational-power},
collected in \cref{sec:per-overview}).

\paragraph{What the results do not say.}
The output gate of a general \(\THR\circ\THR\) circuit can assign
arbitrarily large unequal weights without paying for them in its
gate count. Replacing those weights by repeated wires can increase
our resource drastically, so the near-cubic gate theorem does not
extend to that model. Nor is this a near-quartic total-wire theorem,
a theorem for a hard function in \(\mathrm P\), or a vanishing-correlation
theorem for arbitrary symmetric tops at a near-cubic gate count.
The last of these is a specific open problem, not a consequence of applying an XOR lemma
to \cref{thm:main}; \cref{sec:scope} explains the missing step.

\subsection{Historical context}\label{sec:background}

Lower bounds for depth-two threshold circuits have developed along
two different lines. For a majority output gate,
Hajnal, Maass, Pudl\'ak, Szegedy, and Tur\'an proved exponential lower
bounds for majorities of majority gates~\cite[Lemma~3.2]{HMPST93} and
extended them to arbitrary bottom
weights~\cite[Theorem~3.6]{HMPST93}; Nisan~\cite{Nis94} gave a
communication-complexity proof for majorities of arbitrary threshold
gates. The hard function is
inner product modulo two, which an arbitrary symmetric top computes
with \(n\) gates, as noted at the start of the introduction.

Kane and Williams~\cite{KW16} used random restrictions to prove
\(\widetilde\Omega(n^{3/2})\) gate and
\(\widetilde\Omega(n^{5/2})\) wire lower bounds for depth-two
threshold circuits, with a polynomial-time computable hard function.
Their bounds are already average-case~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{KW16}: for
every \(\varepsilon\gg\sqrt{\log n/n}\), no such circuit with fewer than
\(\Omega(\varepsilon^3n^{3/2}/\log^3n)\) gates, or fewer than
\(\Omega(\varepsilon^3n^{5/2}/\log^{7/2}n)\) wires, agrees with Andreev's
function on a \(1/2+\varepsilon\) fraction of inputs.
For languages in \(\ENP\), the algorithms of
Tamaki~\cite{Tam16} and Alman, Chan, and Williams~\cite{ACW16}
proved lower bounds against subquadratically many gates. Chen, Tal, and
Wang~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{CTW26} crossed the quadratic barrier:
for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), they obtain
\(n^{5/2-\varepsilon}\) gate bounds for both
\(\THR\circ\THR\) and \(\SYM\circ\THR\).

Our main theorem strengthens the \emph{symmetric-top branch} of
that result: the exponent becomes near-cubic, the lengths become
almost everywhere, and the guarantee becomes constant-error
hardness. The exponent gain comes from our counter; the transfers use
Chen--Lyu--Williams and Chen--Williams, with the weighted gadget
controlling the error constant. This paper does not supersede the
\(\THR\circ\THR\) branch of \cite{CTW26}.

The author's earlier preprints state almost-everywhere bounds at the
\(n^{5/2}/\polylog n\) gate scale~\cite{NagGate}, with
vanishing correlation for both classes
in~\cite[Corollary~1.6]{NagSeven}; their posted proofs apply the
printed form of \cite[Theorem~1.1]{BV14} (\cref{rem:bv14-printed} and
\cref{sec:related}). We make no claim about those statements here, and
none of their theorems is used. Here constant-error hardness for
arbitrary symmetric tops reaches a near-cubic gate count.

\subsection{What is borrowed and what is new}\label{sec:related}

Williams' algorithmic method~\cite{Wil13} connects faster circuit
analysis to lower bounds. Its PCP-based refinements by
Chen--Williams and Bathie--Williams~\cite{CW19,BW24}, including the
resulting reduction to acceptance probabilities of XORs of two circuits,
as recorded by Chen--Tal--Wang~\cite[Lemma~3.8]{CTW26}, are the
background for our counter. For almost-everywhere extraction
we use the architecture of Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite{CLW20}.
Their signed-sum reconstruction is based on Levin's proof of Yao's
XOR lemma~\cite{Lev87,GNW95}. The branches in which the source input or
the outer proof has no small general circuit use
Sudan--Trevisan--Vadhan~\cite{STV01}.
\Cref{app:sources} maps the principal published inputs to their source
locations and records the adaptations made here.


The counter of \cite[Sections~4.1--4.2]{NagGate} also restricts to
cells and samples the live sum at nested rates, but it certifies each
sampled value with a low-degree indicator of one short window and
evaluates the top exactly on the retained sample. New here are the
three steps that give the exponent: reconstruction of the score
distribution from its residues modulo many primes, which replaces the
window indicators; sampling at integer ratios whose digits are certified
by even moments for every witness; and conditioning the count on the
certified digits. The prime sketches of \cite[Section~5.2]{NagGate} and
\cite[Lemma~A.7]{NagCubic} approximate single exact-equation indicators;
here joint residue counts reconstruct whole score distributions.

The main intermediate results are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A uniform certified counter for pairs at near-cubic weight
and scalars at near-\(n^{7/2}\) weight, with an explicit accuracy
cost (\cref{thm:pair-uniform}).
\item Integer-ratio nested sampling, short-digit conditioning,
and a quantitative two-score reconstruction window, combined
with moment tests sound for every witness
(\cref{lem:chain,lem:two-window,lem:window-size,prop:sound}).
The factorization method is Hajdu--Tijdeman's; the quantitative
window uses all short primitive directions.
\item A weighted error analysis of the classical MAX-2SAT gadget,
giving the constant-error transfer (\cref{lem:gadget-stability}).
\item A rational four-output-to-scalar reduction for periodic
lookups, a short-direction improvement for prime periods (via
Dirichlet's pigeonhole argument), and
a rounding bound for arbitrary real guessed values
(\cref{lem:per-fourier,lem:per-short-direction,lem:per-rounding}).
\end{enumerate}

No theorem from the author's earlier manuscripts~\cite{NagGate,NagCubic,NagSeven}
is used here. Their posted proofs apply \cite[Lemma~3.10]{CLW20} as
printed at a polynomial time bound, that is, the printed form of
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BV14} (\cref{rem:bv14-printed}).\footnote{\cite{NagCubic}
states near-cubic wire bounds with inverse-polynomial correlation and
\cite{NagSeven} wire--correlation tradeoffs at exponent \(7/2\); since
\(G\) bottom gates can have order \(nG\) input wires, wire bounds do not
imply the present gate exponent. The public Lean~4 proofs of
\cite[Theorem~2.5]{NagCubic} and \cite[Theorem~1.1, row~B of
Table~1]{NagSeven} (\url{https://github.com/devnag/near-cubic-wires},
\url{https://github.com/devnag/wire_three_five}) assume the printed lemma
among their nine published inputs.} The Lean~4 proof of this paper's
main theorem (\url{https://github.com/devnag/near-cubic-gates-lean})
assumes the weaker statement of Murray and Williams instead
(\cref{imp:short-pcp}).

\section{Overview of the proof}\label{sec:overview}\label{sec:idea}

The proof follows Williams' algorithmic method: sufficiently
fast circuit analysis, combined with hypothetical small circuits for
all the required proof bits, would verify a hard computation faster
than a time-hierarchy theorem permits. The hard function is recovered
from this contradiction. We call the fast verifier the \emph{weak
machine}. It builds a PCP verifier for the hard
computation with only logarithmically many random bits, the
\emph{outer verifier}. It guesses small circuits for all the proof bits
that this verifier and a PCP of proximity need, and it checks them with
a fast counting algorithm (\cref{fig:outer-step}).

Concretely, the weak machine receives a source input of length \(N\)
and guesses small circuits for three objects: the source input itself,
the proof of the outer verifier, and the proof table of the PCP of
proximity; it then uses the counter to estimate the acceptance
probability of that PCP. If the hard function had a small
\(\SYM\circ\THR\) circuit with error below \(\gamma/5\), that circuit
would be one of the guessed proof tables, and the weak machine would
accept a yes-instance. The \emph{refuter} of Chen--Lyu--Williams, an
algorithm that, given a machine too fast for the time hierarchy, outputs
an input on which it errs, supplies at every large \(N\) a yes-instance
that the weak machine rejects; on that instance one of three named
objects is therefore hard (``Almost everywhere'' below).

There are two distinct jobs: estimate the probabilities required by
the weak machine, and keep it sound even when its guessed proof data are
wrong. The first job explains the gate exponent. The second explains
why arbitrary symmetric tops yield constant error, whereas the stated
periodic tops permit vanishing correlation. The named paragraphs below,
from ``The counting interface'' to ``Comparison with Chen--Tal--Wang'',
treat the first job; the three after them treat the second.

\paragraph{The counting interface.}
Everything rests on one certified counting theorem. A call asks for
the acceptance probability of one class circuit \(C^1\), of
\(C^1\oplus C^2\) for two class circuits, or of \(C^1\oplus\PAR_\Sigma\),
where \(\PAR_\Sigma(z)=\bigoplus_{i\in\Sigma}z_i\) is an explicit input parity.
These are exactly the means into which the gadget penalty of
``Arbitrary symmetric tops'' below decomposes.
Besides the circuits, the algorithm reads a short auxiliary string,
the \emph{witness}, which the weak machine guesses.
\Cref{sec:algorithm} defines the normalized inputs, which have
polynomially bounded integer descriptions; by \cref{lem:integer},
every circuit used later has one.

Informally: for every witness the counter returns a sound probability
interval; for some polynomial-length witness the radius is \(\beta\)
whenever the top weight is below about \(\beta n^3/\polylog n\) for a
pair and \(\beta n^{7/2}/\polylog n\) for one score; and it runs in
\(2^n/n^{\mu_n}\) time. The formal statement follows;
\cref{sec:parameters} proves it.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform certified pair and scalar counting]
\label{thm:pair-uniform}\label{thm:counting}
Fix positive integers \(B_1,B_2\). There is a constant
\(a_{B_1,B_2}>0\) and one algorithm with the following properties. Its
normalized input has total top weight \(W\le2n^4\), and a rational
requested radius \(n^{-B_1}\le\beta\le1/16\) encoded in at most
\(n^{B_2}\) bits.
Every witness gives a sound probability interval. At every
sufficiently large \(n\), some polynomial-length witness gives radius
at most \(\beta\) if
\[
 W\le a_{B_1,B_2}\beta\mathcal A_3(n),\qquad
 \mathcal A_3(n)=\frac{n^3}{\lambda_n^{21/4}\mu_n^7},
\]
for a pair-XOR call. For a single-circuit or circuit-XOR-raw-parity
call the larger sufficient cap is
\[
 W\le a_{B_1,B_2}\beta\mathcal A_1(n),\qquad
 \mathcal A_1(n)=\frac{n^{7/2}}{\lambda_n^6\mu_n^8}.
\]
For all sufficiently large \(n\), it runs within \(2^n/n^{\mu_n}\) steps,
uniformly over the admitted inputs.
For each prescribed fixed polynomial number of calls, their complete
workload is \(2^n n^{O(1)-\mu_n}\). The algorithm uses no oracle and
reads no guessed data beyond its witness, as the refuter of
\cref{imp:ae-refuter} requires.
\end{theorem}

Soundness for \emph{every} witness lets a verifier guess witnesses
freely, and the superpolynomial saving \(n^{\mu_n}\) in running time pays
for the hierarchy argument.

The transfers in \cref{sec:gadget-transfer,sec:per-overview,sec:per-transfer}
use only this interface: sound intervals for every witness, accurate
intervals for some witness, and the two caps. The window and digit
machinery applies whenever a weighted integer score admits the required
exact residue tabulation; the live-weight estimate and tabulation budget
are specific to threshold gates. The two-score window
(\cref{lem:two-window}) itself concerns rational-valued functions on a
box, independently of circuits. \Cref{fig:counter} shows the algorithm.

\paragraph{The obstacle.}
Chen, Tal, and Wang restrict most input coordinates and batch-evaluate
over the remaining subcubes, the \emph{cells}; on each cell they
approximate the symmetric top by an \(\mathbb F_2\)-probabilistic
polynomial of degree \(\widetilde O(\sqrt\ell)\) in its \(\ell\)
\emph{live} gates, those not constant on the cell~\cite[Section~2]{CTW26}.
Batch evaluation affords degree below \(n\), so \(\ell\) must stay
below \(n^2\); with \(n^{\varepsilon/10}\) free coordinates,
\(\ell\approx Wn^{\varepsilon/10}/\sqrt n\), which caps \(W\) near
\(n^{5/2}\). The paragraphs below never approximate the top.

\paragraph{Counting integer scores in cells.}
Leave \(k=\Theta(\log n\log\log n)\) input coordinates free.
Fixing the others partitions the cube into cells of size \(2^k\).
A bottom gate is \emph{live} in a cell if it is nonconstant there;
only live contributions change the score inside that cell.
The restriction estimate used by Kane--Williams and
Chen--Tal--Wang~\cite{KW16,CTW26} makes the live top weight
\(O(Wk/\sqrt n)\) in most cells: a bottom gate stays nonconstant only
when the fixed part of its sum lands within the range that the \(k\)
free coordinates can move it, which anti-concentration makes rare. Williams' rectangular matrix
multiplication~\cite[Corollary~4.4]{WilACC14}, combined with
Matou\v{s}ek's bucketing~\cite{Mat91}, can compute low-degree polynomial
sums across all cells, in \(2^{n-k}n^{O(1)}\) time per polynomial whose
degree stays within the budget of \cref{lem:print}. Fermat tests and Beigel--Tarui
lifting~\cite{BT94} turn these into exact residue counts of the
integer top scores, for all primes \(p\) in \((P/2,P]\): by Fermat's
little theorem, \(1-X^{p-1}\) indicates \(X\equiv0\pmod p\) up to
multiples of \(p\), and the Beigel--Tarui polynomial of \cref{lem:amp}
sharpens this to an exact indicator modulo a power \(p^e>2^k\), so that
the indicators summed over a cell give the exact count. The degree
that batch evaluation can afford limits \(P\) to
\(n/\operatorname{polylog}n\) (\cref{sec:printing,sec:parameters}).

\paragraph{From residues to distributions.}
Residue counts determine a score distribution only on a short window.
For one score, a rational-valued function supported on
\(D'=1+\sum_p(p-1)=\Theta(P^2/\log P)\) consecutive integers is
determined by its push-forwards modulo these primes, because its
generating polynomial must vanish at all their roots of unity
(\cref{lem:one-window}). Two scores need a smaller box. If a function
on an \(L\times L\) box has zero modular data, then each of its
projections along a primitive direction \(v\in\Z^2\) with
\(\|v\|_1\lesssim D'/L\) is a one-score function with zero data, hence
zero. By the factorization method of
Hajdu--Tijdeman~\cite{HT01}, each vanishing projection forces a
distinct irreducible binomial factor of the generating polynomial.
Counting all short primitive directions gives these factors total
degree \(\sum_{j\le D'/L}j\varphi(j)\approx2(D'/L)^3/\pi^2\) in one
variable. The generating polynomial has degree below \(L\) in that
variable, so the function vanishes once this total reaches \(L\), that
is, for \(L\) up to about \((2/\pi^2)^{1/4}D'^{3/4}\). The resulting
two-score window is
\[
 L_2=\Theta(D'^{3/4})=\Theta\!\left(\frac{P^{3/2}}{(\log P)^{3/4}}\right)
\]
(\cref{lem:two-window,lem:window-size,lem:primes}). A rank count shows
that recovering arbitrary box-supported functions from these data
forces side \(O(P^{3/2}/\sqrt{\log P})\) (\cref{rem:rank}).

\paragraph{Short digits, certified for every witness.}
The score in a cell can vary by its live weight, far more than these
windows. Sample signed unit contributions to a score \(X\) at nested
rates \(\pi_1<\cdots<\pi_J\) with integer ratios
\(a_j=\pi_{j+1}/\pi_j\). Each sampled sum \(K_j\) is an integer
combination of the bottom gates, and the witness proposes its
coefficient vector. Form the short integer digits
\[
 E_0=K_1,\qquad E_j=K_{j+1}-a_jK_j.
\]
The telescoping identity \(K_J=\sum_jA_jE_j\), with
\(A_j=\prod_{i>j}a_i\), holds for \emph{every} proposed vector, not
only for sampled ones, so the algorithm need not find a favorable
sample itself.

The algorithm tests
even moments of the digits and of \(\Phi=M_1K_J-AX\) on every cell,
where \(M_1=1/\pi_1\) is an integer and \(A=\prod_ia_i\), so that
\(\pi_J=A/M_1\). In a passing cell each
digit varies by less than \(P/4\), so its residue modulo any prime in
\((P/2,P]\) determines it. On each digit class (the inputs of the cell with a
fixed digit vector) the \(\Phi\)-test confines \(AX\) to a short
interval around \(M_1K_J\). So the score lies in an explicit interval
of at most \(L_w\) values, where \(L_w=L_2\) for
pairs and \(L_w=D'\) for one score. The tabulated residue counts on
that class therefore determine the exact score distribution there,
whether or not the witness was honest (\cref{prop:sound}). The count
of a failing cell is replaced by \(2^{k-1}\), with that much
uncertainty. For an honest proposal, Bernstein's inequality keeps the
digits short on most cells (\cref{lem:chain,lem:sampled,prop:complete}).
Consequently every guess is sound, and an honest favorable guess is
useful.

\paragraph{Where the cubic exponent comes from.}
Write \(\ell=O(Wk/\sqrt n)\) for a typical cell's live weight,
suppressing a fixed accuracy factor. With concentration parameter
\(T=\Theta(k)\), conditioning on the short digits leaves a score
interval of width \(O(T\ell/P)\). Heuristically, the finest sampling
rate is pushed up until a digit's fluctuation, about \(\sqrt{T\pi\ell}\)
at rate \(\pi\), reaches \(P\), so \(\pi_J\approx P^2/(T\ell)\), and
given the digits the score fluctuates by about
\(\sqrt{T\ell/\pi_J}\approx T\ell/P\); \cref{lem:chain} makes this exact.
Pair counting succeeds when
\[
 \underbrace{\frac{TWk}{P\sqrt n}}_{\text{width after conditioning}}
 \ \lesssim\
 \underbrace{\frac{P^{3/2}}{(\log P)^{3/4}}}_{\text{two-score window}}.
\]
Thus \(W\) can reach roughly \(P^{5/2}\sqrt n\), apart from
logarithms. Since \(P\) is \(n\) divided by logarithmic factors, the
resulting exponent is \(5/2+1/2=3\).
The logarithms are explicit. With \(P=\Theta(n/(\lambda_n\mu_n^2))\),
\(k,T=\Theta(\lambda_n\mu_n)\) and \(L_2=\Theta(P^{3/2}/\lambda_n^{3/4})\)
(\cref{lem:asymptotics}), the pair cap \(\beta\sqrt n\,PL_2/(Tk)\) is
\(\beta n^3/(\lambda_n^{13/4+2}\mu_n^{5+2})=\beta\mathcal A_3(n)\), and
the scalar cap \(\beta\sqrt n\,PD'/(Tk)\), with
\(D'=\Theta(P^2/\lambda_n)\), is
\(\beta n^{7/2}/(\lambda_n^{4+2}\mu_n^{6+2})=\beta\mathcal A_1(n)\).
For one score, the larger \(P^2/\log P\) window instead gives the
counting exponent \(7/2\). That is an algorithmic interface, not
by itself a \(7/2\) gate lower bound: a weak machine must
also have every joint probability it needs.
The exact parameters and their logarithmic costs are established
in \cref{sec:parameters}.

\paragraph{Comparison with Chen--Tal--Wang.}
We never approximate the top (``The obstacle'' above). The same degree budget
buys exact residues modulo primes up to \(P=n/\operatorname{polylog}n\),
and with the digits and the two-score window the count tolerates live
weight up to about \(PL_2/T=n^{5/2}/\operatorname{polylog}n\). Hence
\(W\) reaches \(n^3/\operatorname{polylog}n\).
The \(5/2\) gate-axis barrier discussed in
\cite[Section~9.3]{NagCubic} and~\cite[Section~8]{NagTouching} comes
from directly approximating the symmetric top by low-degree polynomials
in black-box surviving gate outputs. It does not limit restriction and
batch evaluation themselves: here the batch evaluator computes residue
counts and moment tests, and reconstruction on certified digit classes
precedes evaluation of the lookup. Chen--Tal--Wang's counter is also
deterministic; ours uses a guessed witness, with soundness for every
witness and accuracy for some witness (\cref{thm:pair-uniform}), as
Williams' method permits.

\paragraph{Arbitrary symmetric tops: weight the classical gadget.}
The perfectly complete three-query PCP of proximity (PCPP) of
Chen--Williams~\cite[Lemma~24]{CW19} reduces the outer
verifier to ORs of three Boolean literals. It is used with their linear
code~\cite[Lemma~27]{CW19} and the proximity machinery of
Ben-Sasson, Goldreich, Harsha, Sudan, and Vadhan~\cite{BGHSV06}. The classical MAX-2SAT
gadget of Garey--Johnson--Stockmeyer~\cite{GJS76} has maximum
satisfaction seven for a true triple and six for a false triple.
With literal values \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) and helper \(y\), the penalty
\(Q=7-(\text{number of satisfied gadget clauses})\) is quadratic:
\[
 Q(x,y)=1-\sum_ix_i+\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j+2y-y\sum_ix_i.
\]
It is nonnegative, and at least one on a false triple. For an honest
true triple, choose \(y^*=x_1x_2x_3\). The key bound is
\[
 Q(x',y')\le\sum_{i=1}^3|x_i-x_i'|+2|y^*-y'|.
\]
The total weight is five. A nearly uniform table of these five weighted
positions therefore turns error \(d\) into mean penalty at most
\((5+\epsilon)d\), where \(\epsilon>0\) can be made as small as desired
by lengthening the table.

Writing each two-literal clause in the XOR
basis through \(ab=(a+b-(a\oplus b))/2\) expresses \(\mathbb EQ\) by
single, pair-XOR and circuit-XOR-parity means, which are exactly the
counter's three call types. Replacing each signed probability by its
certified endpoint gives an estimate \(U\) with \(U\ge\mathbb EQ\) for
every witness and \(U\le\mathbb EQ+10\omega\) for a useful witness of
radius \(\omega\). The weak machine accepts exactly when
\(U<\gamma-\epsilon\). PCPP soundness forces \(\mathbb EQ\ge\gamma-o(1)\)
on every outer no-instance, so no witness makes it accept there. On the
other hand, if the honest proof table, the hard function of
\cref{thm:main}, had a class circuit with error \(d<\gamma/5\), that
circuit, guessed as the proof table, would bring the estimate below the
acceptance threshold, and some witness would make the weak machine accept
a yes-instance. That contradicts
the choice of the instance: the refuter (see ``Almost everywhere''
below) supplies a yes-instance that the weak machine rejects. This is why the error boundary is
\(\gamma/5\). The gain comes from
analyzing the existing gadget as a whole, with five weighted proof
positions rather than the twenty query positions of its ten
clauses, each charged at two positions; the PCP itself is unchanged.

\paragraph{Periodic tops: four outputs through scalar counting.}
Vanishing correlation requires XOR amplification. The signed-sum XOR reconstruction of Chen--Lyu--Williams
\cite[Lemma~3.8 and Section~6.2]{CLW20} produces real coordinates
\(S=\sum_j\alpha_j C_j\): if a class circuit predicts the XOR of several
copies of a function with small advantage, a signed sum of its
restrictions approximates one copy, and the weak machine guesses the
coefficients \(\alpha_j\). Arbitrary guessed coordinates need not be
Boolean or lie in \([0,1]\). The nonnegative validity test
\(S^2(1-S)^2\) of Chen--Lyu--Williams requires products of four class
outputs. Pair counting
alone does not provide them.

For period \(m\), a four-output predicate is an explicit table on
\((\Z/m\Z)^4\). Classical finite Fourier inversion and
Parseval~\cite[Chapter~10]{Terras99} give coefficient mass at most
\(m^2\). Each character of \((\Z/m\Z)^4\) depends only on one integer
combination of the four scores, so a rational implementation, followed
by decomposition into Boolean level sets, makes each term a genuine
scalar lookup on that one combined score. Its weight is at most
\(m\) times the total original weight. The combined cost is \(m^3\):
a factor \(m\) in helper weight, and a factor \(m^2\) in requested
accuracy, because the radius is divided by the coefficient mass.
For prime periods, short representatives of projective directions
reduce the weight factor to \(O(p^{3/4})\), so the cost becomes
\(p^{11/4}\). The scalar exponent \(7/2\) has a half-power to spare at a
cubic target. The identities
\[
 (n^{1/6})^3=n^{1/2},\qquad
 (n^{2/11})^{11/4}=n^{1/2}
\]
explain the two period ranges. The logarithmic factors in
\cref{thm:periodic} are those of \(\mathcal A_1\), a factor
\(\lambda_n^4\) because the quartic test needs radius about the inverse
fourth power of a coefficient mass \(O(\lambda_n)\), and
\(\mu_n^{7/2}\) from the XOR length \(t=\Theta(\mu_n)\).
Precision, coefficient sizes, and
the final change of input length are paid for in
\cref{sec:per-transfer}. Every rational approximation is verified on
the complete residue table, and its error is added to the interval.

\paragraph{Almost everywhere: one fixed weak machine.}
The hard computation reads a source input of length \(N\), while the
counter needs proof-bit functions on \(q=\Theta(\log N)\) inputs. The
weak machine guesses a small general circuit for the source input and
checks it deterministically at all \(N\) positions, in \(N^{1+o(1)}\)
time. It then applies the short PCP of Ben-Sasson and Viola, in the form
stated by Murray and Williams~\cite{BV14,MW20}, to the description of
that circuit, which has length polynomial in \(q\). The resulting
verifier uses \(q\) random bits and has size polynomial in \(q\); the
source input enters only through the check. \Cref{fig:outer-step} shows
this outer step. The weak machine also guesses small circuits for the
outer proof oracle and the remaining proof-bit functions, and the
counter's witnesses. In total these guesses take a fixed polynomial in
\(q\) bits, which is eventually less than \(N/16\), within the guess
budget that the refuter of \cref{imp:ae-refuter} allows.
The complete algorithm runs in \(o(\Tclk(N))\), where \(\Tclk(N)=N^{O(1)}\)
is the hard machine's time bound, using the counter's
superpolynomial saving.

Fortnow--Santhanam's hierarchy and the
constructive refuter of Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite{FS16,CLW20} then
produce a rejected yes-instance at every large source length. The
refuter returns a word on which the weak machine and the hierarchy
language disagree (\cref{imp:ae-refuter}). Since the weak machine
rejects every no-instance, that word is a yes-instance that it rejects.
On that instance there are three cases, named here and reused in
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer,sec:per-transfer}. (\textsc{NoSource}) No small
circuit reproduces the source input: the source input itself is hard for
general circuits. (\textsc{NoProof}) Some small circuit reproduces it, but
the first honest outer proof for it has no small general circuit: that
proof is the hard function. (\textsc{Tautology}) Both circuits exist: they
make the outer verifier a tautology, and its honest PCPP proof table is
the hard function. In the first two cases the hard function is amplified
by Sudan--Trevisan--Vadhan~\cite{STV01}.
Canonical recovery and careful padding give a fixed \(\ENP\)
language at every sufficiently large final length. The language is in
\(\ENP\) because, at each length, an exponential-time machine with a SAT
oracle can run the refuter, decide which case holds, and find the first
source circuit, the first accepted oracle and the first proof circuit by
prefix search; the hard function's value at an input is read off the
object that the case names (\cref{sec:all-lengths}).

\paragraph{Changes from version 1.}

statement changes. The tabulation imports Williams' multitape

signed printer (\cref{lem:signed-printer}). The refuter is
\cite[Theorem~1.13]{CLW20} read for multitape machines. The short PCP

normalization bound is \cite[Lemma~3.2]{CTW26}. The counter's running
time is proved on every input directly, without a clock.

\paragraph{Machine model.}
Algorithms are deterministic or nondeterministic multitape Turing
machines over a binary alphabet, and time counts machine steps. This is
the model of the imported algorithms (\cref{imp:batch,imp:ae-refuter}).
Arithmetic on polynomial-bit integers uses elementary multiword
algorithms. When an algorithm consults a large table inside a loop, it
first sorts the relevant records by key and then merges, which costs a
logarithmic factor over reading the records once. All such overheads
are fixed polynomial factors.

All logarithms in algorithm parameters are base two; \(\ln\) denotes
the natural logarithm. Empty products are one. For a set
\(I\subseteq[n]\) of size \(k\), write an input as \((y,x)\), where
\(x\in\{0,1\}^I\) and \(y\) assigns the remaining coordinates.
Let
\[
 C_y=\{(y,x):x\in\{0,1\}^I\},\quad z_y=(y,0^I),\quad
 \Delta_y H(y,x)=H(y,x)-H(z_y).
\]
We omit the subscript \(y\) when the cell is fixed. For two circuits,
superscripts \(r=1,2\) distinguish their top sums and gate lists, and
\(W=W^1+W^2\). A sum \(X^r\) can always be expanded as \(W^r\)
signed unit copies of bottom gates. Its live weight \(\ell^r(y)\)
counts the copies whose gates are live. This expansion is used only
in an existence argument; a witness stores one integer coefficient per
gate, rather than individual random choices.

\subsection{Why real bottom weights cause no encoding problem}

The following is a classical integer-normalization fact from threshold
logic~\cite{MTT61}. Only the existence of such a representation
is needed, because circuits are guessed rather than computed.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:integer}
Every threshold function on \(m\ge1\) Boolean inputs has an equivalent
representation whose weights and threshold are integers in the range
\(\pm m^m\). In particular their bit lengths are \(O(m\log(m+1))\).
\end{lemma}
This is \cite[Lemma~3.2]{CTW26}, attributed there to Muroga, Toda, and
Takasu~\cite{MTT61}. H{\aa}stad~\cite[Section~3]{Has94} gives the sharper
bound \((m+1)^{(m+1)/2}\), by Cramer's rule and Hadamard's inequality; the
weaker bound suffices here.

\subsection{Restrictions and concentration}

The next counting argument is the live-gate estimate in the
restriction method~\cite{KW16}, in the form
of~\cite[Lemma~4.1]{CTW26}. Its chain-counting proof is that
of~\cite[Appendix~A.3]{CTW26}, an instance of Lubell's argument for
Sperner's theorem~\cite{Lub66}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:live}
For a threshold gate \(g\), a uniform \(k\)-subset \(I\subseteq[n]\),
and uniform outer assignment \(y\),
\[
 \Pr[g\text{ is live on }C_y]
 \le \frac{k\binom{n-k}{\floor{(n-k)/2}}}{2^{n-k}}
 \le \frac{k}{\sqrt{n-k+1}}.
\]
Consequently, for any collection of circuits of total top weight
\(W\), and any \(\beta>0\), some fixed \(I\) has
\[
 \sum_r\ell^r(y)\le \frac{Wk}{\beta\sqrt{n-k+1}}
\]
on all but a \(\beta\) fraction of cells.
\end{lemma}
The proof specializes the chain-counting argument of
\cite[Appendix~A.3]{CTW26}; it is in \cref{app:live}.

We also use the bounded-variable Bernstein inequality,
Vershynin~\cite[Theorem~2.8.4, first edition]{Vershynin18} with \(K=1\).
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:bernstein}
If independent centered random variables \(Y_t\) satisfy
\(\abs{Y_t}\le1\) and \(\sum_t\E Y_t^2\le V\), then for every \(s>0\),
\[
 \Pr\!\left[\abs{\sum_tY_t}\ge s\right]
 \le2\exp\!\left(-\frac{s^2}{2(V+s/3)}\right).
\]
In particular, for \(T>0\), \(s=\sqrt{2TV}+2T/3\) gives a bound of \(2e^{-T}\).
\end{lemma}
For the particular choice of \(s\), direct expansion gives
\(s^2\ge2T(V+s/3)\).

\section{Recovering distributions from modular counts}\label{sec:windows}

The counter of \cref{sec:printing} will supply exact residue counts of
integer scores. Residues must determine a \emph{distribution}, not just
a single integer. For example, knowing how often a score is even does not
identify which even scores occurred. Our reconstruction lemmas apply
to arbitrary rational-valued functions; positivity is not required.
This permits a clean uniqueness proof by subtracting two candidate
distributions.

Let \(\cP\) be a nonempty finite set of primes and put
\[
 D'=1+\sum_{p\in\cP}(p-1).
\]
For a finitely supported function \(F:\Z^d\to\Q\), define its
modular push-forward by
\[
 F_p(\rho)=\sum_{u\equiv\rho\pmod p}F(u),
 \qquad\rho\in(\Z/p\Z)^d.
\]
Thus \(F_p\) records the total mass of each residue class.

\subsection{One score: a roots-of-unity argument}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:one-window}
If \(F:\Z\to\Q\) is supported on at most \(D'\) consecutive
integers and \(F_p=0\) for every \(p\in\cP\), then \(F=0\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Shift the support to \(\{0,\ldots,D'-1\}\) and form
\(f(t)=\sum_uF(u)t^u\). For every \(p\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\),
\[
 f(\zeta)=\sum_{\rho=0}^{p-1}F_p(\rho)\zeta^\rho=0.
\]
Distinct primes' root sets intersect only at one. Their union therefore
contains \(D'\) distinct roots. A polynomial of degree below \(D'\)
with this many roots is zero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two scores: short projections and binomial factors}

Let \(\varphi\) be Euler's totient function and set
\(S(q)=\sum_{m=1}^q m\varphi(m)\), with \(S(0)=0\).
Hajdu and Tijdeman's discrete-tomography method~\cite[Theorem~1 and
its preliminary divisibility lemma]{HT01} turns zero line sums into
binomial divisibility. We prove this over \(\Q\) and count the usable
directions.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:two-window}
Let \(L\ge2\) and
\(q=\floor{(D'-1)/(L-1)}\). If \(S(q)\ge L\), then a
rational-valued function supported on a given box with both sides at
most \(L\) is uniquely determined, among functions supported on that
box, by its push-forwards \(F_p\), \(p\in\cP\).
\end{lemma}

\emph{Idea of proof.} Take the difference of two functions with the
same data. Each of its projections along a short primitive direction is
a one-score function with zero data, hence zero by the one-score lemma.
Each vanishing projection makes an irreducible binomial divide the
generating polynomial. These binomials have more total degree in one
variable than the polynomial has, so the difference is zero.

\begin{proof}
Subtract two functions with the same data and translate their common
box to \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^2\). It suffices to show that a function
with zero data is zero. Write its generating polynomial as
\[
 f(u,v)=\sum_{a,b=0}^{L-1}F(a,b)u^av^b.
\]

\emph{First, every short primitive projection is zero.}
A vector \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)\) is primitive if
\(\gcd(\abs\alpha,\abs\beta)=1\). If
\(\abs\alpha+\abs\beta\le q\), then
\[
 G(c)=\sum_{\alpha a+\beta b=c}F(a,b)
\]
is supported on an interval containing at most
\((L-1)q+1\le D'\) integers. Its residue data are zero, since
projection commutes with reduction modulo \(p\). By
\cref{lem:one-window}, \(G=0\). Equivalently,
\(f(t^\alpha,t^\beta)=0\) in \(\Q[t,t^{-1}]\).

\emph{Second, each vanishing projection forces a distinct prime factor.}
Choose one vector from each pair \(\pm(\alpha,\beta)\), normalized so
that \(\alpha\ge0\), and \(\beta=1\) if \(\alpha=0\).
The kernel of the substitution \(u\mapsto t^\alpha,v\mapsto t^\beta\)
on \(\Q[u,v]\) is generated by the binomial
\[
 B_{\alpha,\beta}=
 \begin{cases}
 u^\beta-v^\alpha,&\beta\ge0,\\
 u^{\abs\beta}v^\alpha-1,&\beta<0.
 \end{cases}
\]
Here is a direct justification. In the Laurent polynomial ring,
choose integers \(i,j\) with \(\alpha i+\beta j=1\).
The monomials \(\omega_1=u^iv^j\) and \(\omega_2=u^\beta v^{-\alpha}\)
form a coordinate system, because their exponent matrix has
determinant \(-1\). Substitution sends \(\omega_1\) to \(t\) and
\(\omega_2\) to one, so its kernel there is \((\omega_2-1)\). Up to a Laurent monomial unit, this is
the displayed binomial. That binomial has no factor \(u\) or \(v\).
It is irreducible in \(\Q[u,v]\): a factorization gives a Laurent
unit factor, since \(\omega_2-1\) is irreducible in the Laurent ring,
hence a monomial, which must be constant.
Clearing Laurent denominators and using unique factorization gives
the asserted polynomial kernel.

Nonparallel primitive directions give nonassociate binomials:
substitution in the other direction gives two powers of \(t\)
whose exponents differ by \(\pm(\alpha\beta'-\beta\alpha')\ne0\).
By unique factorization, the product of these binomials divides \(f\).

\emph{Finally, there are too many factors for a nonzero \(f\).}
The sum of their degrees in \(u\) is
\(\sum\abs\beta\), over primitive directions modulo sign of norm
at most \(q\). At norm one, the horizontal and vertical directions
contribute one in total. At norm \(m\ge2\), the directions contribute
\[
 2\sum_{\substack{1\le a<m\\\gcd(a,m)=1}}(m-a)
 =m\varphi(m),
\]
using the symmetry \(a\leftrightarrow m-a\). Thus the product has
\(u\)-degree \(S(q)\ge L\), whereas \(\deg_uf\le L-1\).
This is impossible unless \(f=0\).
\end{proof}

For a concrete picture, the projection \((\alpha,\beta)=(1,1)\)
adds masses along diagonals \(a+b=c\). If all those sums vanish,
\(u-v\) divides \(f\). The projection \((1,-1)\) instead forces
\(uv-1\). The lemma uses many such independent factors, not just
the coordinate projections.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:window-size}
For every integer \(q\ge1\),
\[
 S(q)\ge\frac{2q^3}{\pi^2}-q^2(4/3+\ln q).
\]
Call \(L\ge2\) admissible if \(S(\floor{(D'-1)/(L-1)})\ge L\), as in
\cref{lem:two-window}. Let \(L_2\) be the largest admissible \(L\),
or one if none exists. Then
\(L_2=\Theta(D'^{3/4})\) as \(D'\to\infty\).
In particular \(L_2\ge\floor{(3/5)D'^{3/4}}\) for all sufficiently
large \(D'\).
\end{lemma}
The proof is a standard totient-summation calculation, given in
\cref{app:window-size}.

Uniqueness also gives an algorithm: introduce one rational unknown
per box point and one equation per residue class. The matrix entries
are zero or one. Exact Gaussian elimination finds the unique
distribution whenever the data come from a function on the box.
An algorithm may also detect inconsistency and report uncertainty.
A box with one point is determined by its total mass, so the
singleton convention in the definition of \(L_2\) is also sound.

\subsection{How many useful primes are available?}

For an integer \(P\ge3\), let \(\cP\) consist of all primes in
\((P/2,P]\). Only the order of magnitude of this set is needed.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:primes}
For this prime set, as \(P\to\infty\),
\[
 D'=\Theta(P^2/\ln P),\qquad
 L_2=\Theta\!\left(P^{3/2}/(\ln P)^{3/4}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the prime number theorem~\cite[Chapters~4 and~13]{Apostol76}, the
interval \((P/2,P]\) contains \((1+o(1))P/(2\ln P)\) primes;
Erd\H{o}s' elementary argument for Bertrand's postulate~\cite{Erdos32}
already gives this order of magnitude. Each such prime contributes
more than \(P/2-1\) and at most \(P\) to \(D'\), so
\(D'=\Theta(P^2/\ln P)\). Apply \cref{lem:window-size}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The limitation of full-box residue reconstruction]\label{rem:rank}
For distributions on \(\{0,\ldots,L-1\}^d\), the residue map has
rank at most \(1+\sum_{p\in\cP}(p^d-1)\): all primes report the
same total mass. Injectivity therefore requires
\(L^d\le1+\sum_p(p^d-1)\).
For two scores this yields
\(L=O(P^{3/2}/\sqrt{\ln P})\). Our box bound has the same power
of \(P\), with an additional factor \((\ln P)^{1/4}\) loss.
This is a restriction on recovering \emph{arbitrary} box-supported
distributions from these data, not an impossibility theorem for all
circuit-analysis methods or for more structured distributions.
\end{remark}

\section{Nested subsampling produces short integer digits}\label{sec:chains}

We now turn a potentially large live sum into short forms that can
be identified from one residue. Sampling at a single very small rate
would make the first sampled sum short, but leave a poor estimate of
the original sum. The chain increases the rate while keeping each
new correction short. Integer rate ratios preserve exact arithmetic.

\begin{lemma}[Choice of rates]\label{lem:chain}
Let \(T>0\), let \(h\) be an integer with \(h\ge64T\), and let
\(\ell_0\ge0\). Define
\[
 \bar\ell=\max\{\ell_0,h^2/(256T)\},\quad
 \pi^*=\frac{h^2}{8T\bar\ell},\quad
 s(\pi)=\sqrt{2T\pi\bar\ell}+\frac{2T}{3},
\]
\[
 M_1=\ceil{4\bar\ell/h},\qquad \pi_1=1/M_1.
\]
While \(x_j=\pi_j/\pi^*<2^{-8}\), set
\[
 a_j=\floor{1/(4\sqrt{x_j})},\qquad \pi_{j+1}=a_j\pi_j.
\]
Stop at the first index \(J\) with \(x_J\ge2^{-8}\). Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(a_j\ge4\), all rates lie in \((0,1]\), and
\(\pi_j=\prod_{i<j}a_i/M_1\).
\item \(\pi_1\bar\ell+s(\pi_1)\le h/2\), and
\(s(\pi_{j+1})+a_js(\pi_j)\le h/2\) for \(j<J\).
\item \(J\le\ceil{\lgx\lgx(h/T)}+1\).
\item \(s(\pi_J)/\pi_J\le86T\bar\ell/h\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since \(h\ge64T\), the second entry of the maximum gives
\(\bar\ell\ge h^2/(256T)\ge h/4\).
Thus \(4\bar\ell/h\le M_1\le8\bar\ell/h\), and
\[
 T/h\le x_1\le2T/h\le1/32.
\]
Write \(\tau(\pi)=\sqrt{2T\pi\bar\ell}
=(h/2)\sqrt{\pi/\pi^*}\).
For the first digit,
\(\pi_1\bar\ell\le h/4\),
\(\tau(\pi_1)\le\sqrt{Th/2}\le h/8\), and \(2T/3\le h/96\).

During a step, \(a_j\ge4\),
\(a_j\sqrt{x_j}\le1/4\), and
\(a_jx_j\le\sqrt{x_j}/4\), so \(\sqrt{a_jx_j}\le x_j^{1/4}/2<1/8\). Consequently
\[
 \tau(\pi_{j+1})+a_j\tau(\pi_j)
 =\frac h2\bigl(\sqrt{a_jx_j}+a_j\sqrt{x_j}\bigr)
 \le\frac{3h}{16}.
\]
The \(x_j\) increase, so
\(a_j\le\sqrt{h/T}/4\), and
\[
 (1+a_j)\frac{2T}{3}
 \le h/96+\sqrt{hT}/6\le h/32.
\]
These bounds prove (ii).
Every newly produced \(x_{j+1}\) is below \(1/64\), while
\(x_1\le1/32\). Also \(\pi^*\le32\), so all rates are at most one.

Since \(1/(4\sqrt{x_j})>4\) during a step, flooring loses at most
a factor two, and
\(x_{j+1}\ge\sqrt{x_j}/8\).
For \(y_j=\lgx(1/x_j)\), this means
\(y_{j+1}-6\le(y_j-6)/2\).
If the chain has not stopped earlier, iteration gives
\(y_j\le6+2^{-(j-1)}y_1\), with
\(y_1\le\lgx(h/T)\). A step at index \(j\) requires \(y_j>8\), hence
\(2^{j-1}<y_1/2\) and \(j<\lgx y_1\). Applied to \(j=J-1\) when
\(J\ge2\), this gives \(J<\lgx\lgx(h/T)+1\), which proves (iii).
At the stopping index, \(x_J\ge2^{-8}\), so
\(\pi_J\ge h^2/(2048T\bar\ell)\); with \(T/h\le1/64\) this gives
\[
 \frac{s(\pi_J)}{\pi_J}
 \le\frac{64T\bar\ell}{h}
       +\frac{1366T^2\bar\ell}{h^2}
 \le\frac{86T\bar\ell}{h}.
\]
This proves (iv) and completes the argument.
\end{proof}

Expand a top sum \(X\) into signed unit copies \(\sigma_tg_{i(t)}\).
Choose independent uniform \(u_t\in[0,1]\) and define
\[
 K_j=\sum_t\ind[u_t\le\pi_j]\sigma_tg_{i(t)},\qquad
 E_0=K_1,\quad E_j=K_{j+1}-a_jK_j\ (1\le j<J).
\]
Let
\[
 A=\prod_{i=1}^{J-1}a_i,\qquad A_0=A,\qquad
 A_j=\prod_{i=j+1}^{J-1}a_i\quad(1\le j<J).
\]
The identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telescope}
 K_J=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}A_jE_j,\qquad \pi_J=A/M_1
\end{equation}
follow by repeatedly substituting \(K_{j+1}=a_jK_j+E_j\).
The first identity holds for \emph{any} proposed coefficient vectors
\(K_1,\ldots,K_J\), whether or not they arose from nested sampling.
That fact will be essential for soundness.

\begin{lemma}[A good sampled chain inside one cell]\label{lem:sampled}
If a cell has live weight at most \(\ell_0\), then, with probability
at least \(1-2J2^ke^{-T}\), simultaneously for every point of the
cell and every stage,
\[
 \abs{\Delta K_j-\pi_j\Delta X}\le s(\pi_j).
\]
On this event,
\[
 \abs{\Delta E_j}\le h/2\quad(0\le j<J),\qquad
 \abs{\Delta K_J-\pi_J\Delta X}\le s(\pi_J).
\]
On a class with fixed digit vector \(E=\kappa\), the score \(X\)
then lies in an interval with at most
\(2s(\pi_J)/\pi_J+1\) integer values.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed point and stage, the error is
\[
 \sum_t(\ind[u_t\le\pi_j]-\pi_j)\sigma_t
             (g_{i(t)}(y,x)-g_{i(t)}(z_y)).
\]
The summands are independent, centered, and bounded by one.
Only live copies contribute, each with variance
\(\pi_j(1-\pi_j)\le\pi_j\), and there are at most \(\ell_0\le\bar\ell\)
of them. Thus the total variance is at most
\(\pi_j\bar\ell\). Apply \cref{lem:bernstein} and take a union bound
over the \(2^k\) points and \(J\) stages. Duplicate copies of the
same gate do not impair independence: their gate differences are
fixed coefficients, and their \(u_t\)'s are independent.

For \(E_0\), use \(\abs{\Delta X}\le\ell_0\le\bar\ell\), since only live
copies change, and
\cref{lem:chain}(ii). For later digits the mean terms cancel, since
\(\pi_{j+1}=a_j\pi_j\); the error is the difference of two stage
errors with coefficients one and \(-a_j\). The same part of the
chain lemma applies. Finally \eqref{eq:telescope} fixes \(K_J\)
on a digit class, and the last stage's error bounds \(\pi_JX\)
in an interval of length \(2s(\pi_J)\).
\end{proof}

The digits need not be functions of \(X\). Two inputs may have the
same total score while different sampled contributions fire.
We condition on the digits themselves, not on score level sets.

\subsection{Even moments certify arbitrary proposals}

The following elementary moment bound is the pointwise certification
device. The moment method and Markov's inequality are standard; the
role here is to certify a globally proposed chain using operations
that the polynomial tabulator of \cref{sec:printing} can perform.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:moment}
Let \(k\ge1\), \(v:\{0,1\}^k\to\Z\), and \(R\ge1\) be an integer.
If \(\sum_xv(x)^{2k}<R^{2k}\), then \(\abs{v(x)}<R\) everywhere.
Conversely, if \(\abs{v(x)}\le R'\) everywhere and
\(\sqrt2R'<R\), then the same moment inequality holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
One value of magnitude at least \(R\) would contribute at least
\(R^{2k}\). Conversely the sum is at most \(2^k(R')^{2k}<R^{2k}\).
\end{proof}

\section{What the fast algorithm can count}\label{sec:printing}

This section assembles established exact-threshold and polynomial
tools into the tabulation operation used later. ``Tabulate'' means
compute one answer for \emph{every} outer assignment \(y\), in time
\(2^{n-k}\) times a polynomial factor. It does not mean evaluating
all \(2^k\) points separately inside every cell.

\subsection{Thresholds as disjoint exact tests}

An exact threshold gate is \(\ind[u(z)=0]\) for an integer affine
form \(u\). Hansen and Podolskii~\cite[Theorem~7]{HP10} established polynomial-size
disjoint exact-threshold representations, and Chen and
Williams~\cite[Proposition~18(2) and Appendix~B, Lemmas~48--49]{CW19} gave a more
efficient first-crossing construction. We need only the following
conservative bound; its proof (\cref{app:exact}) records a
bounded-remainder encoding that makes all bit lengths explicit.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:exact}
Let \(g\) be a threshold gate on \(m\ge1\) inputs with integer weights
and threshold of magnitude below \(2^{B'}\). Put
\[
 B_b=B'+\ceil{\lgx(m+1)}+1,\qquad
 m_T=1+B_bm\ceil{m/2}.
\]
Then \(g\) is a sum of at most \(m_T\) exact threshold indicators,
at most one of which is one on any input. Their affine coefficients
have bit length \(O(B'+\log(m+1))\).
\end{lemma}

Two other standard facts are worth making explicit; the first is
\cite[Proposition~18(5)]{CW19}.
If integer affine forms \(u_1,\ldots,u_d\) have
\(\abs{u_i(z)}\le R\) on the cube, then
\[
 \bigwedge_{i=1}^d[u_i(z)=0]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_{i=1}^d(R+1)^{i-1}u_i(z)=0.                 \tag{*}\label{eq:pack}
\]
Indeed, if \(i_0\) is the first index with \(u_{i_0}(z)\ne0\), the sum
is \((R+1)^{i_0-1}\) times an integer congruent to
\(u_{i_0}(z)\not\equiv0\) modulo \(R+1\).
The packed coefficients use \(O(d\log(R+1))\) bits. Moreover,
\(\ind[u=0]=\ind[u\ge0]-\ind[u\ge1]\).
These observations explain why signed sums, rather than just Boolean
outputs, are useful.

\subsection{The published rectangular product and signed printing}

The fast tabulation uses one published algorithm, Williams' uniform
rectangular matrix multiplication.

\begin{import}[Williams]\label{imp:batch}
There is one deterministic multitape Turing machine that, for all
sufficiently large \(N\), multiplies over the integers any two
\(0\)-\(1\) matrices of dimensions \(N\times\ceil{N^{0.1}}\) and
\(\ceil{N^{0.1}}\times N\) in \(N^2\poly(\log N)\) steps.
\end{import}

This is \cite[Corollary~4.4, restated as Corollary~C.2]{WilACC14}. In
its Appendix~C, Williams verifies that Coppersmith's rectangular
algorithm~\cite{Cop82} is uniform and can be implemented, ``even on a
multitape TM,'' within this time. The same import underlies
\cite[Appendix~B]{NagCubic}. Williams' weighted threshold
product~\cite[Theorem~3.1]{Wil14} would allow up to \(M^{1/12}\) bottom
gates; we derive the smaller allowance we need from \cref{imp:batch}
instead, so that the only imported algorithm is a multitape matrix
product. The next two lemmas follow \cite[Appendix~B]{NagCubic}: binary
slicing handles signed entries, and Matou\v{s}ek's bucketing~\cite{Mat91}
reduces weighted dominance to rectangular products.

\begin{lemma}[Signed-by-Boolean product]\label{lem:signed-boolean-product}
Fix a constant \(\nu\ge1\). Let \(1\le d\le\ceil{u^{0.1}}\),
\(A\in\Z^{u\times d}\) and \(B\in\{0,1\}^{d\times u}\), with
\(\abs{A_{ij}}<2^b\) and \(b\le(\lgx(u+2))^\nu\). Then the integer
product \(AB\) is computable in \(u^2\poly(\log u)\) steps.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \(0\le t<b\), let \(A_t^+\) contain the \(t\)th magnitude bit of
every positive entry of \(A\), and let \(A_t^-\) contain the \(t\)th
magnitude bit of every negative entry. Both matrices are Boolean, and
\(A=\sum_{t<b}2^t(A_t^+-A_t^-)\); hence
\(AB=\sum_{t<b}2^t(A_t^+B-A_t^-B)\). Zero-pad the common dimension to
\(\ceil{u^{0.1}}\) and apply \cref{imp:batch} to the
\(2b=\poly(\log u)\) Boolean products. A bit-plane product entry lies in
\([0,d]\), so a recombined entry has at most
\(b+\ceil{\lgx(d+1)}+1=\poly(\log u)\) bits. Constructing the zero-padded planes
costs \(u\ceil{u^{0.1}}b\poly(\log u)\), and recombination at all \(u^2\) entries costs
\(u^2\poly(\log u)\). Values of \(u\) below the imported onset are handled
directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Signed exact-threshold printing]\label{lem:signed-printer}
Fix a constant \(c\ge1\). For every sufficiently large \(r\), let
\(G_i(z)=\ind[\ell_i(z)=\theta_i]\), \(1\le i\le s\), be exact threshold
gates on \(r\) Boolean variables whose integer weights and thresholds
have bit length at most \(r^c\), and let \(v_1,\ldots,v_s\) be integers
of bit length at most \(r^c\). If \(s\le2^{r/100}\), the complete table
\[
 z\longmapsto\sum_{i=1}^sv_iG_i(z),\qquad z\in\{0,1\}^r,
\]
can be printed in \(2^r\poly(r)\) steps.
\end{lemma}

\emph{Idea of proof.} Write each exact threshold as a difference of two
ordinary thresholds. Split the input into two halves. An ordinary
threshold then compares a value computed from the left half with one
computed from the right half, so the table is a weighted dominance
matrix. Matou\v{s}ek's bucketing counts the comparisons within a bucket
directly. It turns all the others into one rectangular product of a
signed matrix and a Boolean matrix, which the preceding lemma computes.

\begin{proof}
Replace every equality by two weak cuts,
\(\ind[\ell=\theta]=\ind[\ell\ge\theta]-\ind[\ell\ge\theta+1]\). The table
becomes a signed sum \(\sum_{j=1}^{s'}\omega_jH_j(z)\) of \(s'\le2s\)
threshold gates, whose coefficients are the integers \(\pm v_i\).

First let \(r=2d'\) be even, split \(z=(a,b)\) into two \(d'\)-bit
halves, and put \(N_h=2^{d'}\). Write the linear form of \(H_j\) as
\(\ell_j^{\rm L}(a)+\ell_j^{\rm R}(b)\) with threshold \(\theta_j\), and
define \(A[a,j]=\theta_j-\ell_j^{\rm L}(a)\) and
\(B[j,b]=\ell_j^{\rm R}(b)\). Then \(H_j(a,b)=\ind[A[a,j]\le B[j,b]]\), so
the desired table is the weighted dominance matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dominance}
 \mathsf P[a,b]=\sum_{j=1}^{s'}\omega_j\ind[A[a,j]\le B[j,b]].
\end{equation}
Put \(\delta=0.049\). Since \(s'\le2^{r/100+1}=2N_h^{0.02}\le N_h^\delta\)
eventually, pad the gate index to \(\ceil{N_h^\delta}\) gates with zero
coefficients. For each \(j\), sort together the \(N_h\) values
\(A[a,j]\) and the \(N_h\) values \(B[j,b]\), placing every \(A\) copy
before every equal \(B\) copy. Then \(A[a,j]\le B[j,b]\) exactly when the
former precedes the latter. Partition each sorted list into consecutive
buckets of at most \(\ceil{N_h^{1-\delta}}\) entries. For every pair
\((a,j)\), scan the \(B[j,b]\) entries in the bucket of \(A[a,j]\) and add
\(\omega_j\) for each correctly ordered pair. This inspects at most
\(N_h\ceil{N_h^\delta}\ceil{N_h^{1-\delta}}=O(N_h^2)\) pairs.

Every other correctly ordered pair has its \(B\) entry in a later bucket.
With \(h\) ranging over bucket labels, put
\begin{gather*}
 A'[a,(j,h)]=\begin{cases}\omega_j,&A[a,j]\text{ lies in bucket }h,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\\
 B'[(j,h),b]=\ind[\text{the bucket of }B[j,b]\text{ is later than }h].
\end{gather*}
Each sorted list has at most \(2N_h^\delta+1\) buckets, so the common
dimension is \(O(N_h^{2\delta})=O(N_h^{0.098})\le\ceil{N_h^{0.1}}\) for
large \(N_h\). The left matrix has signed entries of \(\poly(r)\) bits and
the right matrix is Boolean, so \cref{lem:signed-boolean-product} with
\(u=N_h\) computes \(A'B'\) exactly. Its \((a,b)\) entry is the
contribution of later buckets; adding the same-bucket contribution gives
\eqref{eq:dominance} exactly.

Sorting costs \(N_h^{1+\delta}\poly(r)\). Writing out \(A'\) and \(B'\), each with \(O(N_h^{1+2\delta})\) entries, costs \(N_h^{1+2\delta}\poly(r)\le N_h^{1.1}\poly(r)\), and every
table entry is a sum of \(O(N_h^{0.098})\) coefficients of \(\poly(r)\)
bits. With the \(O(N_h^2)\) same-bucket pairs and the \(N_h^2\poly(r)\) product of \cref{lem:signed-boolean-product}, the total is \(N_h^2\poly(r)=2^r\poly(r)\) steps. If \(r\) is odd,
add one ignored variable, run the even case, and keep the slice where it
is zero.
\end{proof}

In particular, a signed sum of at most \(2^{m/100}\) exact-threshold
indicators on \(m\) variables, with polynomial-bit parameters and
coefficients, can be tabulated in \(2^m\poly(m)\) time.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial tabulation]\label{lem:print}
Consider \(G=\poly(n)\) threshold gates on \(n\) inputs with
polynomial-bit integer parameters. Fix \(k<n\) inner coordinates,
and let \(m\) be \(n-k\) rounded up to an even integer.
Let \(Q\) be an integer multilinear polynomial of degree at most
\(D\) in \(G''\le2G\) formal variables, each standing for either
\(g_i(y,x)\) or \(g_i(y,0^I)\). Its coefficients have polynomial bit
length. It may be supplied explicitly, or expanded from a polynomial-size
arithmetic formula of formal degree at most \(D\) with polynomial-bit
intermediate coefficients.
Let \(m_T\) be the bound of \cref{lem:exact} for gates on \(n-k\)
inputs whose parameters have the bit length after restriction (see
the proof). If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:print-condition}
 k+2+D\lgx(1+G''m_T)\le m/100,
\end{equation}
then all integers \(\sum_{x\in H}Q(y,x)\) can be tabulated in
\(2^{n-k}n^{O(1)}\) time, for any fixed subset
\(H\subseteq\{0,1\}^I\). The polynomial overhead is uniform when
all bit-length bounds and \(D\) are bounded by fixed polynomials in \(n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(x\). Each polynomial variable is a threshold gate on \(y\);
fixing the inner coordinates increases its parameter bit length by
at most \(\ceil{\lgx(n+1)}+1\). Replace it by the disjoint sum in
\cref{lem:exact}. Expand a degree-\(d\) monomial and pack each
conjunction by \eqref{eq:pack}. The result is a signed sum of at most
\(m_T^d\) exact-threshold indicators. Summing over \(x\in H\), where
\(\abs H\le2^k\), gives at most
\[
 2^{k}\sum_{d\le D}\binom{G''}{d}m_T^d
 \le2^{k}(1+G''m_T)^D\le2^{m/100-2}
\]
indicators. The binomial-sum inequality also holds for \(D=0\);
for \(D\ge1\), compare each coefficient with the expansion of
\((1+G''m_T)^D\). All packed parameters have polynomial bit length.

Dense polynomial expansion costs at most
\((1+G'')^{2D}n^{O(1)}\le2^{m/50}n^{O(1)}\), and constructing the
threshold list is cheaper than the final tabulation bound. Apply
\cref{lem:signed-printer} on the full cube of \(m\) variables. If a dummy
variable was added, its two slices are identical; keep one slice.
This changes time by a factor of at most two.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fermat tests and Beigel--Tarui lifting}

The next amplifier is due to Beigel and Tarui~\cite{BT94}, building on
earlier modulus-amplification work of Toda and Yao~\cite{Tod91,Yao90}. It is also the
amplifier used in Williams' ACC analysis~\cite{WilACC14}.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:amp}
For \(e\ge1\), define
\[
 \Amp_e(t)=1-(1-t)^e\sum_{j=0}^{e-1}\binom{e-1+j}{j}t^j.
\]
This integer polynomial has degree \(2e-1\), with
\(t^e\mid\Amp_e(t)\) and \((1-t)^e\mid1-\Amp_e(t)\).
Consequently, modulo \(p^e\), it maps an integer congruent to zero
modulo \(p\) to zero, and one congruent to one to one.
\end{lemma}
This is the modulus-amplifying polynomial of Beigel and
Tarui~\cite[Section~2.3]{BT94}.
\begin{proof}
The power-series identity
\((1-t)^{-e}=\sum_{j\ge0}\binom{e-1+j}{j}t^j\) shows that truncating
at degree \(e-1\) leaves an error divisible by \(t^e\).
Multiplication by \((1-t)^e\) proves the first divisibility assertion.
The second is immediate from the definition. Both imply the stated
congruences, including for negative integer arguments.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact joint residue counts]\label{lem:residue-count}
Let \(L_1,\ldots,L_r\) be integer affine forms in the gate outputs,
let \(p\) be prime, let \(e\ge1\) be an integer, and let
\(\rho\in(\Z/p\Z)^r\), with each \(\rho_i\) represented in
\(\{0,\ldots,p-1\}\). If \(p^e>2^k\),
the Boolean multilinearization of
\[
 Q_{p,\rho}
 =\Amp_e\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^r
              \bigl(1-(L_i-\rho_i)^{p-1}\bigr)\right)
\]
has degree at most \((2e-1)r(p-1)\), and
\[
 \#\{x\in H:L_i(y,x)\equiv\rho_i\pmod p\text{ for all }i\}
 =\left(\sum_{x\in H}Q_{p,\rho}(y,x)\right)\bmod p^e.
\]
The remainder is taken in \(\{0,\ldots,p^e-1\}\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fermat's theorem makes each factor an indicator modulo \(p\).
Their product is zero or one modulo \(p\), and \cref{lem:amp} lifts
it to the corresponding indicator modulo \(p^e\). Replacing every
positive power of a Boolean variable by its first power preserves
its values and cannot increase degree. The actual count is at most
\(2^k<p^e\), so there is no ambiguity in the remainder.
\end{proof}

\section{A counting algorithm that certifies every guess}\label{sec:algorithm}

The algorithm has three allowed inputs:
one class circuit \(C^1\); two circuits with predicate
\(C^1\oplus C^2\); or one circuit with predicate
\(C^1\oplus\PAR_\Sigma\), where
\(\PAR_\Sigma(z)=\bigoplus_{i\in\Sigma}z_i\).
The last form keeps an input parity explicit rather than paying for
an unnecessary second class circuit.

We restrict the completeness and running-time analysis to the
encodings that the lower-bound argument uses. On \(n\ge4\) inputs,
let
\[
 G_0=2n^4,\qquad B_0=4n\ceil{\lgx(n+1)}.
\]
A \emph{normalized call} has total top weight \(W\le2n^4\), hence at
most \(G_0\) bottom gates (gates of top weight zero are discarded),
and integer bottom parameters of magnitude
below \(2^{B_0}\). Each \(f^r\) is given as a table on
\([-W^r,W^r]\cap\Z\). The entire encoding has polynomial length.
By \cref{lem:integer}, all circuit representations needed later can
be guessed in this form. A wrapper may return \((1/2,1/2)\) on other
valid encodings without claiming a useful estimate for them.

Fix positive integers \(B_1,B_2\). The input includes a rational
requested radius \(\beta\) satisfying \(n^{-B_1}\le\beta\le1/16\), whose
encoding uses at most \(n^{B_2}\) bits. Failed public precision or encoding
guards return the sound default. The fixed reserve \(c_T\) of
\cref{sec:finite} may depend on \(B_1,B_2\). The algorithm returns dyadic rationals
\((\phat,\khat)\) with
\(\abs{\phat-\Pr[C(z)=1]}\le\khat\).
Its proposed auxiliary data are called the \emph{witness}; it is
guessed when we use the algorithm nondeterministically.

\subsection{Parameters, with exact finite rules}\label{sec:finite}

Choose a sufficiently large fixed integer \(c_T\); its purpose is to
absorb polynomial running-time factors. Set
\[
 J_{\max}=\mu_n+1,\quad
 k=(2J_{\max}+c_T+\mu_n)\lambda_n.
\]
If \(k\ge n\), return the default interval \((1/2,1/2)\).
Otherwise use the following rules.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item Let \(T\) be the smallest nonnegative integer such that
\(2^T\beta\ge4J_{\max}2^k\). Thus
\(T\ge\ln(4J_{\max}2^k/\beta)\) and \(T=\Theta(k)\).
Let \(m\) be \(n-k\) rounded up to even, and put
\[
 B_b=B_0+\ceil{\lgx(n+1)}+\ceil{\lgx(m+1)}+2,
 \quad m_T=1+B_bm\ceil{m/2},\quad U=1+2G_0m_T.
\]
These are the quantities of \cref{lem:exact} for restricted gates,
with \(B'=B_0+\ceil{\lgx(n+1)}+1\) as in the proof of \cref{lem:print}.
\item Let \(D_{\max}\) be the largest nonnegative integer satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree-budget}
 2^{100(k+2)}U^{100D_{\max}}\le2^m.
\end{equation}
If even degree zero is inadmissible, return the default.
This is the exact integer version of
\(k+2+D_{\max}\lgx U\le m/100\).
\item For each integer \(3\le P\le\min(n,D_{\max}+1)\), let \(e_P\)
be the least positive integer with
\(P^{e_P}\ge2^{k+1+e_P}\).
Choose the largest such \(P\) satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prime-budget}
 (2e_P-1)(2J_{\max}+2)(P-1)\le D_{\max}.
\end{equation}
If none exists, return the default. Set \(e=e_P\), take all primes
\(\cP\) in \((P/2,P]\), and compute \(D'\) and \(L_2\) as in
\cref{sec:windows}. If \(\cP\) is empty, return the default.
The search for \(L_2\) is finite: admissible \(L\)'s are at most
\(D'\), and the required totients are computable by a sieve.
Use \(L_w=L_2\) for a two-circuit call and \(L_w=D'\) for the other
two kinds.
\item Set
\[
 h_E=\floor{P/4},\qquad h=\floor{\sqrt{2h_E^2}},\qquad
 R_0=\floor{\sqrt{n-k+1}},\qquad \ell_0=\frac{Wk}{\beta R_0}.
\]
Require \(P\ge100\), \(h\ge64T\), and \(2k\le D_{\max}\), or
return the default. Construct the chain of \cref{lem:chain} and
require \(J\le J_{\max}\). Finally define
\[
 \Lambda=\floor{(L_w-1)A/2}+1.
\]
\end{enumerate}

All choices depend on the normalized input, never on the witness.
The logarithmic floors and ceilings above are integer power
comparisons. Square roots are integer square roots. For rational
\(x_j\), compute \(a_j\) as the largest integer with
\(16a_j^2x_j\le1\), rather than by a real square-root operation.
The rate \(\pi^*\), all \(\pi_j\), and \(\bar\ell\) are rational.
The quantity \(s(\pi)\), which involves a real square root, is only
used in the proof and is never needed by the algorithm.
Trial division or sieving suffices for the prime list. These
preparations take polynomial time and polynomial-bit arithmetic.

If \(W=0\), the circuits are constant and the call is evaluated
exactly, using the fact that a nonempty parity has probability
\(1/2\). This branch may be taken before the parameter preparations.
All other exceptional or small cases return the sound default.

\subsection{Witnesses and the tables to be computed}

The witness specifies a \(k\)-subset \(I\) and integer coefficient
vectors for \(K_1^r,\ldots,K_J^r\), with the coefficient of gate
\(g_i^r\) in \([-\abs{w_i^r},\abs{w_i^r}]\).
A fixed encoding uses \(k\lambda_n\) bits for the ordered coordinate
list and
\[
 J\sum_{r,i}\ceil{\lgx(2\abs{w_i^r}+1)}
\]
bits for the coefficients. Invalid encodings return the default;
missing bits are treated as zeros. Only this polynomial-length
prefix is read. In particular, the algorithm cannot query an
unbounded witness as a random-access oracle.

Define the digits from these proposed \(K_j^r\)'s exactly as in
\cref{sec:chains}, and define the integer affine form
\[
 \Phi^r=M_1K_J^r-AX^r.
\]
The tabulator computes the following tables for every cell:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item For every prime \(p\in\cP\) and every residue vector, the
joint counts of all digits \(E_j^r\) and scores \(X^r\) modulo
\(p\). There are at most \(2J+2\) forms.
For a parity call, make separate counts over each fixed subset
\(H_b=\{x:\PAR_{\Sigma\cap I}(x)=b\}\), \(b=0,1\).
\item The moments
\[
 M_{E,j}^r(y)=\sum_x(\Delta E_j^r(y,x))^{2k},\qquad
 M_{\Phi}^r(y)=\sum_x(\Delta\Phi^r(y,x))^{2k}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

These operations are legal applications of \cref{lem:print}, with
the residue polynomials of \cref{lem:residue-count}.
Indeed \((P/2)^e\ge2^{k+1}\), so every \(p\in\cP\) has
\(p^e>2^k\). The residue polynomials have degree at most the left
side of \eqref{eq:prime-budget}; moments have degree at most
\(2k\le D_{\max}\). At most twice the original number of gate
variables is needed, including the reference values, and
\eqref{eq:degree-budget} supplies the printing condition.
All coefficients and packed threshold parameters have polynomial
bit length. In particular, the chain coefficients satisfy
\(A\le M_1\), because \(\pi_J=A/M_1\le1\), and \(M_1\) is
polynomially bounded on normalized calls.

\subsection{Reconstruction inside a certified cell}

Mark a cell as \emph{uncertain} unless every one of the strict tests
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tests}
 M_{E,j}^r(y)<h_E^{2k},\qquad M_{\Phi}^r(y)<\Lambda^{2k}
\end{equation}
passes. A passing cell has pointwise bounds
\(\abs{\Delta E_j^r}<h_E\) and
\(\abs{\Delta\Phi^r}<\Lambda\), by \cref{lem:moment}.

In such a cell enumerate all integer digit vectors \(\kappa\) with
\(\abs{\kappa_j^r-E_j^r(z_y)}<h_E\). Define
\[
 K^r(\kappa)=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}A_j\kappa_j^r.
\]
For each role the possible scores on that digit class lie in
\begin{align}
 B^r(\kappa)=\biggl(&X^r(z_y)+
 \biggl[
 \ceil{\frac{M_1(K^r(\kappa)-K_J^r(z_y))-\Lambda+1}{A}},\nonumber\\[-2pt]
 &\hspace{42mm}
 \floor{\frac{M_1(K^r(\kappa)-K_J^r(z_y))+\Lambda-1}{A}}
 \biggr]\biggr)\cap[-W^r,W^r]\cap\Z.             \label{eq:box}
\end{align}
This is a consecutive interval, possibly empty, and it contains at
most \(L_w\) integers, since
\[
 \#B^r(\kappa)\le\frac{2\Lambda-2}{A}+1\le L_w.
\]
The intersection with \([-W^r,W^r]\) keeps every table access in
the supplied domain of \(f^r\).

For each prime, take the tabulated joint counts at digit residue
\(\kappa\bmod p\), keeping all score residues. Use these as the
push-forward equations for a distribution \(F_\kappa\) on the
product of the intervals \(B^r(\kappa)\).
For one score use \cref{lem:one-window}; for two use
\cref{lem:two-window}. Solve exactly over \(\Q\).
If a box is empty, its class is empty in a passing cell, so its
tabulated data are zero and its contribution is zero.
If the equations are inconsistent, mark the entire cell uncertain.

Sum \(F_\kappa(u)\) times the requested Boolean predicate:
\(f^1(u_1)\), or \(f^1(u_1)\oplus f^2(u_2)\).
For a parity call do the reconstruction separately on \(H_b\), and
use \(f^1(u_1)\oplus(\PAR_{\Sigma\setminus I}(y)\oplus b)\).
The resulting count \(N_y\) must be an integer between zero and
\(2^k\); otherwise mark the cell uncertain.

Let \(N_{\mathrm{unc}}\) be the number of uncertain cells. Return
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output}
 \phat=2^{-n}\left(\sum_{y\text{ certain}}N_y+2^{k-1}N_{\mathrm{unc}}\right),
 \qquad \khat=2^{-n}2^{k-1}N_{\mathrm{unc}}.
\end{equation}
No clock is used: every step above is bounded on every input and every
witness, and \cref{prop:time} bounds the total.

\section{Why the algorithm is sound and useful}\label{sec:analysis}

\begin{proposition}[Soundness for every witness]\label{prop:sound}
Every normalized call, with every witness, returns an interval
containing its true acceptance probability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The default is sound, so consider a cell that passed all tests.
Each digit is restricted to \(2h_E-1\) consecutive values, fewer
than any prime in \(\cP\). Therefore, within this cell, specifying a
digit residue specifies its exact value. The tabulated data at
\(\kappa\bmod p\) are precisely the score-residue counts on the
\emph{exact} class \(E=\kappa\).

The identity \eqref{eq:telescope} holds for arbitrary proposed
vectors. On this class it sets \(K_J^r=K^r(\kappa)\).
The certified inequality \(\abs{\Delta\Phi^r}<\Lambda\), with
integer-valued \(\Delta\Phi^r\), then gives exactly \eqref{eq:box}.
Thus the true score distribution is supported in the reconstruction
box and solves every residue equation. Injectivity makes the
computed solution equal to that distribution. Nonnegative integer
masses follow from this equality; they are not guessed assumptions.
The parity split is just a partition of the inner assignments, so
the same reasoning applies on each part.

Every certain cell's count is therefore exact. An uncertain cell
has between zero and \(2^k\) accepting points, so replacing its
count by \(2^{k-1}\) incurs error at most \(2^{k-1}\).
Summing these errors proves \eqref{eq:output}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Existence of a useful witness]\label{prop:complete}
Suppose all preliminary checks pass and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window-condition}
 \frac{244T\bar\ell}{h}<L_w-1.
\end{equation}
Then some witness has uncertainty radius \(\khat\le\beta\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:live}, choose \(I\) for which at most a \(\beta\)
fraction of cells have live weight above \(\ell_0\); using
\(R_0\le\sqrt{n-k+1}\) only enlarges the valid live-weight bound.
Sample the unit copies globally, with independent choices for both
roles. On each cell below the live-weight bound, the probability
that either chain fails \cref{lem:sampled} is at most
\[
 4J2^ke^{-T}\le\beta.
\]
Averaging over the global samples gives one proposal whose failures,
in addition to the high-live-weight cells, occupy at most a
\(\beta\) fraction of all cells. The sampled coefficients fit the
witness format.

On every remaining cell \(\abs{\Delta E_j^r}\le h/2\), and
\(\sqrt2(h/2)<h_E\). Thus the digit moments pass.
Also, by the last-stage bound and \(\pi_J=A/M_1\),
\[
 \abs{\Delta\Phi^r}
 \le M_1s(\pi_J)
 =A\frac{s(\pi_J)}{\pi_J}
 \le\frac{86AT\bar\ell}{h}.
\]
Because \(2\sqrt2\cdot86<244\), \eqref{eq:window-condition} gives
\[
 \sqrt2\,\frac{86AT\bar\ell}{h}
 <\frac{(L_w-1)A}{2}<\Lambda.
\]
The score moments pass as well. By the proof of \cref{prop:sound},
the residue systems on these cells are consistent and return the
exact count, so these cells are certain. Their complement has fraction at most
\(2\beta\), so its contribution to the radius is at most
\(\beta\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Running time]\label{prop:time}
With \(c_T\) sufficiently large, for all sufficiently large \(n\) the
algorithm halts within \(2^n/n^{\mu_n}\) steps on every input and every
witness. It uses polynomial-bit words, reads a polynomial-length
witness prefix, and returns dyadic outputs of \(O(n)\) bits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
There are at most \(P^{2J+4}\) tabulations, including the moments
and parity split. By \cref{lem:print}, their total cost is
\(2^{n-k}n^{2J_{\max}+O(1)}\), since \(P\le n\).
Each cell has at most \(P^{2J}\) digit vectors. The reconstruction
boxes have polynomially many points: \(D'\le1+P^2\), and
\(L_2\le D'\). The residue systems also have polynomially many rows.
Their right sides are at most \(2^k\) and their matrix entries are
zero or one. Hadamard bounds on determinants give polynomial bit
lengths for the rational solutions and intermediate values in a
fraction-free elimination algorithm, such as Bareiss' integer-preserving
elimination~\cite{Bareiss68}. Consequently the per-class
cost, including exact arithmetic, is polynomial in \(n\). On a multitape
machine the tabulated values are first sorted into cell order, and inside
a cell the counts at each digit residue are gathered by sorting the digit
records; each sort adds only a polynomial factor.

Combining the costs gives
\[
 2^{n-k}n^{2J_{\max}+c_3}
\]
for a fixed exponent \(c_3\). This exponent can be chosen before
\(c_T\): the elementary bounds \(k,D_{\max},P\le n\),
\(J\le J_{\max}\le n\), and the normalized input bounds control
all arithmetic sizes by fixed polynomials. A fixed value of
\(c_T\) affects constants and the eventual onset, not those degrees.
Since \(2^k\ge n^{2J_{\max}+c_T+\mu_n}\), taking
\(c_T\ge c_3+2\) bounds the time by
\(2^n/n^{\mu_n+2}\le2^n/n^{\mu_n}\) for large \(n\). A call that fails a
parsing or public guard stops with the default after polynomially many
steps, and only a polynomial-length witness prefix is read, so the bound
holds on every input and every witness.

The same size bounds justify polynomial-bit words, including packed
threshold parameters and polynomial coefficients. Integer division,
square roots, and rational operations can instead be performed by
elementary multiword algorithms, at an additional fixed polynomial
cost already absorbed above. The witness prefix is polynomial, and
\eqref{eq:output} gives the asserted output length.
\end{proof}

\section{Parameters and the near-cubic bound}\label{sec:parameters}

The asymptotic calculation is uniform over normalized calls because
the printing budget uses the fixed bounds \(G_0,B_0\), not the
actual gate count. This avoids assuming that a small circuit has
the same parameter asymptotics as a circuit at the cap.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:asymptotics}
For the fixed choices above, as \(n\to\infty\),
\[
 k,T=\Theta(\lambda_n\mu_n),\quad
 D_{\max}=\Theta(n/\lambda_n),\quad
 P=\Theta\!\left(\frac{n}{\lambda_n\mu_n^2}\right),\quad h=\Theta(P),
\]
and
\[
 L_2=\Theta\!\left(\frac{P^{3/2}}{\lambda_n^{3/4}}\right).
\]
All preliminary checks pass for large \(n\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The definitions give \(k=\Theta(\lambda_n\mu_n)\) and
\(T=k+O(\log J_{\max}+\log(1/\beta)+1)
=k+O_{B_1}(\lambda_n)=\Theta(k)\), uniformly over the admitted precision.
Since \(k=o(n)\), we have \(m\sim n\),
\(B_b=\Theta(n\log n)\), and
\(m_T=\Theta(n^3\log n)\). Hence
\(\lgx U=7\lgx n+O(\log\log n)\), and
\eqref{eq:degree-budget} gives \(D_{\max}=\Theta(n/\lambda_n)\).

For a sufficiently small constant \(c>0\), take the candidate
\(P_0=\floor{cn/(\lambda_n\mu_n^2)}\).
Its logarithm is \((1-o(1))\lgx n\), so
\(e_{P_0}=\Theta(k/\lgx n)=\Theta(\mu_n)\).
The left side of \eqref{eq:prime-budget} is then
\(O(cn/\lambda_n)\), which is admissible for small enough \(c\).
This proves the lower bound on the selected \(P\).
For every candidate \(P\le n\), the defining inequality for \(e_P\)
gives \(e_P\ge(k+1)/\lgx(P/2)=\Omega(\mu_n)\).
Thus \eqref{eq:prime-budget} also gives the matching upper bound.
In particular \(\ln P=\Theta(\lambda_n)\).
Apply \cref{lem:primes} to obtain the bound on \(L_2\).

Now \(P/T\to\infty\), so \(h\ge64T\), and
\(D_{\max}/k\to\infty\), so \(2k\le D_{\max}\).
The other size checks are immediate. Finally \(h/T\le n\), and
\cref{lem:chain}(iii) gives \(J\le\mu_n+1=J_{\max}\).
\end{proof}


We now prove the counting interface stated in \cref{sec:overview}.
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:pair-uniform}]
The soundness proof is independent of accuracy and weight asymptotics.
For usefulness, the only remaining check is
\eqref{eq:window-condition}. If
\(\bar\ell=h^2/(256T)\), its left side is \(244h/256\), while
both \(L_2/h\) and \(D'/h\) tend to infinity. Otherwise
\(\bar\ell=\ell_0=Wk/(\beta R_0)\), and it suffices that
\[
 W<\frac{\beta R_0h(L_w-1)}{244Tk}.
\]
For pairs, \(L_w=L_2\), so this right side is
\[
 \Omega\!\left(\frac{\beta P^{5/2}\sqrt n}
 {Tk\lambda_n^{3/4}}\right)
 =\Omega\bigl(\beta\mathcal A_3(n)\bigr).
\]
For the other calls, \(L_w=D'=\Theta(P^2/\lambda_n)\), giving
\[
 \Omega\!\left(\frac{\beta P^3\sqrt n}{Tk\lambda_n}\right)
 =\Omega\bigl(\beta\mathcal A_1(n)\bigr).
\]
Take \(a_{B_1,B_2}\) strictly below both uniform constants. The prescribed
range of \(\beta\) gives \(T=k+O_{B_1}(\lambda_n)\); it changes no
power of \(\lambda_n\) or \(\mu_n\). The chain bound
\(J\le\mu_n+1\) is independent of \(\ell_0\), so reduced accuracy
does not add stages. All rational words and proposed coefficients
have polynomial bit length for fixed \(B_1,B_2\).

The normalized envelope \(W\le2n^4\) enters the parameters only through
\(G_0\), hence only through the constant in \(\lgx U=\Theta(\lambda_n)\),
which \cref{lem:asymptotics} already uses. A fixed larger \(c_T\) absorbs
the polynomial-bit arithmetic, accuracy encoding and any prescribed
polynomial call census in \cref{prop:time}. Every malformed proposal
is rejected or yields the sound default. This proves the claim.
\end{proof}


\section{Published complexity-theoretic inputs}\label{sec:imports}

The counter now provides trustworthy intervals. The remaining task
is to turn them into a hard function. This section records the
published tools used for that conversion, with their assumptions
kept separate from the new counting argument.

There are two input scales to keep in mind. The hierarchy construction
starts from a long source input of length \(N\). Its verifier uses
only \(q=O(\log N)\) random bits, so the functions submitted to the
counter have \(q\) inputs. Encoding proof addresses and amplifying
hardness later produce the final input length \(n\). A size bound
at width \(q\) is not yet a size bound at length \(n\); both transfers
explicitly pay for this change.

The short PCP compresses verification to a short random seed. Its
circuit grows polynomially with its explicit input. So we apply it only
to an explicit input of length polynomial in \(q\): the description of
a small circuit that the weak machine checks against the whole source
input. The PCPPs replace a verifier by local constraints on an encoded
input and auxiliary proof. The fixed-machine refuter supplies a
hard instance at every large source length, rather than only
infinitely often. General-circuit amplification is used only in
the separate case where a small source circuit or a small outer proof
circuit does not exist.

\begin{import}[Short PCP, in the form of Murray--Williams]\label{imp:short-pcp}
In particular, for every verifier algorithm \(V(x,y)\) running in time
\(T(n)\ge n\), where \(T(n)\) is computable from \(n\) in
\(\poly(n,\log T(n))\) time, there is an algorithm \(A\) running in
\(\poly(n,\log T(n))\) time which, given \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\), outputs an
oracle circuit \(C^O_x\) with the following properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(C^O_x\) has \(\ell=\log_2(T(n))+O(\log\log T(n))\) inputs,
\(\poly(n,\log T(n))\) size, and each copy of the oracle gate \(O\) in
\(C^O_x\) has \(\ell\) inputs as well.
\item If there is a \(y\) of length \(T(n)\) such that \(V(x,y)\)
accepts, then there is an oracle \(O:\{0,1\}^\ell\to\{0,1\}\) such that
\(C^O_x\) is unsatisfiable.
\item If \(V(x,y)\) rejects on all \(y\) of length \(T(n)\), then for
every oracle \(O:\{0,1\}^\ell\to\{0,1\}\), \(C^O_x\) has at least
\(2^\ell\cdot(1-1/n)\) satisfying assignments.
\end{itemize}
\end{import}
This is the statement of Murray and Williams~\cite[Section~5, in the
proof of Theorem~1.4, pp.~STOC18-316--STOC18-317]{MW20}, quoted verbatim
except that ``they give an algorithm'' reads ``there is an algorithm'',
and that the computability of \(T\) is stated. Its opening ``In
particular'' refers to their preceding sentence, that the verifier of
Ben-Sasson and Viola ``yields proofs of \(\tilde O(T(n))\) length for all
running times \(T(n)\)''. Murray and Williams leave
the computability hypothesis implicit, but within its time bound the
algorithm \(A\) must output a circuit with
\(\ell=\log_2T(n)+O(\log\log T(n))\) inputs, and hence determine
\(\log T(n)\) to within \(O(\log\log T(n))\); the time
bound to which we apply the input satisfies the hypothesis
(\cref{lem:succinct-verifier}(i)). They credit the
construction to Ben-Sasson and Viola~\cite{BV14}, noting that it ``is
not formally stated in their article, but it is immediate from their
Theorem~1.1''~\cite[footnote~5]{MW20}. As printed, that theorem
fails at polynomial time bounds unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\) (\cref{rem:bv14-printed}).
The statement of Murray and Williams is
weaker. Section~4.4 of~\cite{BV14} obtains it from the verifier of
earlier work; we rely instead on our
proof of the case used here (\cref{thm:short-pcp-ours}). The convention is inverted:
\(C^O_x\) is unsatisfiable when the oracle is accepted on every random
string, and its satisfying assignments are the rejecting random strings.
The size bound keeps the dependence on the explicit input length \(n\).
We apply the input once, to one fixed verifier whose explicit inputs are
short descriptions of circuits (\cref{sec:gadget-transfer},
\cref{lem:succinct-verifier}).

\Cref{app:short-pcp-proof} proves \cref{imp:short-pcp} for
deterministic multitape verifiers with computable time bounds, the case
used here (\cref{thm:short-pcp-ours}). We include this proof for the
following reason. The argument of~\cite[Section~4]{BV14} takes its
polylogarithmic-time verifier from earlier work (``all points but for
preprocessing were proved in [BS08, BGH+05]''~\cite[Section~4.2]{BV14}).
The full construction of such a verifier is written out by Ben-Sasson,
Chiesa, Genkin, and Tromer. They describe their construction as
implying ``a proof of a theorem left implicit in''~\cite{BGHSV05}
(\cite[Remark~8.9]{BCGT13}). The proof in \cref{app:short-pcp-proof}
uses their construction~\cite{BCGT13,BSCGT13} with a
randomness-efficient soundness amplification.

\begin{remark}[The printed form of {\cite[Theorem~1.1]{BV14}}]
\label{rem:bv14-printed}
As printed, \cite[Theorem~1.1]{BV14}, restated as
\cite[Lemma~3.10]{CLW20}, asserts more for every \(T(n)\ge n\): query
circuits \(\mathrm{Query}:\{0,1\}^r\to[2^r]^t\) with
\(t=\poly(r)\), a 3CNF decision predicate on the \(t\) answers,
\(2^r\le T\polylog T\), perfect completeness, and soundness
\(n^{-10}\). This form cannot hold as stated unless \(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\). Apply it to a verifier for
SAT running in time \(T(n)=O(n^2)\). For large \(n\), \(2^r\le T\polylog T<n^{10}\),
so on an unsatisfiable instance no random string is accepted. Hence an
instance is satisfiable exactly when the 3CNF decision predicate at the
single random string \(0^r\), a formula on at most
\(t=\poly(r)=\polylog n\) distinct proof bits, is satisfiable. After
duplicate clauses are deleted, this maps SAT instances of size \(n\) to SAT instances of size
\(\polylog n\) in polynomial time, and iterating it decides SAT in
polynomial time. The argument of~\cite[Section~4.4]{BV14} gives query
and decision complexity \(\poly(n,\log T)\), which is \(\poly(r)\) only
when \(\log T\ge n^{\Omega(1)}\), as in its applications to
\(\NTIME(2^n)\). We use only \cref{imp:short-pcp}.
\end{remark}

\begin{import}[Pointwise two-query PCPP]\label{imp:pcpp}
There are constants \(0<s<c<1\), a polynomial-time computable linear
encoding \(\Enc\), and a polynomial-time transformation taking a
Boolean circuit \(D\) on \(n_D\) inputs, of size at least \(n_D\), to
a polynomial-size 2-SAT instance \(\Psi_D\) on variables \(Y\cup Z\),
with \(\abs Y=\poly(n_D)\) and \(\abs Z=\poly(\abs D)\), such that
for every \(x\):
\begin{itemize}
\item If \(D(x)=1\), some assignment to \(Z\), under
\(Y=\Enc(x)\), satisfies at least a \(c\) fraction of clauses.
\item If \(D(x)=0\), every assignment to \(Z\), under the same
\(Y\), satisfies at most an \(s\) fraction.
\end{itemize}
Each coordinate of \(\Enc(x)\) is a parity of input coordinates.
The number of clauses is a power of two. When \(D(x)=1\), an
honest auxiliary assignment can be computed from \(x\) in time
polynomial in \(\abs D\).
\end{import}

We use the packaged pointwise statement of
Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite[Lemma~3.11]{CLW20}, attributed there to
Chen--Williams and Vyas--Williams~\cite{CW19,VW20}. Chen--Williams
give the two-query system and the linear code in their Lemmas~25
and~27. The underlying robust proximity construction is due to
Ben-Sasson, Goldreich, Harsha, Sudan, and Vadhan~\cite{BGHSV06}.
The conversion to two-query clauses uses the classical MAX-2-SAT
gadget of Garey, Johnson, and Stockmeyer~\cite{GJS76}, as described
in \cite[Lemma~47]{CW19}. These are published inputs, not new PCP
constructions. The parity supports are found by applying the
efficient encoder to each basis vector.

By slightly decreasing \(c\) and increasing \(s\), we may and do
choose them rational while retaining \(s<c\). These constants are
used in the periodic correlation transfer (\cref{sec:per-transfer}).
The native counter takes its accuracy as a rational input; no real-number
comparisons enter either verifier.



\begin{import}[Fixed-machine refuter]\label{imp:ae-refuter}
For every time-constructible \(N\le \Tclk(N)\le2^{\poly(N)}\), there
are a fixed language \(L_{\Tclk}\in\NTIME[\Tclk(N)]\) and a deterministic
SAT-oracle procedure \(R_{\Tclk}\) with the following property. For each
fixed nondeterministic machine \(M\) running in \(o(\Tclk(N))\) time
and guessing at most \(N/10\) bits, the procedure
\(R_{\Tclk}(M,1^N)\) returns, for every sufficiently large \(N\), a word
\(z_N\in\{0,1\}^N\) on which \(M\) and \(L_{\Tclk}\) disagree.
Its running time is \(\poly(\Tclk(N))\).
\end{import}
This is \cite[Theorem~1.13]{CLW20}, read for multitape Turing machines.
Its proof in \cite[Section~4]{CLW20}, with formal version Theorem~4.6,
adapts the almost-everywhere hierarchy of Fortnow--Santhanam~\cite{FS16}
and assumes random-access machines for simplicity. \Cref{lem:multitape-refuter}
in \cref{app:multitape} derives the multitape reading. There the
diagonal language runs each enumerated machine through the
nondeterministic linear-time universal two-tape simulation
of~\cite{FS16} under an \(O(\Tclk(N))\) clock, and the rest of the
proof is unchanged. The Lean proof of this
paper's main theorem uses the same reading. Our weak machines
guess at most \(\floor{N/16}\) bits, within the promise. We do not
introduce a guessed computation trace for them: that would needlessly
increase the number of nondeterministic bits. Arithmetic on
polynomial-bit integers is expanded into polynomially many machine
steps. This is deterministic overhead, absorbed below by the counting
algorithm's superpolynomial saving.

\begin{import}[General-circuit hardness amplification]
\label{imp:ae-amplification}
There is an absolute constant \(b_{\rm amp}\ge1\) with the following
property. For every fixed integer \(G\ge b_{\rm amp}\) there is a constant
\(a_G\ge1\) such that, if a \(q\)-input Boolean function has no general
Boolean circuit of size \(q^G\), its truth table determines, in
\(2^{O(q)}\) time, a function on at most \(a_Gq\) inputs having agreement
less than \(1/2+q^{-G/b_{\rm amp}}\) with every general circuit of size
\(q^{G/b_{\rm amp}}\).
\end{import}
We use \cite[Lemma~3.9]{CLW20} with \(S(q)=q^G\); it is attributed there to
the worst-case-to-average-case amplification of Sudan, Trevisan, and
Vadhan~\cite[Theorem~24]{STV01}. Its constant \(c=b_{\rm amp}\) is fixed
before the size function, while the \(O(q)\) arity and the \(2^{O(q)}\)
construction time may depend on \(S\), hence on \(G\). We state it only
for \(G\ge b_{\rm amp}\), so that \(S(q)=q^G\ge q^{b_{\rm amp}}\);
every use below has \(G>b_{\rm amp}\). Every use below
fixes \(G\) first. We apply this input only in the branches where the
source input or an outer PCP oracle is hard for \emph{general} circuits.
The Lean proof states \cite[Lemma~3.9]{CLW20} as printed, for every
time-constructible \(S\). At lengths with \(S(q)^{1/b_{\rm amp}}<q\) that
statement holds with the parity function: a circuit with fewer nodes than
inputs (size counts input nodes, as in the Lean statement) ignores some input, so it agrees with parity on exactly half of the
inputs. At the other lengths it follows from \cite[Theorem~24]{STV01} or
holds vacuously.



\section{Almost-everywhere hardness from a weighted Boolean proof table}
\label{sec:gadget-transfer}

The transfer keeps every guessed proof coordinate Boolean. Its useful
refinement is to measure the error of the classical two-query gadget as
a whole. The ten clauses of that gadget admit a quadratic penalty with
a weighted stability bound of total weight five. We therefore address
five weighted proof coordinates, rather than all twenty literal
positions of the ten two-literal clauses (unit clauses are written as
\(X\vee X\), as in \cite[footnote~17]{CW19}). The probability estimates still involve at most two
target circuits.

Recall from \cref{thm:pair-uniform} that
\[
 \mathcal A_3(n)=\frac{n^3}{\lambda_n^{21/4}\mu_n^7}.
\]
The constant $\gamma$ below is a rational lower bound on the rejection
gap of a perfectly complete three-query PCPP (\cref{imp:three-pcpp}). It
is fixed throughout this section.

\subsection{The published proximity construction and its local gadget}

\begin{import}[Pointwise three-query PCPP]\label{imp:three-pcpp}
There are a rational constant $0<\gamma<1$, a polynomial-time
computable linear encoding $\operatorname{Enc}$, and a polynomial-time
transformation taking a Boolean circuit $D$ on $q$ inputs to a
polynomial-size list of three-literal clauses on variables $Y\cup Z$,
such that the following hold for every $x\in\{0,1\}^q$ with
$Y=\operatorname{Enc}(x)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $D(x)=1$, an auxiliary assignment computable in polynomial
time satisfies every clause.
\item If $D(x)=0$, every auxiliary assignment leaves at least a
$\gamma$ fraction of the clauses false.
\end{itemize}
Each systematic coordinate is a parity of input coordinates. The
number of clauses can be taken to be a power of two.
\end{import}

This is the pointwise form of Chen--Williams' perfectly complete
three-query PCPP~\cite[Lemma~24 and Appendix~A]{CW19}, using their
linear code and decoder~\cite[Lemma~27]{CW19}. As in the proof of
\cite[Theorem~5]{CW19}, here is the reduction to the stated form. Take a fixed proximity radius smaller than the
code's decoding radius, totalize the decoder by returning an arbitrary
fixed string on failure, and apply the PCPP to
$\widetilde D(y)=D(\operatorname{Dec}(y))$. If $D(x)=0$, every word sufficiently
close to $\operatorname{Enc}(x)$ decodes to $x$, and is therefore
rejected by $\widetilde D$. Thus $\operatorname{Enc}(x)$ is farther than the
proximity radius from every accepting input of $\widetilde D$, and proximity
soundness applies. If $D(x)=1$, perfect completeness and the efficient
honest-proof procedure of~\cite[Lemma~24]{CW19} apply. The underlying proximity construction
is due to Ben-Sasson, Goldreich, Harsha, Sudan, and
Vadhan~\cite{BGHSV06}. In the constant-size clause conversion in
the proof of \cite[Lemma~24]{CW19}, pad to a power-of-two number of
clauses with tautologies before selecting a clause uniformly. This
preserves perfect completeness and changes only the fixed rejection
constant. Finally choose a smaller rational $\gamma$ if necessary.

For one three-literal clause, let $x_1,x_2,x_3$ be its literal values.
The classical Garey--Johnson--Stockmeyer gadget~\cite{GJS76}, in
the form recorded by Chen--Williams~\cite[Lemma~47]{CW19}, introduces
a Boolean helper $y$ and uses the ten constraints
\[
 x_1,\ x_2,\ x_3,\quad
 \neg x_1\vee\neg x_2,\
 \neg x_2\vee\neg x_3,\
 \neg x_3\vee\neg x_1,\quad
 y,\quad x_1\vee\neg y,\ x_2\vee\neg y,\ x_3\vee\neg y.
\]
If $k=x_1+x_2+x_3$, their total satisfaction score is
$\operatorname{sat}(x,y)=6+k-\binom{k}{2}+y(k-2)$. We use the penalty
\begin{align}
 Q(x_1,x_2,x_3,y)
 &=7-\operatorname{sat}(x,y)\notag\\
 &=1-\sum_i x_i+\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j+2y-y\sum_i x_i.
 \label{eq:gadget-penalty}
\end{align}
Its values on Boolean arguments are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 k&0&1&2&3\\ \hline
 Q(k,0)&1&0&0&1\\
 Q(k,1)&3&1&0&0
\end{array}
\]
Thus $Q\ge0$, and a false original clause forces $Q\ge1$ for
either helper. For a true original clause, the choice
$y^*=x_1x_2x_3$ gives penalty zero.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted stability of the gadget]
\label{lem:gadget-stability}
For every true honest triple $x\ne000$, with $y^*=x_1x_2x_3$, and
every Boolean replacement $x',y'$, we have
\[
 Q(x',y')\le\sum_{i=1}^3|x_i-x_i'|+2|y^*-y'|.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $y'=y^*=0$, the honest triple has weight one or two. The only
nonzero penalties with helper zero occur at weights zero and three,
each requiring an input change. If $y'=y^*=1$, the honest triple
is $111$, and the table gives $Q(k',1)\le3-k'$, its Hamming distance
from that triple. If the helper changes, its weight already pays two.
The only penalty exceeding two is $Q(0,1)=3$; this also requires at
least one input change from a true honest triple.
\end{proof}

The aggregate seven-versus-six score is classical~\cite{GJS76}, and
Chen--Williams use it in this aggregate form to prove
\cite[Lemma~25]{CW19}; what is added here is its weighted stability
under proof-table errors. This estimate uses cancellation within the
ten-clause score. Applying
a separate union bound to every clause would discard that cancellation.
The helper has weight two, and each original coordinate has weight one.

\subsection{A certified upper estimate using pair means}

Writing the two-literal clauses in the XOR basis, as Chen--Williams
do for two-query predicates~\cite[proof of Theorem~5]{CW19}, the
identity $ab=(a+b-(a\oplus b))/2$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-pair-identity}
 Q=1+\frac12\left(
 y-\sum_i x_i-\sum_{i<j}(x_i\oplus x_j)
                 +\sum_i(x_i\oplus y)\right).
\end{equation}
There are ten probability terms, all with coefficient of magnitude
$1/2$. A literal is a guessed Boolean proof coordinate or its
complement, or a prescribed systematic parity or its complement. Hence every nonexact
term is a single-circuit, pair-XOR, or circuit-XOR-raw-parity call.
Pure parity terms, constants, and repeated-coordinate identities are
evaluated exactly. No closure of the target class under XOR is used.

For each nonexact term let $(\widehat p,\widehat\kappa)$ be the
counter's estimate and certified radius, and let $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$
be its sign in \cref{eq:gadget-pair-identity}. Substitute
$\sigma\widehat p+\widehat\kappa$ for the signed probability and
average the resulting expressions over all gadgets. Call the result
$U$, and write $\mathbb E Q$ for the exact average of the penalty over
gadgets and over $x\in\{0,1\}^q$, evaluated on the guessed coordinates.
Soundness of the intervals gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-upper}
 U\ge\mathbb E Q\quad\text{for every witness}.
\end{equation}
For a useful witness (\cref{prop:complete}) with all radii at most
$\omega$, it also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-upper-complete}
 U\le\mathbb E Q+10\omega.
\end{equation}
Indeed a signed probability endpoint loses at most $2\omega$, and
its coefficient has magnitude $1/2$. Arbitrary witnesses may return
broader intervals; only the existence of a useful witness asserts the
small-radius bound.

\subsection{The arbitrary-lookup theorem and its common-language refinement}

The fixed-error statement is \cref{thm:main}. We prove it together
with the following refinement, using the same verifier and different
accuracy schedules.

\begin{theorem}[One language approaching the fixed-error boundary]
\label{thm:gadget-boundary}
Fix a nondecreasing function $\rho:\N\to\N$, computable in time
polynomial in its argument, such that $\rho(u)\to\infty$ and
$\rho(u)=o(u)$. There are a positive integer $K$ and one language
$F_\rho\in\ENP$ such that, for every sufficiently large $n$ and every
$C\in\cW$,
\[
 W(C)\le
 \left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal A_3(n)}{K2^{\rho(2\lambda_n)}}\right\rfloor
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{agr}(C,F_\rho)<1-\frac\gamma5+2^{-\rho(\lambda_n)}.
\]
In particular, this same language works for every fixed
$A,\alpha>0$ and every fixed $d<\gamma/5$: eventually every
circuit of weight at most $An^{3-\alpha}$ disagrees with it
on more than a $d$ fraction of inputs.
\end{theorem}

The cap in \cref{thm:gadget-boundary} is $n^{3-o(1)}$. The limiting
disagreement bound remains a fixed constant. By output complementation,
the absolute signed correlation is less than
$1-2\gamma/5+2^{1-\rho(\lambda_n)}$.

\paragraph{One language approaching the fixed-error boundary.}
\Cref{thm:main} fixes \(d\) first. A different accuracy schedule
gives one language whose forced error approaches \(\gamma/5\), at the
cost of one additional logarithmic factor.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:main-boundary}
There are a positive integer \(C_0\) and one \(F\in\ENP\) such that,
for every sufficiently large \(n\) and every \(C\in\cW\),
\[
 W(C)\le\floor{\frac{n^3}{C_0\lambda_n^{25/4}\mu_n^7}}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{agr}(C,F)<1-\frac\gamma5+\frac1{\lambda_n}.
\]
\end{corollary}

This is the case \(\rho(u)=\ceil{\lgx(u+2)}\) of the general scheduling
statement, \cref{thm:gadget-boundary}. It still gives constant-error
hardness, not vanishing correlation.

\subsection{A fixed weak machine and its source input}
\label{sec:gadget-machine}

The next three subsections prove \cref{thm:main,thm:gadget-boundary}.

\emph{Idea of proof.} The steps are to construct a verifier sound on
every guess, extract a hard Boolean proof table from a rejected
yes-instance, and then account for every final input length.
\begin{itemize}
\item This subsection builds one fixed weak machine
(\cref{fig:outer-step}). It guesses a small source circuit for its
input, checks it at every position, and applies the short PCP to the
circuit's description. It then guesses a proof-oracle circuit, applies
the three-query PCPP, and accepts when the certified estimate \(U\) of
the gadget penalty is small. PCPP soundness makes every branch reject a
hierarchy no-instance. The counter's time saving keeps the machine fast
enough for the refuter.
\item \Cref{sec:gadget-extract} fixes a yes-instance that the machine
rejects. Either some function on \(q\) inputs is hard for general
circuits, and the amplifier finishes the proof, or a canonical pair of
circuits makes the outer verifier a tautology. In the second case, a
target circuit that approximated the honest weighted proof table too
well would yield an accepting branch.
\item \Cref{sec:all-lengths} pads the hard function to every final
length and recovers it in \(\ENP\).
\end{itemize}

The constants of this subsection are chosen in the order $\epsilon$ (from
$d$ or $\rho$ and $\gamma$), $\eta_0$ (from \cref{thm:pair-uniform}), $d_0$,
$G$, $G'$, $C_{\rm clk}$, $A_{\rm pad}$, $d_G$, and finally the padding and
cap constants; the order-of-constants list at the end of
\cref{app:multitape} checks that no constant depends on one chosen after
it. Both theorems use one parameter template. For the fixed-error
theorem, choose a fixed $\epsilon=2^{-e_0}$, for a positive integer $e_0$, with
$\epsilon<\gamma/10$ and $d<\gamma/5-\epsilon$, and set
$\epsilon_q=\epsilon$. For the boundary theorem, set
\[
 \epsilon_q=2^{-\rho(2\lambda_q)}.
\]
In both cases put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-parameters}
 P_q=128/\epsilon_q,\qquad
 \omega_q=\epsilon_q/128,\qquad
 b(q)=\left\lfloor\eta_0\epsilon_q\mathcal A_3(q)\right\rfloor,
\end{equation}
where $\eta_0>0$ is a fixed rational smaller than $a_{1,1}/512$,
and $a_{1,1}$ is the constant in \cref{thm:pair-uniform} for
inverse-polynomial precision exponent and bit envelope both equal to
one. Think of $P_q$ as the number of addresses per gadget (the honest
table repeats each of its five entries about $P_q/5$ times), of
$\omega_q$ as the radius requested from the counter, and of $b(q)$ as the
native weight cap, an $\eta_0\epsilon_q$ fraction of the counter's pair
cap. The integer $P_q$ is a power of two. Eventually
$\omega_q\ge q^{-1}$, its representation has at most $q$ bits,
and $\epsilon_q<\gamma/10$. The weak machine checks these conditions
before guessing anything. Two circuits of weight at most $b(q)$
have total weight below $a_{1,1}\omega_q\mathcal A_3(q)$.
Check the exponent and representation-length bounds before expanding
any power of two; failed finite public guards cause immediate rejection.

We use the published short PCP, general-circuit amplifier, and
fixed-machine refuter from \cref{sec:imports}. There is a fixed
integer $h_{\rm sim}$ such that, for every fixed $A\ge1$ and all
sufficiently large $q$, every target circuit on at most $Aq$ inputs and
of weight at most $q^3$ has a general Boolean simulation of size
$q^{h_{\rm sim}}$. Normalize its bottom thresholds, evaluate their integer
linear forms, form the top sum, and look up the polynomial-length output
table. All integers have polynomial bit length, and the size is a fixed
polynomial in $q$ and the number of inputs, so one exponent serves every
fixed $A$. Choose a fixed integer $G$ with $G/b_{\rm amp}>h_{\rm sim}+1$,
using the exponent in \cref{imp:ae-amplification}, and let $a_G$ be its
arity constant for this $G$.

Take $\Tclk(N)=N^{C_{\rm clk}}$. The hierarchy language
$L_{\Tclk}$ of \cref{imp:ae-refuter} has a nondeterministic multitape
verifier $\mathcal D$ that halts within $O(\Tclk(N))$ steps on every input and witness (\cref{lem:multitape-refuter}), with no short-witness
restriction. Only source lengths $N=2^\nu$ are used
(\cref{sec:all-lengths}); the weak machine rejects every input whose
length is not a power of two.

The circuit of \cref{imp:short-pcp} grows with the length of its
explicit input, so the short PCP is not applied to the source input
itself. Instead the weak machine guesses a small general circuit for
the source input, checks it at every position, and applies the short
PCP to the description of that circuit.

For a general circuit $B$ with
$a\ge\nu$ inputs, let $\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\in\{0,1\}^N$ be the word whose
$i$th bit is $B(\mathrm{bin}_\nu(i)0^{a-\nu})$, where
$\mathrm{bin}_\nu(i)$ is the $\nu$-bit binary expansion of $i<N$. Fix a
self-delimiting binary description of general circuits that lists the
gates, so that a description of $n$ bits has at most $n$ gates, and
whose length is polynomial in the numbers of gates and inputs. Fix an integer
$G'\ge2$, depending only on $G$ and on this description format, such
that for all large $\nu$ every description of a circuit with at most
$q^G$ gates on $q$ inputs has fewer than $\nu^{G'}$ bits. Such a $G'$
exists: these descriptions have length at most a fixed polynomial in
$q=O(\nu)$ whose degree depends only on $G$ and the format, and any $G'$
above that degree works. Put
\[
 L_\nu=\nu^{G'},
\]
and, when the description of $B$ has at most $L_\nu$ bits, let
$\langle B\rangle_\nu\in\{0,1\}^{L_\nu}$ be that description followed by
zeros.

\Cref{lem:succinct-verifier} in \cref{app:multitape} gives one
deterministic multitape verifier $\mathcal V_G(u,y)$, a nondecreasing
time bound $T_G(n)\ge n$ within which $\mathcal V_G$ halts on every input
of length $n$ and every witness, and a constant $c_G$. They satisfy
$T_G(L_\nu)=c_G\Tclk(N)$, and, for every such $B$, some witness of length
$T_G(L_\nu)$ makes $\mathcal V_G$ accept $\langle B\rangle_\nu$ exactly
when $\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\in L_{\Tclk}$. The only size bound that
$\mathcal V_G$ places on $B$ is the length $L_\nu$, which depends on
$N$ and the fixed $G'$ alone; the weak machine enforces the bound $q^G$
separately. Only three properties of $L_\nu$ are used:
\begin{itemize}
\item it is a fixed polynomial in $\nu$;
\item it has degree at least two;
\item it eventually exceeds the description length of every circuit
with at most $q^G$ gates on $q$ inputs.
\end{itemize}
$L_\nu$ is fixed before $\mathcal V_G$, and these
properties hold whatever constants are chosen after it.

We apply \cref{imp:short-pcp} once, to $\mathcal V_G$ with the time bound
$T_G$, which is computable from the input length in polynomial time, as
that input requires (\cref{lem:succinct-verifier}(i)). On an input $\langle B\rangle_\nu$ it constructs, in time
$\poly(L_\nu,\log T_G(L_\nu))=\nu^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}$, an oracle circuit
$C^O_{\langle B\rangle_\nu}$ of size $\nu^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}$ with
$\ell_{\rm out}=\lgx\Tclk(N)+O(\log\log\Tclk(N))$ inputs. Pad it to the
exact native width
\[
 q(N)=\lceil\log_2\Tclk(N)\rceil+
 A_{\rm pad}\lceil\log_2(\log_2\Tclk(N)+2)\rceil+A_{\rm pad},
\]
by adding inputs that it ignores, for a sufficiently large fixed integer
$A_{\rm pad}$, chosen after $C_{\rm clk}$ as at the end of
\cref{app:multitape} so that $\ell_{\rm out}\le q(N)$. Then
$q=\Theta(\log N)$ and $2^q/\Tclk(N)=q^{O(1)}$.

On a source input $z\in\{0,1\}^N$, the weak machine first rejects
unless $N=2^\nu$ is a power of two. It computes a
fixed polynomial envelope for all its guesses and rejects if it
exceeds $\lfloor N/16\rfloor$. It also checks the precision guards
above and $\gamma/L_\nu<\epsilon_q$, and it rejects unless the
description of the source circuit $B_{\rm src}$ guessed below has at
most $L_\nu$ bits. By the choice of $G'$, once $\nu$ is large every
general circuit with $q$ inputs and at most $q^G$ gates has such a
description, so the last guard rejects at only finitely many lengths.
Since $q=\Theta(\nu)$ and
$G'\ge2$, $\gamma/L_\nu=O(q^{-2})$, while $\epsilon_q\ge128/q$
eventually under both schedules; so these guards, too, fail at only
finitely many lengths.

The machine guesses a general circuit $B_{\rm src}$ with $q$ inputs and
at most $q^G$ gates, the \emph{source circuit}, rejecting malformed
descriptions. It then checks deterministically that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-check}
 B_{\rm src}\bigl(\mathrm{bin}_\nu(i)0^{q-\nu}\bigr)=z_i
 \qquad\text{for every }i<N,
\end{equation}
that is, $\mathrm{tt}_N(B_{\rm src})=z$, and rejects otherwise. It
constructs $C^O_{\langle B_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}$, whose number
$\ell_{\rm out}$ of inputs may depend on $B_{\rm src}$, and guesses a
general circuit $B_{\rm pf}$ with $q$ inputs and at most $q^G$ gates,
the outer proof oracle, again rejecting malformed descriptions. It
answers an oracle query $a\in\{0,1\}^{\ell_{\rm out}}$ by
\[
 O_{B_{\rm pf}}(a)=B_{\rm pf}\bigl(a0^{q-\ell_{\rm out}}\bigr),
\]
the same zero padding as in \eqref{eq:source-check}. Write
$B=(B_{\rm src},B_{\rm pf})$. Substituting $O_{B_{\rm pf}}$ for every
oracle gate and negating the output gives the outer verifier
\[
 D_B(x)=\neg\,C^{O_{B_{\rm pf}}}_{\langle B_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}
 (x_1,\ldots,x_{\ell_{\rm out}}),\qquad x\in\{0,1\}^q,
\]
which ignores its last $q-\ell_{\rm out}$ inputs. The negation turns
the convention of \cref{imp:short-pcp} into the usual one: $D_B(x)=1$
means that the outer verifier accepts the random string $x$. Since
$L_\nu\le q^{G'}$, $D_B$ is a circuit of size $q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}$ on $q$
inputs. The exponent depends on $C_{\rm clk}$ as well, through
$\mathcal V_G$; this is harmless, because the constants that depend on
it, beginning with the exponent $d_G$ below, are chosen after
$C_{\rm clk}$ (\cref{app:multitape}). Apply \cref{imp:three-pcpp}. Choose a fixed $d_G$ bounding
all resulting clause counts and repeat the whole clause list to
exactly
\[
 M=2^{\lceil d_G\log_2(q+2)\rceil}
\]
clauses. If the list is longer than $M$, which happens at only
finitely many lengths, the machine rejects. The original length divides
this number. This fixes the eventual table arity independently of the
guessed pair.

For each resulting three-literal clause introduce a fresh helper name,
disjoint from all original auxiliary and systematic names. The
machine guesses one normalized target circuit of weight at most
$b(q)$ for each distinct auxiliary name, including every fresh
helper. All appearances of one name use this same circuit.
Systematic coordinates are supplied exactly as the prescribed
parities. Reject malformed or oversized descriptions. The weight test $W\le b(q)$ is the
integer comparison $W^4\lambda_q^{21}\mu_q^{28}\le(\eta_0\epsilon_q)^4q^{12}$. Only existence
of normalized integer descriptions is required; an efficient
normalization algorithm is not assumed.

Finally guess the polynomial-length witness prefixes for the nonexact
counter calls in \cref{eq:gadget-pair-identity}, each requesting
radius $\omega_q$, form the certified upper
estimate $U$, and accept exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-acceptance}
 U<\gamma-\epsilon_q.
\end{equation}

On a hierarchy no-instance $z$, consider a branch whose source circuit
passes the check \eqref{eq:source-check}; every other branch rejects.
Then $\mathrm{tt}_N(B_{\rm src})=z\notin L_{\Tclk}$, so $\mathcal V_G$
rejects $\langle B_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu$ with every witness, and by
\cref{imp:short-pcp} the circuit
$C^{O_{B_{\rm pf}}}_{\langle B_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}$ is satisfied by at
least a $1-1/L_\nu$ fraction of its inputs. Hence $D_B(x)=1$
on at most a $1/L_\nu$ fraction of inputs. On every remaining
input, \cref{imp:three-pcpp} forces at least a $\gamma$ fraction
of false original clauses, each contributing penalty at least one.
All other penalties are nonnegative. Consequently every Boolean
guess satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E Q\ge\gamma(1-1/L_\nu)>\gamma-\epsilon_q.
\]
Every witness has $U\ge\mathbb E Q$, so every branch rejects.

All guesses have a fixed polynomial bound in $q$ and hence are
$o(N)$; the initial guard enforces the stated limit at every length.
Evaluating a circuit with at most $q^G$ gates takes $q^{O(G)}$ steps,
so the check \eqref{eq:source-check} takes $Nq^{O(G)}\le N^{d_0}$ steps
for $d_0=2$ and all large $N$. This exponent depends on no PCP
construction. Constructing $C^O_{\langle B_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}$ and
$D_B$ takes $q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}=N^{o(1)}$ steps by \cref{imp:short-pcp}. Choose an integer
$C_{\rm clk}>d_0+2$. The polynomially many
pair calls and their deterministic multiword arithmetic overhead
take
\[
 2^q q^{O(1)-\mu_q}=o(\Tclk(N)).
\]
Polynomial-bit arithmetic is expanded into operations on
$O(\log T)$-bit words; no new nondeterministic bits are needed.
The refuter therefore applies to this one fixed weak machine. At
every sufficiently large source length it returns a hierarchy
yes-instance that the weak machine rejects.

\subsection{Extracting the weighted hard table}
\label{sec:gadget-extract}

Fix such a rejected yes-instance $z\in\{0,1\}^N$, with $N=2^\nu$. The
two cases below, the
SAT-checked canonical choices, and the clause-indexed proof table
follow Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite[Sections~6.2.4--6.2.5]{CLW20}.
Their proof of~\cite[Lemma~6.5]{CLW20} averages cofactors into
real-valued proofs; we instead select one
Boolean cofactor per name and weight the list positions. Call a general
circuit with $q$ inputs and at most $q^G$ gates a \emph{source circuit
for $z$} if it satisfies \eqref{eq:source-check}. Descriptions are
ordered lexicographically, and ``first'' refers to this order. The
canonical choices are made in three steps, which yield the three cases
named in \cref{sec:overview}.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item (\textsc{NoSource}) If $z$ has no source circuit, let $X_z:\{0,1\}^q\to\{0,1\}$ map
$u$ to $z_i$, where $\mathrm{bin}_\nu(i)$ is the first $\nu$ coordinates
of $u$. A general circuit of at most $q^G$ gates computing $X_z$ would
be a source circuit for $z$. Hence $X_z$ has general circuit complexity
greater than $q^G$.
\item Otherwise let $B^*_{\rm src}$ be the first source circuit for $z$.
Then $\mathrm{tt}_N(B^*_{\rm src})=z\in L_{\Tclk}$, so some witness of
length $T_G(L_\nu)$ makes $\mathcal V_G$ accept
$\langle B^*_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu$, and \cref{imp:short-pcp} provides an
oracle on $\ell_{\rm out}$ inputs that makes
$C^O_{\langle B^*_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}$ unsatisfiable, that is,
accepted on every random string. Let $O^*$ be the first such oracle,
viewed as a truth table, and let $\widehat O^*(av)=O^*(a)$ for
$a\in\{0,1\}^{\ell_{\rm out}}$ and $v\in\{0,1\}^{q-\ell_{\rm out}}$.
\item (\textsc{NoProof}) If no general circuit $B_{\rm pf}$ with $q$ inputs and at most
$q^G$ gates satisfies $O_{B_{\rm pf}}=O^*$, then $\widehat O^*$ has
general circuit complexity greater than $q^G$, since a circuit computing
it would satisfy that equation.
\end{enumerate}
Cases (\textsc{NoSource}) and (\textsc{NoProof}) are treated together. In each of them
we obtain a function on $q$ inputs with general circuit complexity
greater than $q^G$. By
\cref{imp:ae-amplification} and the choice of $G$, the
amplified function, on at most $a_Gq$ inputs, has agreement $1/2+o(1)$
against every target circuit considered here. This is eventually below
$1-\gamma/5+\epsilon_q$.

Otherwise, in case (\textsc{Tautology}), let $B^*_{\rm pf}$ be the first
general circuit with $q$ inputs and at most $q^G$ gates such that
$O_{B^*_{\rm pf}}=O^*$, and put
$B=(B^*_{\rm src},B^*_{\rm pf})$. Then $D_B$ is a tautology, because
$C^{O^*}_{\langle B^*_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}$ is unsatisfiable, and its
source circuit passes the check \eqref{eq:source-check}, so the weak
machine can guess $B$. This canonical choice suffices: the argument
below uses only that $B$ is a pair whose source circuit passes the
check, that $D_B$ is a tautology, and that the weak machine rejected $z$
on every branch, including those that guess $B$. The efficient honest proof from
\cref{imp:three-pcpp} defines Boolean coordinate functions $\Pi_X(x)$
that make every three-literal clause true. For each fresh helper, define its
honest function to be the AND of that clause's three honest literal
values. For gadget $a$, form the five-entry list of underlying
coordinate names
\[
 (X_{a,1},X_{a,2},X_{a,3},Y_a,Y_a).
\]
The first three entries are unsigned names: the clause's literal
signs remain in the verifier, not in the table definition. Fill
$P_q$ address positions by cycling through this list, and define
the target table to return the corresponding honest coordinate.
Its exact arity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-table-arity}
 q_0(q)=q+\log_2M+\log_2P_q=q+O(\log q).
\end{equation}

Suppose a target circuit $C$ of weight at most $b(q)$ approximates
this table with error $\delta$. For each auxiliary name choose,
among the addresses whose list entry is that name, one minimizing
the error of the cofactor
$C(x,\text{address})$ against that name's honest function. Use that
one cofactor at every occurrence of the name. These are literal
restrictions of $C$, with unchanged top weight. Systematics remain
exact. The chosen integer descriptions can be guessed by the weak
machine.

Let $t_q=\lfloor P_q/5\rfloor$. If a name has total stability weight
$I_X$ across the five-entry lists (one per literal occurrence, two
for a helper, as in \cref{lem:gadget-stability}), it appears in at
least $t_qI_X$
table positions. The minimum error is at most its occurrence-average
error. Write $e_X$ for the chosen cofactor's error and
$\mathrm{err}(a,j)$ for the error of $C$ at address $(a,j)$, so that
$\delta$ is the average of $\mathrm{err}$ over all $MP_q$ addresses.
Applying \cref{lem:gadget-stability} pointwise and summing over names,
\[
 \mathbb E Q\le\frac1M\sum_XI_Xe_X
 \le\frac1{Mt_q}\sum_{a,j}\mathrm{err}(a,j),
\]
which gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gadget-table-error}
 \mathbb E Q\le\frac{P_q}{\lfloor P_q/5\rfloor}\delta.
\end{equation}
This argument also covers repeated names within one clause and
different literal signs on the same name. Each fresh helper has one
common guessed value despite appearing twice in its five-entry list.

Since $P_q=128/\epsilon_q$ and $0<\epsilon_q<1$,
\[
 \frac{P_q}{\lfloor P_q/5\rfloor}\le5+\epsilon_q.
\]
If $\delta\le\gamma/5-\epsilon_q$, a useful counting witness yields
\begin{align*}
 U&\le(5+\epsilon_q)(\gamma/5-\epsilon_q)+10\omega_q\\
  &\le\gamma-4\epsilon_q+10\epsilon_q/128
   <\gamma-\epsilon_q.
\end{align*}
This would be an accepting branch on the rejected source input.
We conclude that the table has agreement strictly below
$1-\gamma/5+\epsilon_q$ at native weight $b(q)$.

\subsection{Every sufficiently large length and uniform recovery}
\label{sec:all-lengths}

Choose a fixed integer $K_{\rm amp}\ge a_G$ large enough
to accommodate the general amplifier, and pad the function in either
case to exactly $m(q)=K_{\rm amp}q_0(q)$ inputs, ignoring a suffix.
Use source lengths $N_\nu=2^\nu$. The native widths $q(N_\nu)$ have
bounded additive jumps. For a final length $n$, choose the largest $\nu$
with $m(q(N_\nu))\le n/2$. When none exists, set the slice to zero.

The boundary schedule can have jumps, so bounded additive gaps in
$m(q)$ should not be assumed. Instead, the next native width is
$q+O(1)$ and, by \cref{eq:gadget-table-arity}, its entire core has
length $K_{\rm amp}q+O(\log q)$. Maximality consequently gives
\[
 q\le n\le B_{\rm len}q
\]
eventually, for a fixed constant $B_{\rm len}$. Monotonicity of $\rho$
makes the core schedule monotone, so this selection is effective.
If a circuit approximates the padded function, averaging over the
ignored suffix gives a restriction with at least the same agreement
and no larger top weight.

For the fixed-error schedule, $b(q)=\lfloor\eta_0\epsilon
\mathcal A_3(q)\rfloor$ and
$\mathcal A_3(n)\le B_{\rm len}^3\mathcal A_3(q)$. Choosing
$K_d>2B_{\rm len}^3/(\eta_0\epsilon)$ handles the floor and the native cap.
Since $d<\gamma/5-\epsilon$, this proves
\cref{thm:main}.

For the boundary schedule, $\lambda_n\le2\lambda_q$ eventually,
and hence
\[
 \epsilon_q\le2^{-\rho(\lambda_n)},\qquad
 2^{-\rho(2\lambda_n)}\mathcal A_3(n)
 \le B_{\rm len}^3\,2^{-\rho(2\lambda_q)}\mathcal A_3(q).
\]
Choose $K>2B_{\rm len}^3/\eta_0$ to handle the cap and its floor. These
inequalities prove the claimed advantage bound. Moreover
$2^{\rho(2\lambda_n)}=n^{o(1)}$, so the same cap eventually
contains every fixed $An^{3-\alpha}$. The error bound tends
to $\gamma/5$, proving the common-language quantifiers in
\cref{thm:gadget-boundary}.

For completeness, all canonical choices are recoverable in $\ENP$.
The fixed-machine refuter runs in $\operatorname{poly}(\Tclk(N_\nu))
=2^{O(q)}$ time with SAT access. The choices of
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer} are recovered in three stages, by prefix
self-reduction on SAT formulas of size $2^{O(q)}$. First, a formula over
the description of $B_{\rm src}$ with the $N$ constraints
\eqref{eq:source-check}, of size $Nq^{O(G)}$, finds the first source
circuit $B^*_{\rm src}$, or shows that there is none, in which case the
table is $X_z$. Second, the algorithm of \cref{imp:short-pcp} is run
deterministically on $\langle B^*_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu$, in
$q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}$ time; a formula with one variable per oracle address and the
constraints $C^O_{\langle B^*_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}(R)=0$ for all
$2^{\ell_{\rm out}}$ inputs $R$, of size
$2^{2\ell_{\rm out}}q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}=2^{O(q)}$ (each oracle gate
becomes a selector over the $2^{\ell_{\rm out}}$ oracle variables),
finds the first oracle $O^*$. The algorithm of
\cref{thm:short-pcp-ours} is non-adaptive: its queries depend on the
random string alone (\cref{rem:short-pcp-nonadaptive}), so for it each oracle gate reads a fixed
variable once $R$ is fixed, and the size is also
$2^{\ell_{\rm out}}q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}$. Third, a formula over the description of
$B_{\rm pf}$ with the $2^{\ell_{\rm out}}$ constraints
$B_{\rm pf}(a0^{q-\ell_{\rm out}})=O^*(a)$, of size
$2^{\ell_{\rm out}}q^{O(G)}$, finds the first $B^*_{\rm pf}$, or shows
that there is none, in which case the table is $\widehat O^*$. No formula
contains the algorithm of \cref{imp:short-pcp}. Run the general amplifier
on $X_z$ or $\widehat O^*$. If no oracle $O^*$ exists at an exceptional
initial source length, the second stage detects that case and the slice
is set to zero. In the
small-oracle branch, the honest PCPP proof and each AND helper are
computable in polynomial time once the pair is known. Every new
schedule is computable in polynomial time in $q$. Since $q=O(n)$,
the final functions belong to $\ENP$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{cor:main-boundary}]
Take \(\rho(u)=\ceil{\lgx(u+2)}\) in
\cref{thm:gadget-boundary}. This is a polynomial-time computable,
nondecreasing, unbounded sublinear integer schedule. If \(K\)
is its cap constant, then
\(2^{\rho(2\lambda_n)}\le4\lambda_n+4\le8\lambda_n\).
Taking \(C_0\ge8K\) places the corollary's cap within that theorem's
cap, including its floor. Also
\(2^{-\rho(\lambda_n)}\le1/(\lambda_n+2)<1/\lambda_n\).
Use the theorem's fixed language as \(F\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{A limited optimality observation for symmetric gadgets}

Within the following quadratic representation, weight five is optimal
even if extra Boolean helpers are allowed. The statement is specific to
that representation.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:symmetric-gadget-weight}
Suppose a penalty of degree at most two in three input bits and any
number of Boolean helper bits is symmetric in the three input
bits, is nonnegative for every input and helper assignment, is at
least one on the all-zero input, and has an honest zero-penalty
helper for each nonzero input. Suppose a weighted Hamming stability
bound, relative to these honest helpers, assigns a common nonnegative
weight $w_x$ to each input bit and nonnegative helper weights of total
weight $H$ to the helpers. Then $3w_x+H\ge5$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Writing $k$ for the input Hamming weight and $h$ for the helper
assignment, degree two forces
\[
 Q(k,h)=a_0(h)+ka_1(h)+a_2\binom{k}{2},
\]
where $a_2$ is independent of $h$, since a monomial of degree at most
two cannot contain both an input pair and a helper bit. Let $h_1,h_3$ be the honest helpers of the stability bound
at weights one and three. Nonnegativity and the zero at weight one
give
$a_2=Q(0,h_1)+Q(2,h_1)\ge1$.
The zero at weight three then gives
$Q(0,h_3)=3a_2+3Q(2,h_3)\ge3$.
Compare an honest weight-one input first with the all-zero input
using helper $h_1$, then with the all-zero input using helper $h_3$.
Weighted stability yields $w_x\ge1$ and $w_x+H\ge3$.
Therefore $3w_x+H\ge2w_x+3\ge5$.
\end{proof}

This proposition assumes symmetry of the penalty in the original
input bits for every fixed helper assignment. No reduction from
arbitrary verifier representations to this symmetric form is claimed.


\section{Strong correlation bounds for periodic lookup tables}
\label{sec:per-overview}

The near-cubic counter has more room when it is asked about one
integer score. We use that room to handle several periodic lookup
tables on the same input. The reduction is an elementary Fourier
expansion, with two details that matter for the complexity bound.
First, a bounded real lookup can be expressed using Boolean level
sets with bounded total coefficient mass. Second, over a prime
period, a Fourier direction has a short integer representative
after multiplication by a nonzero field element. Neither step
assumes independence of the scores.

We say that a supplied lookup table has \emph{period} \(m\) if
its values at any two supplied integers congruent modulo \(m\)
agree. This condition is checked on its entire integer frame,
including entries not attained by the circuit. A circuit may have
several eligible periods. Restrictions of input coordinates and
output complementation preserve any chosen period. A constant
shift of the score only shifts the residue lookup.

Our first target is \cref{thm:periodic}, stated in the introduction.
For every fixed \(A,\delta>0\), its weight cap eventually exceeds
\(A n^{3-\delta}\). The quantitative refinements below trade some
of that size for a smaller advantage; all retain the same two period
conditions.

\paragraph{Periodic tops: trading size for advantage.}
Under the period conditions of \cref{thm:periodic}, with all parameters
fixed before the hard language is chosen:
\begin{itemize}
\item for every positive rational \(a\), advantage \(\lambda_n^{-a}\)
against top weight at most \(n^3/(C_a\lambda_n^{6+4a}\mu_n^{23/2})\)
(\cref{thm:per-log-powers}; the case \(a=1\) is \cref{thm:periodic});
\item advantage \(2^{-\rho(\lambda_n)}\) against weight \(n^{3-o(1)}\), for
regular, effectively computable sublinear schedules \(\rho\) such as
\(\rho(u)=\ceil{\sqrt u}\) (\cref{thm:per-regular});
\item for every rational \(0<\delta<3\), one language with advantage
\(n^{-\delta/4+o(1)}\) against weight \(An^{3-\delta}\), for every
fixed \(A>0\) (\cref{cor:per-rational-power}).
\end{itemize}
Each statement is almost everywhere and uses an \(\ENP\) language,
and one language covers all eligible periods within each statement.
No single language is asserted to realize all choices of
\(a,\rho,\delta\) simultaneously.


\begin{theorem}[Inverse-polynomial advantage with power slack]
\label{thm:per-power}
For every fixed \(0<\delta<3\) and \(0<\xi<\delta/4\), there is a
language \(F_{\delta,\xi}\in\ENP\) such that,
for every fixed \(A>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\), every
\(C\in\cW\) with \(W(C)\le A n^{3-\delta}\) and either period
condition in \cref{thm:per-endpoint} satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{agr}(C,F_{\delta,\xi})<\frac12+n^{-\xi}.
\]
The language is independent of \(A\), the chosen period, and the
candidate circuit.
\end{theorem}

\Cref{sec:per-power-proof} proves this theorem from the common transfer
of \cref{sec:per-transfer}.

The period conditions are restrictions on the original lookup table, not on the
primes used internally by the counter. They give strong correlation
bounds at a near-cubic weight, but do not cover arbitrary lookup
tables. We give the algorithm and the full transfer, including the
cost of the affine reconstruction.

\subsection{The scalar budget and polynomial precision}
\label{sec:per-scalar}

Recall from \cref{thm:pair-uniform} the scales
\[
 \mathcal A_1(q)=\frac{q^{7/2}}{\lambda_q^6\mu_q^8},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_3(q)=\frac{q^3}{\lambda_q^{21/4}\mu_q^7}.
\]
The second expression is the near-cubic scale already used for
pair counting. We record explicitly the scalar consequence of the
uniform counter, because the helper circuits below can have weight
larger than \(q^3\).

\begin{lemma}[Scalar interface]
\label{lem:per-scalar}
Fix polynomial bounds for parameter words, precision, and the
number of calls. There is a constant \(c_*>0\) such that the
following holds eventually. A scalar call of top weight
\(W\le c_*\beta \mathcal A_1(q)\), or its XOR with one explicitly given
raw input parity, has a sound probability interval for every
witness, and some polynomial-length witness gives radius at
most \(\beta\). Here \(\beta\) is a rational precision in a fixed
inverse-polynomial envelope. A pair call has the analogous cap
\(c_*\beta \mathcal A_3(q)\).

The source parser admits top weight at most \(2q^4\), normalized
integer bottom parameters of magnitude below \(2^{B_0}\), where
\(B_0=4q\ceil{\lgx(q+1)}\) as in \cref{sec:algorithm},
and complete finite lookup tables.
Any prescribed fixed polynomial family of these calls, including
all witness reads and arithmetic, fits \(2^q/q^{g(q)}\) time for
some \(g(q)\to\infty\). Finite guard failures return the sound
default interval.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is \cref{thm:pair-uniform} at input length \(q\), with
\(a_{B_1,B_2}\) renamed \(c_*\); we explain the scalar scale, the helper
lookups, and the prescribed call family. The normalized calls of \cref{sec:algorithm} already admit top
weight \(W\le2q^4\), hence at most \(G_0=2q^4\) bottom gates; this enters
\(\log(1+G''m_T)\) only through an additive \(O(\log q)\). Thus \cref{lem:print} still permits
\(D_{\max}=\Theta(q/\lambda_q)\); its proof already pays the
full signed output coefficients and polynomial-bit packed
threshold parameters. The larger lookup tables have polynomial
length and are included in the parser and the time bound.

Choose the fixed inner-coordinate reserve to pay the prescribed
call family. Uniformly over the admitted precisions, the existing
construction has
\[
 k,T=\Theta(\lambda_q\mu_q),\qquad
 P,h=\Theta\!\left(\frac q{\lambda_q\mu_q^2}\right).
\]
Indeed \(\log(1/\beta)=O(\lambda_q)\), so it changes neither
the order of \(T\) nor the prime budget. The one-dimensional
window of \cref{lem:one-window} has length
\(D'=\Theta(P^2/\lambda_q)\). The sufficient strict condition
from the sampled-chain proof is
\[
 W<\frac{\beta R_0h(D'-1)}{244Tk},
 \qquad R_0=\Theta(\sqrt q).
\]
Its right side is \(\Theta(\beta \mathcal A_1(q))\). The floor branch
\(\bar\ell=h^2/(256T)\) contributes only \(O(h)=o(D')\).
The two-dimensional calculation gives \(\mathcal A_3(q)\) in the same
way. Choose one smaller constant \(c_*\) for both inequalities.

The finite algorithm uses the actual degree, prime, chain and
strict-window guards, not their asymptotic constants. Its witness
still consists of a single coordinate set and coefficient vectors
indexed by the original gates; their total length is polynomial.
Even moments certify every proposed vector exactly as before.
Raw parity is handled by fixed inner parity subsets and the outer
phase, so it requires no new bottom gate. The fixed reserve absorbs
the increased polynomial factors in source construction, word
arithmetic, and the total number of calls.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A rational Fourier reduction to scalar calls}
\label{sec:per-fourier}

Fourier inversion and Parseval on finite abelian groups are
classical; see, for example, Terras~\cite[Chapter~10]{Terras99}.
In~\cite[Appendix~E.2]{NagSeven}, finite Fourier expansion is used
only in the error analysis; here it is implemented through verified
rational scalar calls.
We include the reduction because its coefficient mass and its
rational implementation determine the allowable period.

\begin{lemma}[Verified rational scalar representation]
\label{lem:per-fourier}
Let \(m\ge2\), let
\(h:(\Z/m\Z)^4\to\{0,1\}\) be an explicit table, and let
\(0<\eta\le1\) be rational. One can deterministically construct
directions \(v_j\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}^4\), Boolean tables
\(b_j:\Z/m\Z\to\{0,1\}\), and rational coefficients
\(\theta_j\) such that
\begin{align}
 \left|h(u)-\sum_j\theta_jb_j(v_j\cdot u\bmod m)\right|
 &\le\eta\quad\text{for every }u,                \label{eq:per-approx}\\
 \#\{j\}&\le2m^5,\qquad
 \sum_j|\theta_j|\le8m^2.                        \label{eq:per-mass}
\end{align}
The construction and a full exact verification of
\eqref{eq:per-approx} take polynomial time and polynomial bit
length in \(m\) and the bit length of \(\eta\).
\end{lemma}

\emph{Idea of proof.} Finite Fourier inversion writes \(h\) as a
combination of characters. Parseval and Cauchy--Schwarz bound its
coefficient mass by \(m^2\). Rational approximations of the
coefficients and of the cosine and sine tables give a rational expansion
whose mass is still of order \(m^2\). Each real lookup table in it is
then split into Boolean level sets. A final exact check on all \(m^4\)
residues certifies the result.

\begin{proof}
Put \(N=m^4\) and
\[
 \widehat h(v)=m^{-4}\sum_u h(u)e^{-2\pi i v\cdot u/m}.
\]
Character orthogonality gives
\[
 h(u)=\sum_v\widehat h(v)e^{2\pi i v\cdot u/m},\qquad
 \sum_v|\widehat h(v)|^2=m^{-4}\sum_u h(u)^2\le1.
\]
Consequently \(\sum_v|\widehat h(v)|\le m^2\) by
Cauchy--Schwarz. This uniform average is a calculation of the
coefficients; it makes no assumption about the eventual score law.

Choose the largest power of two \(d\) with
\(d\le\min\{1/4,\eta/(16N)\}\). Construct rational tables
\(c(t),s(t)\) approximating \(\cos(2\pi t/m)\) and
\(\sin(2\pi t/m)\) within \(d\), and rational \(a_v,a'_v\)
approximating \(\operatorname{Re}\widehat h(v),\operatorname{Im}\widehat h(v)\) within
\(d\). Computing the finite Fourier sums with the approximate
trigonometric tables gives this accuracy. These approximations
require only polynomial-time rational arithmetic. For an explicit
implementation, approximate \(\pi\) by Machin's arctangent
formula and use the sine and cosine Taylor series on a bounded
interval. Their remainder bounds determine a polynomial number
of terms and polynomial word lengths.

Define
\[
 \widetilde h(u)=\sum_v\bigl(a_vc(v\cdot u)-a'_vs(v\cdot u)\bigr).
\]
Since \(|\widehat h(v)|\le1\) and \(|c|,|s|\le1+d\), each summand
differs from its exact real Fourier contribution
by at most \(4d+2d^2\le5d\). Hence
\(\max_u|\widetilde h(u)-h(u)|\le5Nd<\eta\). Moreover,
\[
 \sum_v(|a_v|+|a'_v|)
 \le\sqrt2\sum_v|\widehat h(v)|+2Nd
 \le\sqrt2m^2+2Nd.
\]

It remains to replace the real scalar lookups by Boolean ones.
For a rational table \(g\) valued in \([-R,R]\), list its
distinct positive values \(0<t_1<\cdots<t_a\), set \(t_0=0\),
and write
\[
 g_+(x)=\sum_{j=1}^a(t_j-t_{j-1})\ind[g(x)\ge t_j].
\]
Apply the same identity to \((-g)_+\) and subtract. This uses
at most \(m\) Boolean tables in total and has coefficient mass
\(\max g_++\max(-g)_+\le2R\). Apply it to each \(c,s\), with
\(R=1+d\), and multiply by \(a_v,-a'_v\). The resulting mass is
at most
\[
 2(1+d)(\sqrt2m^2+2Nd)\le8m^2,
\]
and there are at most \(2m^5\) terms.

Finally verify \eqref{eq:per-approx} by evaluating the rational
representation on all \(m^4\) residues, and check its actual
coefficient mass. All these are exact rational comparisons. A
direct construction uses \(O(m^8)\) coefficient operations and
\(O(m^9)\) operations for this final check; such crude polynomial
bounds suffice. A failed check produces no certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Periodic products from scalar counting]
\label{prop:per-supplier}
Let at most four original class outputs on the same \(q\)-bit
input have a common period \(m\), and let their total top weight
be \(W_{\rm tot}\). Any Boolean predicate of these outputs has
a probability interval that is sound for every concatenated native
witness. For a requested radius \(\omega\) in a fixed
inverse-polynomial envelope, some witness gives radius at most
\(\omega\), eventually, whenever
\begin{equation}
 m^3W_{\rm tot}\le c_{\rm tar}\omega \mathcal A_1(q)                 \label{eq:per-tariff}
\end{equation}
for a sufficiently small fixed \(c_{\rm tar}>0\). The construction uses
polynomially many scalar calls and polynomial total witness length when
\(m=\poly(q)\), and fits the saved time bound of
\cref{lem:per-scalar} with a fixed larger reserve.
\end{proposition}
Its proof works with any \(c_{\rm tar}\le c_*/64\).
\begin{proof}
Verify each original period against the complete supplied lookup
frame. Extend to all residues by assigning zero to any residue
with no representative in that frame. This is consistent, and
every attained score has a representative. Form the Boolean table
\(h\) of the requested predicate on the four residue coordinates;
pad missing coordinates by constants.

Write the actual scores as
\(X_i=c_i+\sum_a w_{ia}g_{ia}\). For a direction \(v_j\), the
event \(b_j(v_j\cdot X\bmod m)=1\) is one genuine scalar circuit
with top score
\[
 \sum_{i,a}(v_j)_i w_{ia}g_{ia}.
\]
The constant offset is incorporated in its lookup. Its actual
weight is at most \((m-1)W_{\rm tot}\). The bottom gates are the
original thresholds, not the compound class outputs.

Choose \(\eta\le \omega/2\) and a dyadic \(\beta\) with
\[
 \frac{\omega}{64m^2}<\beta\le\frac{\omega}{32m^2}.
\]
Use \cref{lem:per-fourier} and make a native scalar call for each
Boolean term. Exact signed interval arithmetic, followed by an
enlargement by \(\eta\) in each direction and intersection with
\([0,1]\), is sound by the pointwise inequality
\eqref{eq:per-approx}. If every scalar radius is at most
\(\beta\), the final radius is at most
\[
 \eta+\beta\sum_j|\theta_j|
 \le \omega/2+8m^2\beta\le3\omega/4.
\]
Condition \eqref{eq:per-tariff}, with \(c_{\rm tar}\le c_*/64\), makes
all helper weights fit their native scalar caps.

All input laws here are the original laws on the same \(x\).
No independence is used. Distinct helpers may have different
useful coordinate sets and sampling witnesses: there are at most
\(2m^5\) calls, so all their polynomial-length prefixes can be
concatenated. Reading the full helper tables, the \(O(m^9)\)
coefficient verification, exact interval operations, and every
native call are within one fixed polynomial envelope. The fixed
reserve pays that envelope. Period one is evaluated exactly.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Short directions for prime periods}
\label{sec:per-prime}

\begin{lemma}[A short representative of a Fourier direction]
\label{lem:per-short-direction}
If \(p\) is prime and \(v\in(\Z/p\Z)^4\) is nonzero, there
are a nonzero \(c\bmod p\) and \(a\in\Z^4\) with
\[
 a\equiv cv\pmod p,\qquad \|a\|_\infty\le4p^{3/4}.
\]
They can be found in time polynomial in \(p\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set \(N_b=\floor{(p-1)^{1/4}}\ge1\). Divide each coordinate
interval \([0,p)\) into \(N_b\) half-open intervals of equal length.
Assign an integer coordinate \(x\) to bin \(\floor{N_bx/p}\).
There are \(N_b^4<p\) boxes but \(p\) points \(tv\bmod p\), for
\(0\le t<p\), which are distinct because some \(v_i\) is invertible
modulo \(p\). Two lie in the same box.
Subtract their standard integer representatives to obtain \(a\),
and their indices modulo \(p\) to obtain \(c\ne0\). Then
\(|a_i|<p/N_b\le2^{5/4}p^{3/4}\le4p^{3/4}\), since
\(N_b\ge(p-1)^{1/4}/2\ge(p/2)^{1/4}/2\).
Enumerating and sorting the bin labels finds the pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Prime-period counting reduction]
\label{cor:per-prime-supplier}
For a prime common period \(p\), the sufficient condition in
\cref{prop:per-supplier} improves to
\begin{equation}
 p^{11/4}W_{\rm tot}\le c_1\omega \mathcal A_1(q)             \label{eq:per-prime-tariff}
\end{equation}
for a sufficiently small fixed \(c_1>0\). The soundness,
precision, polynomial witness length and saved-time conclusions are
unchanged.
\end{corollary}
Its proof works with any \(c_1\le c_{\rm tar}/4\).
\begin{proof}
For each nonzero Fourier direction replace its scalar lookup by
\[
 b(v\cdot X\bmod p)=b(c^{-1}a\cdot X\bmod p),
\]
using \cref{lem:per-short-direction}. The inverse \(c^{-1}\)
is placed in the lookup table. The new signed integer score has
weight at most \(4p^{3/4}W_{\rm tot}\); the coefficient mass
remains at most \(8p^2\). Repeat the preceding proof, absorbing
the constant four in \(c_1\). Zero directions are constant.
Finding the directions for all frequencies is polynomial in \(p\).
\end{proof}

The primality requirement has a specific role. A collision
multiplier for a composite modulus need not be invertible. For
example, if \(m=2^e\) and \(v=(1,m/2,0,0)\), every unit
multiple has second coordinate of least-residue magnitude \(m/2\).
We do not use the prime estimate for such a modulus.

\section{From periodic counting to vanishing correlation}
\label{sec:per-transfer}

We now use the periodic counting reduction in the pointwise PCPP framework of
Chen--Williams and Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite{CW19,CLW20}.
The published complexity-theoretic inputs are exactly
\cref{imp:short-pcp,imp:pcpp,imp:ae-refuter,imp:ae-amplification}.
The additional issue is that XOR reconstruction gives real affine
fields, whereas PCPP soundness concerns Boolean assignments. A
quartic validity statistic, the test \(S^2(1-S)^2\) of
Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite[Section~6.2.2]{CLW20}, resolves that issue
without making any
assumption about a dishonest guess.

\subsection{From a weak XOR predictor to an affine approximation}
\label{sec:per-xor}

The following is the quantitative contrapositive construction in
Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite[Appendix~A]{CLW20}; \cref{app:xor} proves the
version used here, including its finite list and coefficient bounds.
Their construction already bounds the coefficient mass by
\(O(1/e_t)\); we record this bound and the cofactor structure
explicitly.

\begin{lemma}[An actual affine cofactor list]
\label{lem:per-xor}
Fix rational \(0<\zeta<1/2\), and set
\(e_t=(1-\zeta)^{t-1}(1/2-\zeta)\). If a class circuit agrees
with \(f^{\oplus t}(y_1,\ldots,y_t)=f(y_1)\oplus\cdots\oplus f(y_t)\),
where \(f\) has \(q_0\) inputs, on at
least \(1/2+e_t\) of its inputs, then there is a rational field
\[
 \mathcal L(y)=c_0+\sum_{r=1}^J\alpha_r C_r(y)
\]
with \(0\le\mathcal L\le1\), \(\E|\mathcal L-f|\le\zeta\),
\[
 J=O_\zeta((q_0+1)e_t^{-2}),\qquad
 |c_0|+\sum_r|\alpha_r|=O_\zeta(e_t^{-1}).
\]
Each \(C_r\) is an actual restriction or output complement of
the original circuit. In particular its top weight does not
increase and its declared period is preserved. The rational
words have \(O_\zeta(\log(q_0+2)+\operatorname{bits}(e_t))\)
bits.
\end{lemma}

The pointwise range in this lemma is a fact about its honest
construction. The weak machine will not assume that an arbitrary
guessed list has this range, or that its circuits all come from
one original predictor.

\subsection{Why guessed real values need a validity test}
\label{sec:per-validity}

After the PCPP transformation, simplify a clause whose two
positions have the same name: identical literals give a unary
clause and opposite literals give the constant one. Keep its
indexed position in the clause distribution, using exact dummy
bits to retain two positions. Let \(M\) be the indexed clause
count. Use one real field \(S_X(x)\) for each auxiliary name,
with that same value at every occurrence. Systematic fields are
the exact raw parities in \(\Enc(x)\), and dummy fields are exact.
For an auxiliary name occurring \(d_X\) times, put
\(p_X=d_X/(2M)\); discard names with zero incidence. Define
\[
 \psi(u)=u^2(1-u)^2,\qquad
 V=\E_x\sum_Xp_X\psi(S_X(x)).
\]
Let \(\operatorname{val}(S)\) denote the mean multilinear clause value, with
negations evaluated as \(1-S_X\). On a dishonest real field this
need not be a probability. The following rounding argument is an
aggregate form of the validity analysis of
Chen--Lyu--Williams~\cite[Lemma~6.3]{CLW20}; their separate
second-moment test is replaced by the bound
\(u^2,(1-u)^2\le2+8\psi(u)\).

\begin{lemma}[Rounding with one aggregate certificate]
\label{lem:per-rounding}
For every real collection of fields, if \(V\le1\), then
\[
 \operatorname{val}(S)\le s+\Pr[D_B(x)=1]+15\sqrt V,
\]
where \(D_B\) is the circuit to which the PCPP was applied: the outer
verifier for a guessed source circuit with a guessed proof-oracle circuit
substituted, \(B=(B_{\rm src},B_{\rm pf})\) (\cref{sec:per-machine}).
If \(D_B\) is a tautology, \(0\le S_X\le1\), and
\(\E_x\sum_Xp_X|S_X(x)-\Pi_X(x)|\le\zeta\) for an honest Boolean
proof \(\Pi\), which satisfies at least a \(c\) fraction of the
clauses at every \(x\), then
\(V\le\zeta\) and \(\operatorname{val}(S)\ge c-2\zeta\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Round each real value \(u\) to its nearest bit \(b\), with one
fixed tie rule. Directly,
\[
 |u-b|^2\le4\psi(u),\qquad
 u^2,(1-u)^2\le2+8\psi(u).
\]
The first inequality uses that the distance to the farther bit
is at least \(1/2\); the second follows by writing
\(u=b+(u-b)\). In either clause position the averaged \(\psi\)
is at most \(2V\). Thus the squared rounding error has mean at
most \(8V\), and the unrounded literal has second moment at
most \(2+16V\).

For OR, telescope
\[
 (a+b-ab)-(a'+b'-a'b')
 =(a-a')(1-b)+(b-b')(1-a').
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the average absolute change by
\(\sqrt{8V}(\sqrt{2+16V}+1)\le15\sqrt V\) for \(V\le1\).
The same calculation applies to negated literals; exact and
unary terms only reduce it. The rounded fields form one consistent
Boolean assignment at each \(x\), so pointwise PCPP soundness
gives \(\operatorname{val}(\widehat S)\le s+\Pr[D_B=1]\), where \(\widehat S\) denotes
the rounded fields. This also proves the claim at
\(V=0\).

For the honest case, \(\psi(u)\le|u-b|\) on \([0,1]\) for either
Boolean target \(b\). Multilinear OR is one-Lipschitz in each
coordinate on \([0,1]^2\). Sum the errors at the two clause positions under the
true incidence distribution to obtain the remaining assertions.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A verifier that checks every guess}
\label{sec:per-machine}

\emph{Idea of the common transfer.} This subsection and the next two
prove the common transfer, which
\cref{sec:per-log-powers,sec:per-regular,sec:per-power-proof}
specialize. The weak machine has the same outer step as in
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer} (\cref{fig:outer-step}). It then applies the
two-query PCPP and guesses an addressed affine list of class circuits
for the proof table. It accepts only if a certified upper estimate of
the validity statistic \(V\) is small and a certified lower estimate of
the clause value is large.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Soundness.} By the rounding lemma of
\cref{sec:per-validity}, real fields with small \(V\) have clause value
close to that of a Boolean assignment. So PCPP soundness makes every
branch reject a hierarchy no-instance.
\item \emph{Completeness.} At a canonical pair that makes the outer
verifier a tautology, a good XOR predictor gives the affine list of
\cref{sec:per-xor}. That list passes both tests once the accuracy
schedule of \cref{sec:per-schedule} keeps the interval losses within
fixed reserves.
\end{itemize}
\Cref{sec:per-canonical} then bounds the running time, applies the
refuter, and recovers a hard function at every length.

Choose fixed rational constants \(s<\bar s<\bar c<c\), put
\(\Gamma=\bar c-\bar s\), and fix
\[
 \zeta=\Gamma^2/10^6,\qquad
 v_*=(\Gamma/200)^2,\qquad \tau_*=\bar s+\Gamma/2.
\]
Then \(\zeta<v_*/4\) and \(15\sqrt{v_*}=3\Gamma/40\).

We describe one machine template and one common schedule;
\cref{sec:per-log-powers,sec:per-regular,sec:per-power-proof}
specialize it. Fix a general-circuit exponent \(G\). For the
hierarchy clock \(\Tclk(N)=N^{C_{\rm clk}}\), use the exact source
width
\[
 q(N)=\ceil{\lgx \Tclk(N)}+
 A_{\rm pad}\ceil{\lgx(\lgx \Tclk(N)+2)}+A_{\rm pad}.
\]
As in the preceding transfer, \(A_{\rm pad}\) is chosen after
\(C_{\rm clk}\) so that the width \(\ell_{\rm out}\) of the short PCP
applied to the verifier \(\mathcal V_G\) of \cref{lem:succinct-verifier}
is at most \(q\) (\cref{app:multitape}). The weak machine is a multitape machine. Thus \(q=\Theta(\log N)\) and
\(2^q/\Tclk(N)=q^{O(1)}\).

On a hierarchy input \(z\) of length \(N\), reject unless \(N=2^\nu\) is
a power of two. Guess a source
circuit \(B_{\rm src}\) with \(q\) inputs and at most \(q^G\) gates,
reject unless its description has at most \(L_\nu\) bits (as in
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer}, this fails at only finitely many lengths),
check it at every position of \(z\) as in \eqref{eq:source-check}, and
reject if the check fails. Then construct
\(C^O_{\langle B_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}\) by \cref{imp:short-pcp},
guess a proof-oracle circuit \(B_{\rm pf}\) with \(q\) inputs and at
most \(q^G\) gates, answering oracle queries through
\(O_{B_{\rm pf}}\), and form the outer verifier \(D_B\) on \(q\)
inputs exactly as in \cref{sec:gadget-transfer}, with
\(B=(B_{\rm src},B_{\rm pf})\). It has size \(q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}\), the exponent depending on \(C_{\rm clk}\) through \(\mathcal V_G\) as there. Apply the pointwise PCPP, padding \(D_B\)
to size at least \(q\) if needed. Choose a fixed exponent \(d_G\)
bounding every possible clause count, and repeat the \emph{whole}
power-of-two list to exactly
\[
 M=2^{\ceil{d_G\lgx(q+2)}},\qquad q_0=q+\lgx M+1.
\]
The old length divides \(M\). Whole-list repetition preserves
clause and incidence laws; unused address padding would not.

The machine guesses an addressed affine list
\[
 \mathcal L(x,i,j)=c_0+\sum_{r=1}^J\alpha_r C_r(x,i,j),
 \qquad
 \Lambda=|c_0|+\sum_r|\alpha_r|,
\]
with \(J\le q^4\), mass \(\Lambda\le\Lambda_0(q)\), top weight at most
\(w(q)\) for every atom, and fixed polynomial word and description
bounds; the mass bound \(\Lambda_0(q)\) and the weight cap \(w(q)\) are
fixed by the schedule of \cref{sec:per-schedule}
(\eqref{eq:per-source-cap}). It guesses a common period and a flag selecting
the ordinary-period or prime-period guard. The period is checked
on every full original frame; the prime flag additionally requires
a deterministic primality check. A guessed list is not assumed to
come from one original predictor or to produce values in \([0,1]\).

For every auxiliary name set
\[
 S_X(x)=\frac1{d_X}\sum_{(i,j):X(i,j)=X}\mathcal L(x,i,j).
\]
This is one field per name. Each term after taking an actual
address cofactor remains an original class circuit with the same
weight and period. Convex occurrence averaging does not increase
\(\Lambda\).

Check every constructed cofactor against the native bottom-word
bound before calling the counter. Honest list descriptions can
meet this guard: normalize their bottom thresholds on \(q_0\)
inputs using \cref{lem:integer}. Its bound
\(q_0^{q_0}\), together with
\(q_0=q+O(\log q)\), leaves more than enough room under
\(2^{4q\ceil{\lgx(q+1)}}\) after fixing the address coordinates.
All scalar helpers keep these bottom gates unchanged.

Expand \(V\) into same-input products of at most four cofactor
outputs. Its total absolute coefficient mass is at most
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda^2+2\Lambda^3+\Lambda^4.                 \label{eq:per-validity-mass}
\end{equation}
There is no factor \(M\) or \(d_X\): the name incidences and
within-name coefficients are normalized. These quantities affect
the number of terms, which is polynomial, but not the displayed
mass. Quadratic auxiliary clause terms have mass
\(O((1+\Lambda)^2)\); mixed auxiliary/systematic terms have
mass \(O(1+\Lambda)\). For a Boolean cofactor \(C\) and a raw
parity \(\chi\), use
\[
 \E[C\chi]=\frac{\E C+\E\chi-\E[C\oplus\chi]}2
\]
to obtain the mixed product from the scalar/parity interface.
Products of exact parities are computed by their supports and
phases over \(\mathbb F_2\).

Use the appropriate periodic counting reduction
(\cref{prop:per-supplier} or \cref{cor:per-prime-supplier}) for each
auxiliary product
and exact outward arithmetic throughout. This gives
\(L_V\le V\le U_V\) and \(L_{\rm val}\le\operatorname{val}(S)\) for every legal
guess and every native witness. Accept only if
\begin{equation}
 U_V\le v_*,\qquad L_{\rm val}\ge\tau_*.              \label{eq:per-tests}
\end{equation}

On a hierarchy NO input, every guessed pair whose source circuit
passes the check has \(\Pr[D_B=1]\le1/L_\nu\), by
\cref{imp:short-pcp} as in \cref{sec:gadget-transfer}, and
\(1/L_\nu\le\Gamma/8\) eventually. If a branch accepted, then
\(V\le U_V\le v_*<1\), and
\cref{lem:per-rounding} would imply
\[
 L_{\rm val}\le s+\Gamma/8+15\sqrt{v_*}
 =s+\Gamma/5<\tau_*
\]
eventually, a contradiction. This soundness statement covers
unbounded real fields, inconsistent sampling proposals, and the
zero-validity endpoint. Malformed descriptions and failed resource
guards cannot produce an accepting branch.

For honest completeness, suppose the guessed pair passes the check
and makes
\(D_B\) a tautology. Let \(\Pi_X(x)\) be its canonical efficiently
computed honest PCPP assignment and let
\(f(x,i,j)=\Pi_{X(i,j)}(x)\), including exact systematic and dummy
positions. A predictor for \(f^{\oplus t}\) at advantage \(e_t\)
gives the actual list of \cref{lem:per-xor}. Convex occurrence
averaging gives
\[
 \E_x\sum_Xp_X|S_X(x)-\Pi_X(x)|\le\zeta.
\]
Consequently \(V\le\zeta\) and \(\operatorname{val}(S)\ge c-2\zeta\).
If the schedule below makes the total honest interval errors
smaller than the fixed completeness reserves, some concatenated native witness satisfies
\eqref{eq:per-tests}. This is the only completeness premise
needed from the XOR construction.

\subsection{Choosing accuracy without overspending the gap}
\label{sec:per-schedule}

It is enough to make the total interval loss a small fixed
constant. Requiring it to tend to zero would unnecessarily reduce
the weight cap. The following common schedule will be used in
all the applications.

Let \(H(q)\) be a
nondecreasing, unbounded integer function,
computable in \(q^{O(1)}\) time, with \(H(q)\le\lambda_q\)
eventually. Suppose its change between consecutive native source
widths is bounded by a fixed constant. Put
\[
 R(q)=2^{H(q)},\qquad
 t(q)=\min\{t\ge1:e_t\le1/(8R(q))\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1-\zeta}{8R(q)}<e_{t(q)}\le\frac1{8R(q)},
 \qquad t(q)=\Theta(1+H(q)).                    \label{eq:per-schedule-bias}
\end{equation}
Choose one fixed integer \(C_\Lambda\) large enough that the
honest list in \cref{lem:per-xor} has
\(\Lambda\le\Lambda_0(q):=C_\Lambda R(q)\). For example,
\(C_\Lambda=6\) suffices: the proof in \cref{app:xor} gives
\(\Lambda\le3/(2\varrho)\), where \(\varrho=e_t(2+\zeta)/(1-\zeta)\),
and \(e_{t(q)}>(1-\zeta)/(8R(q))\) gives
\(3/(2\varrho)<12R(q)/(2+\zeta)<6R(q)\).
The honest list has \(O(qR(q)^2)<q^4\) entries eventually.
All these bounds become finite parser guards. Check the bound on
\(H(q)\) before expanding \(R(q)\); failed public guards cause
immediate rejection.

Set
\begin{equation}
 c_{\rm gap}=v_*/64,\qquad
 \omega(q)=\frac{c_{\rm gap}}{(1+\Lambda_0(q))^4}.   \label{eq:per-schedule-radius}
\end{equation}
Request this radius for each periodic product and for the scalar
or scalar/parity terms used in clauses. For every useful family
of native witnesses, signed interval endpoints lose at most twice
the sum of coefficient magnitudes times their radii. Thus
\eqref{eq:per-validity-mass} gives
\[
 U_V\le V+2c_{\rm gap}.
\]
Each literal has affine coefficient mass at most \(1+\Lambda\).
The OR expression \(a+b-ab\) consequently has mass at most
\(2(1+\Lambda)+(1+\Lambda)^2\le3(1+\Lambda)^2\).
The normalized clause average has the same bound, so
\[
 L_{\rm val}\ge\operatorname{val}(S)-6c_{\rm gap}.
\]
The identity for a mixed raw-parity product preserves radius up
to a fixed constant, which is paid by decreasing its requested
native radius if needed. Constants and pure parity terms are exact.
On the honest list this yields the strict margins
\begin{equation}
 U_V\le\zeta+2c_{\rm gap}<9v_*/32<v_*,
 \qquad
 L_{\rm val}\ge c-2\zeta-6c_{\rm gap}
          >c-19v_*/32>\tau_*.                 \label{eq:per-fixed-margins}
\end{equation}
The last inequality follows from \(c-\tau_*>\Gamma/2\) and
\(v_*=\Gamma^2/40000\). Hence fixed reserves suffice.

Choose a sufficiently small fixed rational \(\eta_0>0\) and
give each atom the weight cap
\begin{equation}
 w(q)=\floor{\eta_0
       \frac{q^3}{\lambda_q^6\mu_q^8\sqrt{t(q)}\,R(q)^4}}.
                                                               \label{eq:per-source-cap}
\end{equation}
The defining floor is computable by integer comparisons after
squaring. The final padding below has \(n\le B_{\rm len}qt(q)\) for a
fixed \(B_{\rm len}\). Choose fixed integers \(K_m,K_p\) with
\(K_m^6\ge B_{\rm len}\), \(K_p^{11}\ge B_{\rm len}^2\), and admit either
\begin{equation}
 m^6\le K_m^6qt(q),
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad
 p\text{ prime and }p^{11}\le K_p^{11}q^2t(q)^2.
                                                               \label{eq:per-period-guards}
\end{equation}
These guards are polynomial-size exact integer comparisons.

A product involves at most four atoms, so \(W_{\rm tot}\le4w(q)\).
For the ordinary-period branch,
\[
 m^3W_{\rm tot}
 \le4K_m^3\eta_0\frac{q^{7/2}}
                    {\lambda_q^6\mu_q^8 R(q)^4}.
\]
For the prime branch replace \(m^3\) by \(p^{11/4}\) and
\(K_m^3\) by \(K_p^{11/4}\). On the other hand,
\[
 \omega(q)\mathcal A_1(q)\ge
 \frac{c_{\rm gap}}{(1+C_\Lambda)^4}
 \frac{q^{7/2}}{\lambda_q^6\mu_q^8R(q)^4}.
\]
Choose the same \(\eta_0\) sufficiently small for both
\eqref{eq:per-tariff} and \eqref{eq:per-prime-tariff}.
Every periodic product then has useful radius \(\omega(q)\).
The requested native scalar precisions are
\(\Theta(\omega(q)/m^2)\) or \(\Theta(\omega(q)/p^2)\).
Since \(R\le2q\), \(t=O(\lambda_q)\), and both periods
are \(q^{O(1)}\), these lie in one fixed inverse-polynomial
envelope; for example a lower bound \(q^{-6}\) suffices
eventually. Every helper has top weight below \(2q^4\).
Scalar/raw-parity calls on the original atoms satisfy their
scalar caps with this same requested radius and ample slack.

This proves honest completeness \eqref{eq:per-fixed-margins}
for any schedule satisfying the stated conditions. The
soundness argument in \cref{sec:per-machine} did not use those
conditions or any honesty assumption about the guessed fields.

\subsection{Running time and a hard function at every length}
\label{sec:per-canonical}

There are polynomially many names and occurrences, at most
\(q^4\) guessed atoms, and tuples of at most four atoms in the
validity expansion. Every tuple uses at most twice the fifth
power of its period in scalar calls, and polynomial work for
the full frames, rational coefficients, short prime directions
and exact pointwise verification. The \emph{sum} of the guessed
descriptions of \(B_{\rm src}\) and \(B_{\rm pf}\), the period word and flag, all native
witness prefixes, the list description and all rational words is
therefore bounded by one explicit fixed polynomial in \(q\).
Compute an upper envelope for this total before making any guess
and reject if it exceeds \(\floor{N/16}\). Since
\(q=\Theta(\log N)\), this guard is eventually inactive.

Choose the fixed inner-coordinate reserve of \cref{lem:per-scalar}
large enough to pay the full call count and arithmetic overhead.
Every call and the whole machine halt within their stated bounds on
every witness, so the total work is
\[
 2^q q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}/q^{g(q)}=o(\Tclk(N)).
\]
The check \eqref{eq:source-check} takes
\(Nq^{O(G)}\le N^{d_{\rm ver}}\) steps for \(d_{\rm ver}=2\) and all
large \(N\), and constructing the outer verifier takes
\(q^{O_{G,C_{\rm clk}}(1)}=N^{o(1)}\) steps; \(d_{\rm ver}\) depends neither on
\(C_{\rm clk}\) nor on a PCP construction. Choose an integer \(C_{\rm clk}>d_{\rm ver}+2\). All reserves and machine
constants are fixed before applying the refuter. The weak machine
has no NP oracle and guesses no computation trace.

Apply \cref{imp:ae-refuter} to this fixed machine. At every
sufficiently large \(N\), its disagreement \(z_N\) must be a
hierarchy YES input that the weak machine rejects, since every
NO branch rejects. An external SAT-oracle computation makes the
canonical choices of \cref{sec:gadget-transfer}, with the same three
stages of SAT formulas of size \(2^{O(q)}\) as in
\cref{sec:all-lengths}: the first source circuit \(B^*_{\rm src}\) for
\(z_N\), the first oracle \(O^*\) making
\(C^O_{\langle B^*_{\rm src}\rangle_\nu}\) unsatisfiable, and the first
circuit \(B^*_{\rm pf}\) with \(q\) inputs and at most \(q^G\) gates
such that \(O_{B^*_{\rm pf}}=O^*\).

If \(B^*_{\rm pf}\) exists, the pair \(B=(B^*_{\rm src},B^*_{\rm pf})\)
passes the check and makes \(D_B\) a tautology. Its canonical
honest addressed PCPP table \(f\) is fixed before considering
any target approximator. Rejection and honest completeness imply
that \(f^{\oplus t(q)}\) has agreement below
\(1/2+e_{t(q)}\) with every source predictor admitted by either
period branch.

Otherwise we are in case (\textsc{NoSource}) or (\textsc{NoProof}) of
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer}: either \(z_N\) has no source circuit, and the
\(q\)-input function \(X_{z_N}\) has no general circuit of size \(q^G\),
or \(B^*_{\rm pf}\) does not exist, and the \(q\)-input function
\(\widehat O^*\) has no general circuit of size \(q^G\). In
either subcase, apply \cref{imp:ae-amplification}, due to
Sudan--Trevisan--Vadhan and packaged by Chen--Lyu--Williams
\cite{STV01,CLW20}, to obtain the general-circuit hard core.

Choose \(G\) initially large enough for this case. There is one fixed
\(h'_{\rm sim}\), the analogue of \(h_{\rm sim}\) in \cref{sec:gadget-machine},
such that, for every fixed \(A\ge1\) and all large \(q\),
every target of top weight at most \(n^3\) on \(n\le Aq\log q\) inputs
has a general Boolean simulation of size \(q^{h'_{\rm sim}}\): normalize the
bottom thresholds, use binary arithmetic, and look up the polynomial-size
integer table. Choose
\(G/b_{\rm amp}>\max\{h'_{\rm sim},2\}+2\). Since \(R\le2q\),
the general amplifier's bias is eventually smaller than
\(e_{t(q)}\). This choice is independent of any candidate or
period; a larger fixed \(G\) can be used for any changed fixed
word envelope.

Pad either core to
\[
 \ell_N=K_{\rm pad}t(q)q_0,
\]
with fixed \(K_{\rm pad}>a_G+1\). The XOR core uses its
\(t(q)\) addressed blocks; the general-hard core ignores the
remaining coordinates. All canonical recovery takes
\(2^{O(q)}\) SAT-oracle time. Even recovery of a \(2^q\)-entry
proof by prefix queries has that total cost. At finitely many
exceptional lengths, an absent honest table is detected by the
same SAT formulation and the slice is set to zero.

For source lengths \(N_\nu=2^\nu\), consecutive native widths differ
by \(O(1)\). The assumed bounded changes in \(H\) imply bounded
changes in \(t\); since \(t\to\infty\), consecutive core
lengths have ratio tending to one. At final length \(n\), choose
the largest core at most \(n/2\) and ignore the suffix. Eventually
that core is between \(n/3\) and \(n/2\), and
\begin{equation}
 q\le n\le B_{\rm len}qt(q)                    \label{eq:per-dilation}
\end{equation}
for a fixed \(B_{\rm len}\), which may be chosen from \(K_{\rm pad}\)
and \(q_0\le2q\). Averaging over ignored suffix and internal
padding bits gives a genuine restriction preserving weight,
period and at least the original agreement. In particular a final
period \(m\le n^{1/6}\), or prime period \(p\le n^{2/11}\),
satisfies the corresponding guard \eqref{eq:per-period-guards}.
Thus any final weight cap eventually below \(w(q)\), and any
target advantage eventually above \(e_{t(q)}\), inherits the
strong lower bound; we call this conclusion the common transfer.
Recovery and evaluation take \(2^{O(q)}\)
time with SAT; hence the padded language is in \(\ENP\).

There is one language for the union. The single grammar has two
verified period branches, every branch is sound, and any failed
hardness claim from either branch supplies an honest accepting
list. We did not choose a different refuter input or hard table
for each period.

\subsection{Every fixed inverse-polylogarithmic advantage}
\label{sec:per-log-powers}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:per-log-powers}
For every fixed positive rational \(a\), there are a positive integer
\(C_a\) and one language \(F_a\in\ENP\) such that, at every
sufficiently large length, circuits satisfying either period
condition in \cref{thm:per-endpoint} and
\[
 W(C)\le\floor{\frac{n^3}
 {C_a\lambda_n^{6+4a}\mu_n^{23/2}}}
\]
have agreement less than \(1/2+\lambda_n^{-a}\) with \(F_a\).
In particular \(a=1\) proves \cref{thm:per-endpoint}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the common schedule take
\(H(q)=\ceil{a\lgx\lambda_q}\). For rational \(a\), this
ceiling is found by integer power comparisons. Then
\[
 \lambda_q^a\le R(q)<2\lambda_q^a,
 \qquad t(q)=\Theta(\mu_q).
\]
The changes in \(H\) are bounded, and all schedule hypotheses
hold. The native cap \eqref{eq:per-source-cap} is bounded below
by a positive constant times
\(q^3/(\lambda_q^{6+4a}\mu_q^{17/2})\).
From \eqref{eq:per-dilation}, \(n\le B_aq\mu_q\) for a fixed
\(B_a\). Monotonicity therefore gives
\[
 \frac{n^3}{\lambda_n^{6+4a}\mu_n^{23/2}}
 \le B_a^3\frac{q^3}{\lambda_q^{6+4a}\mu_q^{17/2}}.
\]
Choose \(C_a\) sufficiently large, with a factor two to absorb
the source floor. Also \(\lambda_n/\lambda_q\to1\), so
\(e_{t(q)}\le1/(8\lambda_q^a)<\lambda_n^{-a}\) eventually.
The common transfer proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Regular subpolynomial advantages}
\label{sec:per-regular}

There is no fastest vanishing subpolynomial rate in the following
family. Its regularity hypothesis simply ensures that adjacent
core lengths leave no large gaps.

\begin{theorem}[A regular schedule of advantages]
\label{thm:per-regular}
Let \(\rho:\N\to\N\) be nondecreasing and unbounded, with
\(\rho(u)=o(u)\), and suppose that eventually
\(\rho(u+1)-\rho(u)\in\{0,1\}\). Assume \(\rho(u)\) is
computable in \(2^{O(u)}\) time. Put
\[
 h_n=\rho(\lambda_n+2\mu_n).
\]
There are a positive integer \(C_\rho\) and one language
\(F_\rho\in\ENP\) such that, at every sufficiently large length,
every circuit satisfying either period condition in
\cref{thm:per-endpoint}, with
\begin{equation}
 W(C)\le\floor{\frac{n^3}
 {C_\rho\lambda_n^6\mu_n^8(1+h_n)^{7/2}2^{4h_n}}},
                                                               \label{eq:per-regular-cap}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{agr}(C,F_\rho)<\frac12+2^{-\rho(\lambda_n)}.
\]
The cap in \eqref{eq:per-regular-cap} is \(n^{3-o(1)}\), and
the advantage is vanishing and subpolynomial.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use \(H(q)=\rho(\lambda_q+2\mu_q)\) in the common schedule.
It is computable in \(q^{O(1)}\) time, is \(o(\lambda_q)\),
and is eventually at most \(\lambda_q\). At consecutive source
widths, \(\lambda_q\) changes by at most one, and so does
\(\mu_q\). The argument of \(\rho\) changes by at most three,
so \(H\), and then \(t\), have bounded changes. All witness,
description, word and call envelopes remain polynomial.

Since \(t(q)=\Theta(1+H(q))=O(\lambda_q)\),
\eqref{eq:per-dilation} implies
\[
 \lambda_n\le\lambda_q+\mu_q+O(1)
              \le\lambda_q+2\mu_q
\]
eventually. Thus \(H(q)\ge\rho(\lambda_n)\), and
\(e_{t(q)}\le2^{-H(q)}/8<2^{-\rho(\lambda_n)}\).
On the other hand, monotonicity gives \(h_n\ge H(q)\),
\(\lambda_n\ge\lambda_q\) and \(\mu_n\ge\mu_q\).
Since \(t(q)\le C_t(1+H(q))\),
\begin{align*}
 \frac{n^3}{\lambda_n^6\mu_n^8(1+h_n)^{7/2}2^{4h_n}}
 &\le B_{\rm len}^3
 \frac{q^3t(q)^3}
 {\lambda_q^6\mu_q^8(1+H(q))^{7/2}2^{4H(q)}}\\
 &\le B_{\rm len}^3C_t^{7/2}
 \frac{q^3}{\lambda_q^6\mu_q^8\sqrt{t(q)}\,2^{4H(q)}}.
\end{align*}
Choose \(C_\rho\) sufficiently large to fit
\eqref{eq:per-source-cap}, including its floor. The common
transfer proves the bound at all sufficiently large lengths.
Finally \(h_n=o(\lambda_n)\); hence the entire denominator is
\(n^{o(1)}\), while \(\rho(\lambda_n)\to\infty\) and is
\(o(\log n)\).
\end{proof}

For example, \(\rho(u)=\ceil{\sqrt u}\) satisfies all the
hypotheses. Since
\(\sqrt{\lambda_n+2\mu_n}-\sqrt{\lambda_n}\to0\), the
theorem gives, after changing its fixed constant, weight
\[
 \floor{\frac{n^3}
 {C_{\rm sq}\,2^{4\ceil{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}}
       \lambda_n^{31/4}\mu_n^8}}
\]
and agreement below \(1/2+2^{-\ceil{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}}\), for
one language and the same union of period conditions.

\subsection{Power slack: every exponent below \texorpdfstring{\(\delta/4\)}{delta/4}}
\label{sec:per-power-proof}

To prove \cref{thm:per-power}, fix \(0<\xi<\delta/4\) and choose
a rational \(\kappa\) with
\(\xi<\kappa<\delta/4<3/4\). Use
\(H(q)=\ceil{\kappa\lambda_q}\) in the common schedule.
Then
\[
 R(q)=\Theta(q^\kappa),\qquad
 t(q)=\Theta(\lambda_q),\qquad
 w(q)=\Omega\!\left(
 \frac{q^{3-4\kappa}}{\lambda_q^{13/2}\mu_q^8}\right).
\]
The honest list is \(O(q^{1+2\kappa})<q^4\); the
precision, source and clock bounds of the common schedule still
apply. We use its exact period guards, rather than enlarging
the period by a further power of \(q\).

Here \(n=\Theta(q\log q)\). For every fixed \(A>0\),
\(A n^{3-\delta}\le w(q)\) eventually, because
\(\delta-4\kappa>0\) absorbs the logarithmic factors and the
fixed multiplier. Also
\(e_{t(q)}\le2^{-H(q)}/8=O(q^{-\kappa})<n^{-\xi}\).
The common transfer proves \cref{thm:per-power}; its language
depends on \(\delta,\xi\), but not on \(A\) or on a period.

\begin{corollary}[One language at the limiting power tradeoff]
\label{cor:per-rational-power}
For every rational \(0<\delta<3\), there are a positive integer
\(C_\delta\) and one language \(F_\delta\in\ENP\) such that,
for every fixed \(A>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\), circuits
of top weight at most \(A n^{3-\delta}\) satisfying either period
condition in \cref{thm:per-endpoint} have agreement less than
\[
 \frac12+n^{-\delta/4}
       2^{\ceil{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}+C_\delta\mu_n}.
\]
In particular the advantage is \(n^{-\delta/4+o(1)}\), and the
same language works for every fixed exponent \(\xi<\delta/4\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fix rational \(0<\delta<3\) and in the common schedule put
\[
 H(q)=\max\left(0,
 \max_{1\le v\le\lambda_q}
    \left\{\floor{\delta v/4}-\ceil{\sqrt v}\right\}\right).
\]
This is a computable nondecreasing integer schedule with bounded
increments, and
\(H(q)=\delta\lambda_q/4-\sqrt{\lambda_q}+O_\delta(1)\).
Indeed the unrounded function is eventually increasing, the
roundings change its value by at most two, and each new term
can increase the running maximum by at most one.
Thus \(t(q)=\Theta(\lambda_q)\), and its native cap is
\[
 \Omega_\delta\!\left(
 \frac{q^{3-\delta}2^{4\sqrt{\lambda_q}}}
 {\lambda_q^{13/2}\mu_q^8}\right).
\]
This eventually covers \(A n^{3-\delta}\) for every fixed
\(A>0\). Its native advantage is at most a fixed constant times
\(q^{-\delta/4}2^{\ceil{\sqrt{\lambda_q}}}\).
Since \(n\le B_\delta q\lambda_q\) and \(\lambda_q\le\lambda_n\),
this is at most
\[
 C'\,n^{-\delta/4}\lambda_n^{\delta/4}
                  2^{\ceil{\sqrt{\lambda_n}}}
\]
for a fixed \(C'\). Since \(2^{\mu_n}\ge\lambda_n\) and \(\delta/4<1\),
already \(C_\delta=1\) gives \(C'\lambda_n^{\delta/4}<2^{C_\delta\mu_n}\)
eventually.
The common transfer proves the displayed bound, and
\(\sqrt{\lambda_n}+C_\delta\mu_n=o(\log n)\) proves the final
claims. Rationality makes this schedule effective.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:per-power}, for real constants \(\delta,\xi\), needs no
computability assumption on them: a rational \(\kappa\) strictly
between \(\xi\) and \(\delta/4\) is fixed in its machine.

\paragraph{The remaining question for arbitrary lookups.}
These improvements use a paid representation of actual periodic
lookup products by scalar lookups. An arbitrary original table
need not admit either eligible period, and a common parent
circuit does not supply one. The near-cubic proof for arbitrary
lookups therefore retains its constant-error conclusion. A
vanishing-correlation result for arbitrary lookups requires a further universally useful
representation or verification primitive, with its original-law
precision, coefficient mass, helper weight, witness length and clock
all accounted for.


\section{What remains open at the cubic scale}\label{sec:scope}

The arbitrary-lookup theorem uses genuine Boolean witnesses and reaches
a fixed error boundary. The stronger periodic theorem uses signed
real witnesses and certifies their validity through four-output
probabilities. Its scope includes every declared original period in
the stated ranges; it does not include an arbitrary symmetric lookup.
The target for arbitrary lookups
\[
 W(C)=n^{3-o(1)},\qquad
 \operatorname{agr}(C,F)<\tfrac12+o(1)
\]
is not proved here. In particular, the XOR lemma cannot simply be
appended to the arbitrary-lookup Boolean-table theorem.

The missing interface is precise. An honest XOR reconstruction gives
bounded signed sums of target outputs, but a guessed signed sum need
not be Boolean or even lie in \([0,1]\). Chen--Lyu--Williams'
globally nonnegative test \(S^2(1-S)^2\) handles arbitrary real
guesses. It requires four-output probabilities. For arbitrary
lookups, the scalar counting reduction proved here has no near-cubic cost bound
for those probabilities. Extending a finite table periodically at
its full width gives a period of order \(W\), which does not satisfy
the proved small-period budget. Clearing the denominators of the
signed coefficients rescales the reconstruction but leaves every
atom's original lookup, and hence its period, unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[Moments through degree three are not a validity certificate]
\label{prop:quadratic-failure}
There is a constant-size signed sum of threshold outputs whose first
three moments agree with those of a uniform Boolean bit,
\(\E S=\E S^2=\E S^3=1/2\), while
\(\operatorname{dist}(S,\{0,1\})=1/2\) at every input.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the three-bit cube put
\[
 S(x)=\tfrac12-\ind[x=000]+\ind[x=111].
\]
Both indicators are threshold functions. The values of \(S\) are
\(-1/2\), six copies of \(1/2\), and \(3/2\). Direct averaging
gives the three stated moments and the stated distance; the fourth
moment is \(11/16\), so \(\E S^2(1-S)^2=3/16>0\) does detect it.
\end{proof}

Restricting to convex mixtures also does not reproduce the signed-sum
amplifier. For a Boolean target \(f\), Boolean circuits \(C_j\), and
nonnegative weights \(p_j\) summing to one,
\[
 \E\left|f-\sum_jp_jC_j\right|
 =\sum_jp_j\Pr[f\ne C_j].
\]
Thus some atom is at least as accurate as the mixture. By contrast,
the signed reconstruction uses cancellation and cannot be replaced
by this identity.

The quartic is minimal within one narrowly specified certificate
family. A nonzero polynomial nonnegative on all of \(\R\) and
vanishing at both zero and one must have both roots with even
multiplicity. It is therefore divisible by \(u^2(u-1)^2\) and has
degree at least four. This does not rule out a different verifier,
a source-specific certificate, a more efficient four-output counter,
or a different hardness-amplification representation.

The quantitative schedules trade size for advantage, and there is no
fastest subpolynomial decay rate: a larger
sublinear schedule can give a stronger advantage at a larger
subpolynomial gate loss. The theorems state this tradeoff explicitly.
The limited optimality result, \cref{prop:symmetric-gadget-weight},
for the weight-five gadget applies
only to its stated symmetric quadratic grammar. The period-dependent costs
are proved upper bounds on counting cost, not universal lower bounds
on all algorithms.

The historical comparison is therefore twofold. The arbitrary-lookup
result raises the gate exponent from the published \(5/2\) regime
to a near-cubic regime for symmetric tops, with an almost-everywhere
fixed-error guarantee. The periodic result adds vanishing correlation
at that exponent. Both use \(\ENP\) languages and top-wire
multiplicity; neither changes the complexity class of the hard
function to \(\mathrm P\), nor establishes a corresponding
\(\THR\circ\THR\) gate theorem with unbounded top weights.

\section{Proofs of supporting lemmas with explicit constants}\label{app:standard}

The four lemmas below are variants of published results, recorded
with the explicit constants that the counting algorithm and the
correlation schedule use. Attributions appear with each statement.




\subsection{Live gates}\label{app:live}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:live}]
A threshold gate is unate, so the chain-counting argument in the
proof of \cite[Lemma~A.3.1]{CTW26}, which combines their (14) and (15),
bounds the fraction of restrictions leaving \(g\) live by
\(k\binom{n-k}{\floor{(n-k)/2}}2^{-(n-k)}\). For the second inequality
put \(M=n-k\). If \(M=2r\), the quantities
\((\binom{2r}r4^{-r})^2(2r+1)\) start at one and decrease, because
their successive ratio is \((2r+1)(2r+3)/(2r+2)^2<1\).
The odd case \(M=2r+1\) follows from
\(\binom{2r+1}r2^{-2r-1}=\binom{2r}r4^{-r}(2r+1)/(2r+2)
\le\sqrt{2r+1}/(2r+2)<1/\sqrt{2r+2}\).
Finally, sum the probability estimate over signed unit copies, fix
\(I\) by averaging, and apply Markov's inequality over \(y\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Totient sums and the two-score window}\label{app:window-size}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:window-size}]
These are standard totient-summation calculations; see
Apostol~\cite[Chapters~2--3 and~11]{Apostol76} for the M\"obius
identities, arithmetic averages, and Euler products.
Write \(\mu\) for the M\"obius function. Since
\(m\varphi(m)=\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(d)m^2/d\),
\[
 S(q)=\sum_{d\le q}\mu(d)d\sum_{e\le q/d}e^2.
\]
The sum-of-squares formula gives
\(\abs{\sum_{e\le y}e^2-y^3/3}\le y^2\) for \(y\ge1\).
Therefore
\[
 S(q)\ge\frac{q^3}{3}\sum_{d\le q}\frac{\mu(d)}{d^2}
          -q^2\sum_{d\le q}\frac1d.
\]
Use \(\sum_{d\ge1}\mu(d)/d^2=6/\pi^2\), a tail of magnitude at most
\(1/q\), and \(\sum_{d\le q}1/d\le1+\ln q\).
For \(L=\floor{cD'^{3/4}}\), the associated \(q\) is
\((1/c+o(1))D'^{1/4}\). Thus \(S(q)\ge L\) eventually whenever
\(c^4<2/\pi^2\); this includes \(c=3/5\).
Conversely \(S(q)\le\sum_{m\le q}m^2=O(q^3)\), and
\(q\le(D'-1)/(L-1)\), so every admissible \(L\) is
\(O(D'^{3/4})\).
\end{proof}



\subsection{Disjoint exact-threshold decomposition}\label{app:exact}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:exact}]
Complement negative-weight inputs, obtaining
\(g=\ind[\sum_i\abs{a_i}\widehat z_i\ge t']\).
If \(t'\le0\) or \(t'>\sum_i\abs{a_i}\), use a constant exact test.
Otherwise \(0<t'\le\sum_i\abs{a_i}<2^{B_b}\). Define
\[
 d_j=\floor{t'/2^j}-\sum_i\floor{\abs{a_i}/2^j}\widehat z_i,
 \qquad 0\le j\le B_b.
\]
Then \(d_{B_b}=0\), \(g=\ind[d_0\le0]\), and
\[
 d_j=2d_{j+1}+r_j,\qquad -m\le r_j\le1.
\]
Here \(r_j=\tau_j-\sum_i\delta_{ij}\widehat z_i\), where \(\tau_j\) and
\(\delta_{ij}\) are the \(j\)th binary digits of \(t'\) and \(\abs{a_i}\),
because \(\floor{c/2^j}=2\floor{c/2^{j+1}}+c_j\) for every integer
\(c\ge0\) with \(j\)th binary digit \(c_j\). Thus \(r_j\) is an affine
form with coefficient magnitudes at most one.
If \(d_{j+1}<0\), then \(d_j\le-1\): negativity persists toward
smaller indices. Thus
\[
 \ind[d_0\le0]
 =\ind[d_0=0]+\sum_{j<B_b}\ind[d_{j+1}\ge0>d_j]
\]
is a disjoint sum. At a crossing, the pair
\((a,b)=(d_{j+1},d_j)\) satisfies
\(0\le a\le\floor{(m-1)/2}\) and \(-m\le b\le-1\).
There are at most \(m\ceil{m/2}\) such pairs per level.

Discard a pair if \(b-2a\notin[-m,1]\). For each remaining pair, use
the single exact test
\[
 (m+2)(d_{j+1}-a)+\bigl(r_j-(b-2a)\bigr)=0.
\]
The second term has magnitude at most \(m+1\). Reducing the equality
modulo \(m+2\) forces this term to be zero, then forces
\(d_{j+1}=a\) and \(d_j=b\). All displayed coefficients have the
claimed bit length. The tests are disjoint because the crossing
level and its pair are unique.
\end{proof}


\subsection{The affine cofactor list}\label{app:xor}
\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:per-xor}]
Induct on \(t\). For \(t=1\), take the Boolean predictor itself.
Otherwise split the input into one block \(y\) and the remaining
blocks \(z\). For each fixed \(y\) put
\[
 T(y)=\Pr_z[C(y,z)=f(y)\oplus f^{\oplus(t-1)}(z)].
\]
If some \(y\) has \(|T(y)-1/2|>e_t/(1-\zeta)=e_{t-1}\), then
\(C(y,\cdot)\oplus f(y)\), complemented if \(T(y)<1/2\), agrees with
\(f^{\oplus(t-1)}\) on more than \(1/2+e_{t-1}\) of its inputs; apply
induction to it.

Otherwise \(|T(y)-1/2|\le e_t/(1-\zeta)\) for every \(y\), and
\(\E T\ge1/2+e_t\). Set
\[
 \nu=\frac{\zeta e_t}{2(1-\zeta)},\qquad
 J=\ceil{\frac{8(q_0+2)(1-\zeta)^2}{\zeta^2e_t^2}}.
\]
Hoeffding's inequality~\cite[Theorem~2.2.6]{Vershynin18} and a union bound over all \(2^{q_0}\)
inputs give a fixed list \(z_1,\ldots,z_J\) whose empirical
correctness average differs from \(T(y)\) by at most \(\nu\)
everywhere. Indeed \(2J\nu^2\ge4(q_0+2)\).
Define the labeled cofactors
\[
 C_r(y)=C(y,z_r)\oplus f^{\oplus(t-1)}(z_r),\quad
 A(y)=J^{-1}\sum_rC_r(y),\quad
 \varrho=\frac{e_t(2+\zeta)}{1-\zeta}.
\]
The labels are fixed bits. The conditional bound and the sample
error imply \(|A(y)-1/2|\le\varrho/2\). Hence
\(\mathcal L=(A-1/2)/\varrho+1/2\) lies in \([0,1]\). Rescaling the
empirical correctness average in the same way gives a quantity
\(\widetilde P\) with
\[
 \E\widetilde P\ge\frac{2-\zeta}{2+\zeta}\ge1-\zeta.
\]
Where \(f=1\), \(\mathcal L=\widetilde P\); where \(f=0\),
\(\mathcal L=1-\widetilde P\). Thus \(\E|\mathcal L-f|\le\zeta\).
Here \(t\ge2\) and \(0<\varrho<1\), so the affine mass is at
most \(3/(2\varrho)\). The displayed formulas give the stated
rational word bounds. Each reduction replaces \(e_t\) by the larger
\(e_{t-1}\), so the bounds stated for \(e_t\) also cover the level at
which the recursion stops.
\end{proof}

\section{Multitape readings of the refuter and the succinct verifier}\label{app:multitape}

This appendix supplies the machine-model facts that both lower-bound
transfers use (\cref{sec:gadget-transfer,sec:per-transfer}). Both
transfers apply \cref{imp:ae-refuter} and \cref{imp:short-pcp} to
multitape machines, the model of \cref{sec:prelim}. The refuter's
proof in \cite[Section~4]{CLW20}, however, assumes random-access
machines for simplicity. The appendix has three parts.
\begin{itemize}
\item \Cref{lem:multitape-refuter} derives the multitape reading of the
refuter from the proof in \cite[Section~4]{CLW20}. It imports the
universal simulation of Fortnow and Santhanam~\cite{FS16}, and it gives
the hierarchy language \(L_{\Tclk}\) a multitape verifier
\(\mathcal D\).
\item \Cref{lem:succinct-verifier} constructs the one fixed machine to
which the short PCP is applied, the succinct hierarchy verifier
\(\mathcal V_G\). It reads a source input through the short description
of a circuit and runs \(\mathcal D\) on the described word.
\Cref{app:short-pcp-proof} proves the short PCP for it.
\item The paragraph after \cref{lem:succinct-verifier} fixes the order
in which the constants of both transfers are chosen.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[Multitape refuter]\label{lem:multitape-refuter}
\Cref{imp:ae-refuter} holds with every machine in it, the class
\(\NTIME\) and the procedure \(R_{\Tclk}\) read for multitape Turing
machines, with the same quantifier order. The language \(L_{\Tclk}\) has a
nondeterministic multitape verifier that halts within \(O(\Tclk(N))\)
steps on every input and every guess.
\end{lemma}

\emph{Idea of proof.} The diagonal language of Chen, Lyu, and Williams
runs each enumerated machine through a universal simulation. We use the
linear-time universal simulation of Fortnow and Santhanam for multitape
machines, under an explicit clock of \(O(\Tclk(N))\) steps (Step~1). For
each admitted machine the clock stops no simulation at large lengths, so
\cite[Lemma~4.3]{CLW20} holds for it (Step~2). The rest
of their proof uses the machine model only through that lemma and
through SAT-oracle queries, which the Cook--Levin tableau handles for
multitape machines (Step~3). So their refuter applies verbatim
(Step~4), and the guess budgets are unchanged (Step~5).

\begin{proof}
\emph{Conventions.} A nondeterministic multitape machine reads its
guessed bits from a witness tape and is deterministic once the witness
is fixed. It guesses at most \(g(N)\) bits when every run on an input of
length \(N\) reads at most \(g(N)\) witness cells. Fix \(\Tclk\), which is
computable from \(1^N\) in \(O(\Tclk(N))\) steps, and an enumeration of
these machines. As in \cite[Section~4.1]{CLW20}, \(V_M(x,T,z)\) is the
outcome of running \(M\) on \(x\) with witness \(z\) for at most \(T\)
steps. It is \(\bot\) when the run does not halt within \(T\) steps or
reads more than \(\abs z\) witness bits. A machine that guesses at most
\(g(N)\le\abs z\) bits behaves the same with the witness \(z\) and with
its prefix of length \(g(N)\). So it may equally be given exactly
\(g(N)\) witness bits. The weak machines of this paper guess at most
\(\floor{N/16}\) bits, and the Lean proof gives them exactly
\(\floor{N/16}\) framed witness bits. Conversely, a machine that receives
exactly \(\floor{N/16}\) framed witness bits is read as the machine that
first copies the first \(\floor{N/16}\) cells of its witness tape into a
framed word on a work tape and then runs on that word; this adds
\(O(N)=o(\Tclk(N))\) steps, reads exactly \(\floor{N/16}\) witness cells,
and decides the same language, so the lemma applies to it.

\emph{The universal simulation.} We use the universal simulation of
Fortnow and Santhanam~\cite[proof of Theorem~7.1]{FS16}. A
nondeterministic multitape machine that runs in time \(t\) is simulated
by a two-tape nondeterministic machine, given its description, in time
\(O(t)\). The constant depends only on the simulated machine.

\emph{Step 1: the hierarchy verifier.} Let \(\mathcal D\) be the following
clocked variant of the algorithm \(A^{\Tclk}_{\rm FS}\) of
\cite[Definition~4.2]{CLW20}. First \(\mathcal D\) computes \(\Tclk(N)\).
It then parses its input as \(\langle M,N,z\rangle\) with
\(\abs z=\floor{N/10}\), rejecting otherwise. This takes at most \(c_0N\)
steps for a constant \(c_0\) that does not depend on \(M\). It then runs
two universal simulations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item the machine \(\overline M\), obtained from \(M\) by exchanging its
accepting and rejecting halting states, on input
\(\langle M,N,0^{\floor{N/10}}\rangle\) with witness \(z\);
\item when \(z\ne1^{\floor{N/10}}\), the machine \(M\) on input
\(\langle M,N,z+1\rangle\) with a guessed witness of \(\floor{N/10}\)
bits.
\end{enumerate}
It accepts exactly when both simulations accept. Because the run in (a)
has a fixed witness, it is deterministic. So simulation (a) accepts
exactly when that run of \(M\) halts and rejects. A clock rejects every
branch whose steps after computing \(\Tclk(N)\) exceed
\((c_0+2)\Tclk(N)\). Hence every branch halts within \(O(\Tclk(N))\)
steps on every input, and \(L_{\Tclk}=L(\mathcal D)\) has the stated
verifier.

The language \(L(\mathcal D)\) may differ from \(L(A^{\Tclk}_{\rm FS})\)
on inputs where a simulation exceeds the clock or the encoded machine
breaks its promise. Step~2 shows that it obeys the formula of
\cite[Lemma~4.3]{CLW20} on every input that the proofs use.

\emph{Step 2: Lemma~4.3 of \cite{CLW20} holds for every admitted machine.}
Fix \(M\) that runs in time \(t_M(N)=o(\Tclk(N))\) on every input and
witness and guesses at most \(\floor{N/10}\) bits. Consider an input
\(x^{(N)}_i=\langle M,N,w^{(N)}_i\rangle\) of \cite[Section~4.2]{CLW20}.
After computing \(\Tclk(N)\), \(\mathcal D\) spends at most
\(c_0N+O_M(1)\) steps parsing and writing the descriptions of \(M\) and
\(\overline M\), and at most \(2c_Mt_M(N)\) steps in the two
simulations. Here \(c_M\) is the constant of the universal simulation;
it depends only on \(M\). Since \(N\le\Tclk(N)\) and
\(t_M(N)=o(\Tclk(N))\), the total is below \((c_0+2)\Tclk(N)\) for all
sufficiently large \(N\). So the clock never stops a simulation. Every
run of \(M\) halts within \(t_M(N)\le\Tclk(N)\) steps and reads at most
\(\floor{N/10}\) witness bits. Hence \(\mathcal D\) accepts
\(x^{(N)}_i\) exactly when \(V_M(x^{(N)}_1,\Tclk(N),w^{(N)}_i)=0\) and,
for \(i<2^{\floor{N/10}}\), \(M(x^{(N)}_{i+1})=1\). This is the formula
of \cite[Lemma~4.3]{CLW20}, which therefore holds for \(\mathcal D\) and
\(M\) at every sufficiently large \(N\). The overhead is the constant
factor \(c_M\), so the little-\(o\) promise needs no logarithmic slack.

\emph{Step 3: the oracle queries.} The proofs of \cite[Theorems~1.12
and~4.6]{CLW20} use the machine model only through Lemma~4.3 and through
SAT-oracle queries of three kinds:
\begin{itemize}
\item whether \(\mathcal D\) accepts a given input;
\item whether \(M\) accepts a given input within \(\Tclk(N)\) steps with
some witness in a given lexicographic interval;
\item whether \(V_M(x,\Tclk(N),w)=1\) for given \(x\) and \(w\).
\end{itemize}
Each query concerns runs of \(O(\Tclk(N))\) steps of one nondeterministic
multitape machine. The Cook--Levin tableau turns it into a CNF formula
of size \(O(\Tclk(N)^2)\). The formula is constructed in
\(\poly(\Tclk(N))\) time by a polynomial whose degree does not depend on
\(M\); for queries about \(M\), only the constant factor depends on
\(M\).

\emph{Step 4: the refuter.} With Steps~2 and~3, the proof of
\cite[Theorem~4.6]{CLW20} applies verbatim. \(R_{\Tclk}\) first asks
whether \(M\) accepts \(x^{(N)}_1\). If not, it runs the binary search of
\cite[Corollary~4.5]{CLW20} on the lists \(\mathcal D(x^{(N)}_i)\) and
\(M(x^{(N)}_i)\), \(1\le i\le2^{\floor{N/10}}\). If so, it finds by
binary search the least \(j\) with \(V_M(x^{(N)}_1,\Tclk(N),w^{(N)}_j)=1\)
and runs the same search on the first \(j\) entries. Either way it makes
\(O(N)\) adaptive queries and runs in \(\poly(\Tclk(N))\) time, with a
degree that does not depend on \(M\). At every sufficiently large \(N\) it
returns a word \(x^{(N)}_p\) with \(\mathcal D(x^{(N)}_p)\ne M(x^{(N)}_p)\).

\emph{Step 5: the guess budgets.} The guesses made by the simulations in
Step~1 are guesses of \(\mathcal D\), not of \(M\), so the promise on \(M\)
is unchanged. The weak machines of
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer,sec:per-transfer} guess at most
\(\floor{N/16}\le\floor{N/10}\) bits and run in time \(o(\Tclk(N))\), so
the lemma applies to them.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Succinct hierarchy verifier]\label{lem:succinct-verifier}
Fix integers \(G\ge1\), \(G'\ge2\) and \(C_{\rm clk}\ge2\), let
\(\Tclk(N)=N^{C_{\rm clk}}\), let \(\mathcal D\) be the verifier of
\(L_{\Tclk}\) from \cref{lem:multitape-refuter}, and let
\(L_\nu=\nu^{G'}\). Use the self-delimiting binary description of
general circuits fixed in \cref{sec:gadget-transfer}, and let
\(\mathrm{tt}_N\) and \(\langle B\rangle_\nu\) be as there. There are a deterministic multitape machine
\(\mathcal V_G(u,y)\), with input \(u\) and witness \(y\), and a constant
\(c_G\ge1\) such that, with
\[
 T_G(n)=c_G\,2^{C_{\rm clk}\floor{n^{1/G'}}},
\]
the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(T_G\) is nondecreasing, \(T_G(n)\ge n\) for every \(n\ge1\),
\(T_G(n)\) is computable from \(n\) in \(\poly(n)\) steps, and
\(T_G(L_\nu)=c_G\Tclk(2^\nu)\) for every \(\nu\ge1\).
\item For every \(n\ge1\), every \(u\in\{0,1\}^n\) and every \(y\),
\(\mathcal V_G\) halts within \(T_G(n)\) steps.
\item Let \(\nu\ge1\) and \(N=2^\nu\), and let \(B\) be a general circuit
with at least \(\nu\) inputs whose description has at most \(L_\nu\)
bits. Some \(y\) of length \(T_G(L_\nu)\) makes
\(\mathcal V_G(\langle B\rangle_\nu,y)\) accept if and only if
\(\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\in L_{\Tclk}\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\emph{Idea of proof.} The verifier \(\mathcal V_G\) checks that its
input has the length \(L_\nu\) of a padded description. It then expands
the described circuit \(B\) into its truth table \(\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\)
and runs \(\mathcal D\) on that word with its own witness. Expanding
takes \(O(NL_\nu^3)\) steps and running \(\mathcal D\) takes
\(O(\Tclk(N))\) steps, so at the lengths \(L_\nu\) the time bound
\(T_G\) is a constant multiple of \(\Tclk(N)\). At every other length
\(\mathcal V_G\) rejects within \(O(n)\) steps.

\begin{proof}
On \((u,y)\) with \(\abs u=n\), the machine \(\mathcal V_G\) works in four
stages.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item It writes \(n\) in binary, computes \(\nu=\floor{n^{1/G'}}\), and
rejects unless \(\nu\ge1\) and \(n=\nu^{G'}\). This takes \(O(n)\)
steps.
\item It parses \(u\) as the description of a general circuit \(B\)
followed by zeros. It rejects if the description is malformed or \(B\)
has fewer than \(\nu\) inputs. This takes \(O(n)\) steps.
\item For \(i=0,1,\ldots,N-1\), where \(N=2^\nu\), it evaluates \(B\) at
\(\mathrm{bin}_\nu(i)0^{a-\nu}\), where \(a\) is the number of inputs of
\(B\). It writes the value into cell \(i\) of a work tape, which then
holds \(\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\). The circuit has at most \(n\) gates. Keeping
the gate values on a tape and fetching the two arguments of each gate by
a scan evaluates it in \(O(n^3)\) steps. So this stage takes
\(O(Nn^3)\) steps.
\item It runs \(\mathcal D\) on \(\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\) and accepts exactly
when \(\mathcal D\) accepts. The work tape of stage~(3) serves as the
input tape of \(\mathcal D\), its own witness tape as the witness tape
of \(\mathcal D\), and further work tapes as those of \(\mathcal D\). By
\cref{lem:multitape-refuter} this takes at most
\(c_{\mathcal D}\Tclk(N)\) steps for a constant \(c_{\mathcal D}\).
\end{enumerate}
If \(n=L_\nu\), the total is
\[
 O(n)+O(NL_\nu^3)+c_{\mathcal D}\Tclk(N)\le c'2^{C_{\rm clk}\nu}
\]
for a constant \(c'\), because \(L_\nu^3=\nu^{3G'}\) and
\(C_{\rm clk}\ge2\). Otherwise the run stops in stage~(1) within
\(c''n\) steps, and \(n<(\nu+1)^{G'}\) with \(\nu=\floor{n^{1/G'}}\).
Choose \(c_G\ge c'\) so large that
\(c_G2^{C_{\rm clk}\nu}\ge(c''+1)(\nu+1)^{G'}\) for every \(\nu\ge1\).
This is possible because \((\nu+1)^{G'}2^{-C_{\rm clk}\nu}\) is bounded.
Then (ii) holds, and so does \(T_G(n)\ge n\). The other assertions of (i)
are immediate from the definition.

For (iii), if \(\mathcal V_G(\langle B\rangle_\nu,y)\) accepts, then
\(\mathcal D\) accepts \(\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\) with a witness, so
\(\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\in L(\mathcal D)=L_{\Tclk}\). Conversely, suppose that
\(\mathrm{tt}_N(B)\in L_{\Tclk}\), and take an accepting run of
\(\mathcal D\) on it. The run has at most
\(c_{\mathcal D}\Tclk(N)\le T_G(L_\nu)\) steps, so it reads only witness
cells below \(T_G(L_\nu)\). Writing the witness bits it reads into a word
\(y\) of length \(T_G(L_\nu)\), with zeros elsewhere, gives the same run,
and \(\mathcal V_G(\langle B\rangle_\nu,y)\) accepts.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Use in the transfers and the order of constants.} In
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer,sec:per-transfer}, \(\mathcal D\) is the
multitape verifier of \(L_{\Tclk}\) from \cref{lem:multitape-refuter},
with \(\Tclk(N)=N^{C_{\rm clk}}\), and \cref{imp:short-pcp} is applied to
\(\mathcal V_G\) with the time bound \(T_G\). The constants are chosen
in this order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(d_0=2\), written \(d_{\rm ver}\) in \cref{sec:per-transfer}. It
bounds the exponent of \(N\) in the check \eqref{eq:source-check} and
depends on no PCP construction.
\item The general-circuit exponent \(G\), from \(h_{\rm sim}\) or \(h'_{\rm sim}\) and
\(b_{\rm amp}\); it does not depend on \(C_{\rm clk}\).
\item The exponent \(G'\ge2\), from \(G\) and the description format of
general circuits (\cref{sec:gadget-transfer}). It fixes
\(L_\nu=\nu^{G'}\), and it does not depend on \(C_{\rm clk}\) or
\(A_{\rm pad}\).
\item An integer \(C_{\rm clk}>d_0+2\). This fixes \(\mathcal D\), and
\cref{lem:succinct-verifier} then fixes \(\mathcal V_G\), \(c_G\) and
\(T_G\).
\item \Cref{imp:short-pcp}, applied to \(\mathcal V_G\) and \(T_G\), fixes
a constant \(c_\ell\ge1\) with
\[
 \ell\le\lgx T_G(n)+c_\ell\lgx(\lgx T_G(n)+2)
\]
for every \(n\), and the polynomials bounding the size of \(C^O_u\) and
the running time of its construction. (\Cref{thm:short-pcp-ours} derives
them from the constants of~\cite{BCGT13,BSCGT13}.)
\item An integer \(A_{\rm pad}\ge c_\ell\) with
\(A_{\rm pad}\ge b+c_\ell\lgx(b+1)\), where \(b=\lgx c_G\). For
\(N=2^\nu\) write \(\tau=\lgx\Tclk(N)=C_{\rm clk}\nu\). Since
\(T_G(L_\nu)=c_G2^\tau\) and \(\tau+b+2\le(\tau+2)(b+1)\),
\[
 \ell_{\rm out}\le\tau+b+c_\ell\lgx(\tau+b+2)
 \le\tau+c_\ell\lgx(\tau+2)+b+c_\ell\lgx(b+1)\le q(N).
\]
So the outer verifier fits the native width. Its oracle queries
\(a\in\{0,1\}^{\ell_{\rm out}}\) can be zero-padded to the \(q\)
inputs of \(B_{\rm pf}\), and the tables \(X_z\) and
\(\widehat O^*\) of \cref{sec:gadget-transfer,sec:per-canonical} have
\(q\) inputs. Moreover
\[
 2^{q(N)}\le2\cdot4^{A_{\rm pad}}\Tclk(N)(\lgx\Tclk(N)+2)^{A_{\rm pad}},
\]
so \(2^q/\Tclk(N)=q^{O(1)}\).
\item Then the remaining constants, in this order:
\begin{itemize}
\item the clause-count exponent \(d_G\);
\item in \cref{sec:per-transfer}, also the fixed reserve of
\cref{lem:per-scalar}, \(c_*\) and the schedule constants;
\item finally the padding and cap constants: \(K_{\rm amp}\),
\(B_{\rm len}\) and \(K_d\), \(K\) or \(C_0\) in
\cref{sec:gadget-transfer}, and \(K_{\rm pad}\), \(B_{\rm len}\) and
\(C_{\rm per}\) or its analogues in \cref{sec:per-transfer}.
\end{itemize}
The constant \(\eta_0\) of \cref{sec:gadget-transfer} depends only on
\cref{thm:pair-uniform}.
\end{enumerate}
No constant depends on one chosen after it. In particular,
\(\mathcal V_G\) bounds the size of a source circuit only through the
length \(L_\nu\). That length depends on \(N\) and \(G'\) alone, not on
\(q\) or \(A_{\rm pad}\). The weak machines enforce the bound \(q^G\)
themselves.

\section{The short PCP for the verifiers used here}\label{app:short-pcp-proof}

This appendix proves \cref{imp:short-pcp}, the short PCP in the form of
Murray and Williams, for deterministic multitape verifiers with
computable time bounds (\cref{thm:short-pcp-ours}). This case covers its
only use. Both transfers apply \cref{imp:short-pcp} to one verifier: the
succinct hierarchy verifier \(\mathcal V_G\) of
\cref{lem:succinct-verifier}, in \cref{sec:gadget-transfer} and in
\cref{sec:per-machine}. We import the PCP of Ben-Sasson, Chiesa, Genkin,
and Tromer for succinct algebraic constraint satisfaction problems
(sACSP)~\cite{BCGT13}, and their reduction to it from random-access
machines~\cite{BSCGT13}. References to numbered statements
of~\cite{BCGT13} are to its full version.

\paragraph{Plan of the proof.} The verifier of~\cite{BCGT13} amplifies
the soundness of its proximity sub-verifiers by naive sequential repetition. This
multiplies the randomness by a polylogarithmic factor
\cite[Remark~11.2 and Lemmas~C.6--C.8]{BCGT13}, whereas
\cref{imp:short-pcp} has randomness \(\lgx T+O(\log\log T)\). We
therefore run each sub-verifier once and amplify the whole verifier with
a small-bias set, as in~\cite[Section~4.4]{BV14}. The appendix has four
parts.
\begin{itemize}
\item \Cref{lem:small-bias,lem:patch-r} give small-bias sets and the
amplification that uses them.
\item \Cref{lem:tm-ram} turns a multitape verifier into a random-access
machine whose run has an exact length.
\item \Cref{thm:short-pcp-ours} assembles the pieces in four steps.
\item The routine proof of \cref{lem:tm-ram} comes last.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Notation.} As in \cref{imp:short-pcp}, \(n\) and \(T(n)\) are the
input length and the time bound of a general verifier. The symbols
\(c,d,f,k,r,s,t,S,\varepsilon,\eta,\mu,\sigma\) are local to this
appendix. Write \(\alpha\cdot\sigma\) for the inner product over
\(\mathbb F_2\).

\begin{lemma}[Small-bias sets]\label{lem:small-bias}
Let \(r\ge1\) and \(0<\varepsilon<1\). A multiset
\(S\subseteq\{0,1\}^r\) is \emph{\(\varepsilon\)-biased} if
\(\abs{\E_{\sigma\in S}(-1)^{\alpha\cdot\sigma}}\le\varepsilon\) for every
nonzero \(\alpha\in\{0,1\}^r\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item An \(\varepsilon\)-biased multiset of size less than
\((2r/\varepsilon)^2\) can be computed deterministically in
\(\poly(r/\varepsilon)\) steps.
\item If \(S\) is \(\varepsilon\)-biased and
\(\mathcal B\subseteq\{0,1\}^r\) has density \(\mu>0\), then the set of
\(R\in\{0,1\}^r\) with \(R\oplus\sigma\notin\mathcal B\) for every
\(\sigma\in S\) has density at most \(\varepsilon^2/\mu\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) This is the powering construction of Alon, Goldreich, H\aa{}stad,
and Peralta~\cite{AGHP92}; see also Naor and Naor~\cite{NN93}. Let \(m\)
be the least integer with \(2^m\ge r/\varepsilon\), so that
\(2^m<2r/\varepsilon\). Find an irreducible polynomial of degree \(m\)
over \(\mathbb F_2\) by exhaustive search, in \(2^m\poly(m)\) steps, and
identify \(\mathbb F_{2^m}\) with \(\mathbb F_2^m\). For
\(a,b\in\mathbb F_{2^m}\) let \(\sigma_{a,b}\in\{0,1\}^r\) have
coordinates \(a^j\cdot b\), \(0\le j<r\), and let \(S\) consist of all
\(2^{2m}<(2r/\varepsilon)^2\) vectors \(\sigma_{a,b}\). For nonzero
\(\alpha\), \(\alpha\cdot\sigma_{a,b}=p_\alpha(a)\cdot b\), where
\(p_\alpha(X)=\sum_j\alpha_jX^j\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree less
than \(r\). If \(p_\alpha(a)\ne0\), the bit \(p_\alpha(a)\cdot b\) is
unbiased over \(b\), and \(p_\alpha(a)=0\) for fewer than \(r\) values
of \(a\). Hence the bias is less than \(r/2^m\le\varepsilon\).

(ii) Let \(g(R)=\E_{\sigma\in S}\ind_{\mathcal B}(R\oplus\sigma)\), and
write \(\widehat h(\alpha)=\E_R h(R)(-1)^{\alpha\cdot R}\). Then
\(\E_Rg(R)=\mu\) and
\(\widehat g(\alpha)=\widehat{\ind_{\mathcal B}}(\alpha)
\E_{\sigma\in S}(-1)^{\alpha\cdot\sigma}\). By Parseval,
\[
 \E_R\bigl(g(R)-\mu\bigr)^2=\sum_{\alpha\ne0}\widehat g(\alpha)^2
 \le\varepsilon^2\sum_{\alpha\ne0}\widehat{\ind_{\mathcal B}}(\alpha)^2
 =\varepsilon^2(\mu-\mu^2).
\]
On the set in question \(g=0\), so \((g-\mu)^2=\mu^2\) there, and its
density is at most \(\varepsilon^2(\mu-\mu^2)/\mu^2\le\varepsilon^2/\mu\).
\end{proof}

Part (ii) is the hitting property~\cite[Lemma~4.7]{BV14}. Equivalently,
it follows from the expander mixing lemma of Alon and
Chung~\cite{AC88} for the Cayley graph of \(\mathbb F_2^r\) generated by
\(S\), whose nontrivial eigenvalues are the biases
\(\E_{\sigma\in S}(-1)^{\alpha\cdot\sigma}\).

\begin{lemma}[Randomness-efficient amplification]\label{lem:patch-r}
Let \(V_0\) be a PCP verifier that uses \(r_0\) random bits on an
instance \(x\), and let \(0<s\le1\) and \(0<\sigma_0\le1\). Let
\(S\subseteq\{0,1\}^{r_0}\) be \(\varepsilon\)-biased with
\(\varepsilon^2\le s\sigma_0\). Let \(V\) accept \(x\) with proof \(\pi\)
and random string \(R\in\{0,1\}^{r_0}\) exactly when \(V_0\) accepts
\(x\) with \(\pi\) and \(R\oplus\sigma\) for every \(\sigma\in S\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \(V\) uses \(r_0\) random bits and \(\abs S\) times as many queries
as \(V_0\); if \(V_0\) is non-adaptive, so is \(V\).
\item A proof that \(V_0\) accepts with probability one is accepted by
\(V\) with probability one.
\item If \(V_0\) rejects \(\pi\) with probability at least \(\sigma_0\),
then \(V\) accepts \(\pi\) with probability at most \(s\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Parts (i) and (ii) are immediate, because \(R\oplus\sigma\) is again a
random string of \(V_0\). For (iii), let \(\mathcal B\) be the set of
random strings on which \(V_0\) rejects \(\pi\); its density is
\(\mu\ge\sigma_0\). The verifier \(V\) accepts exactly on the set of
\cref{lem:small-bias}(ii), whose density is at most
\(\varepsilon^2/\mu\le s\sigma_0/\sigma_0=s\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Multitape machines as random-access machines]\label{lem:tm-ram}
Let \(V(x,y)\) be a deterministic multitape machine that halts within
\(T(n)\ge n\) steps on every \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\) and every \(y\). There are
constants \(c_V,k\ge1\) with the following properties. For each \(n\)
let the register width
\(w_n=2^{\ceil{\lgx(\ceil{\lgx T(n)}+c_V)}}\) be the least power of two
with \(w_n\ge\ceil{\lgx T(n)}+c_V\), for \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\) let
\(\bar x=x10^j\) be its padding to length
\(2^{\ceil{\lgx\max(n+1,w_n)}}\), a multiple of \(w_n\), and let \(T_n\)
be the least power of two with \(T_n\ge c_V(T(n)+w_n)\). There are an
arithmetic unit \(\mathsf A\) and, for each \(n\), a code
\(\mathsf C_n\) and the random-access machine
\(M_n=\langle w_n,k,\mathsf A,\mathsf C_n\rangle\)
of~\cite[Definition~6.6]{BSCGT13}, such that for every
\(x\in\{0,1\}^n\) and every witness \(w\), the computation of \(M_n\) on
\((\bar x,w)\) reaches its \texttt{out} instruction at exactly step
\(T_n-1\), so that its sequence of configurations has length exactly
\(T_n\), accesses fewer than \(T_n\) memory addresses, and accepts
exactly when \(V\) accepts \(x\) with the witness \(y_w\) of length
\(T(n)\) consisting of the first \(T(n)\) bits of \(w\), followed by
zeros if \(w\) is shorter. Moreover \(\abs{\bar x}\le T_n\), and the
transition function of \(M_n\) has size and degree polynomial in
\(w_n\), as a Boolean circuit and as an arithmetic circuit over
\(\mathbb F_2\), respectively.
\end{lemma}

The proof of \cref{lem:tm-ram} is a direct simulation followed by an
idle phase that pads the run to exactly \(T_n\) configurations. It is
routine, and we give it after the proof of \cref{thm:short-pcp-ours}.

\begin{theorem}[\Cref{imp:short-pcp} for multitape verifiers]
\label{thm:short-pcp-ours}
Let \(V(x,y)\) be a deterministic multitape machine and \(T:\N\to\N\) a
function with \(T(n)\ge n\), such that \(V\) halts within \(T(n)\) steps
on every \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\) and every \(y\), and \(T(n)\) is computable
from \(n\) in \(\poly(n,\log T(n))\) steps. Then the conclusion of
\cref{imp:short-pcp} holds for \(V\) and \(T\). In particular it holds
for \(\mathcal V_G\) and \(T_G\) of \cref{lem:succinct-verifier}.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Idea of proof.} \Cref{lem:tm-ram} turns \(V\) into a random-access
machine whose run has exactly \(T_n\) configurations. The reduction
of~\cite{BSCGT13} turns the question whether that run accepts into an
sACSP instance (Step~1). The PCP verifier of~\cite{BCGT13} for such
instances repeats its proximity sub-verifiers, which costs randomness;
we run each of them once (Step~2). The resulting verifier \(V_0\) is
sound only in a weak sense: it rejects every proof of a false instance
with probability at least \(1/\poly(\log T)\). A small-bias set amplifies this to soundness
\(1/n\) without new random bits (Step~3). Finally, the verifier is
written as a circuit whose proof reads are oracle gates (Step~4). The
governing bounds are
\[
 r_0\le\log T+O(\log\log T),\qquad
 \sigma_0\ge\frac1{\poly(\log T)},\qquad
 \abs S<\frac{4r_0^2n}{\sigma_0},\qquad
 \ell=\log T+O(\log\log T),
\]
for the randomness, the base rejection probability, the size of the
small-bias set, and the number of inputs of \(C^O_x\). Every step runs
in time \(\poly(n,\log T)\).

\begin{proof}
Fix \(n\) and \(x\in\{0,1\}^n\). Write \(T=T(n)\), and take \(w_n\),
\(\bar x\), \(T_n\) and \(M_n\) from \cref{lem:tm-ram}. The construction
needs \(T\) explicitly. The verifier of~\cite{BCGT13} receives the time
bound as part of its instance \((\bar x,1^t)\) with \(T_n=2^t\), and
\(w_n\) and the code of \(M_n\) depend on \(T\). By hypothesis \(T\), and
hence \(w_n\), \(T_n\) and \(t\), are computed in \(\poly(n,\log T)\)
steps. All logarithms below are base two, and
\(\poly(n,\log T)\) bounds have polynomials depending only on \(V\) and
\(T\).

\emph{Step 1: reduction to sACSP.} By \cref{lem:tm-ram}, \(V(x,y)\)
accepts for some \(y\) of length \(T\) if and only if, for some witness
\(w\) of length at most \(T_n\), the computation of \(M_n\) on
\((\bar x,w)\) is accepting. That computation has exactly \(T_n\)
configurations and accesses fewer than \(T_n\) addresses. Given \(y\),
take for \(w\) the word \(y\) padded with zeros to a multiple of
\(w_n\); its length is at most \(T+w_n\le T_n\). A valid sequence of
configurations consistent with \((\bar x,w)\)~\cite[Definitions~6.10
and~6.13]{BSCGT13} is the computation of \(M_n\) on \(\bar x\) and the
witness formed by the words that it reads from the witness tape.

Since \(\abs{\bar x}\le T_n\), this says that \((\bar x,T_n,T_n)\) is in
the bounded-halting language \(\mathrm{BH}_{\rm RAM}(M_n)\)
of~\cite[Definition~6.14]{BSCGT13}. That language asks for an accepting
sequence of exactly \(2^t\) configurations, here with \(2^t=T_n\) as
time and space bound. By~\cite[Theorem~2]{BSCGT13} there is a parameter
choice \(\mathrm{par}=p(M_n)\) for sACSP, computed in linear time, such
that \((\bar x,T_n,T_n)\in\mathrm{BH}_{\rm RAM}(M_n)\) if and only if the
instance lies in \(\mathrm{sACSP}(\mathrm{par})\). The field \(F\) of
that choice and the number \(c_N\) of neighbor functions of its
constraint satisfy
\[
 \abs F=O((\log T_n+kw_n)\log T_n\cdot d)T_n,\qquad
 c_N=O(\log T_n+kw_n).
\]
Here \(d\) is the degree of the transition function of \(M_n\), and each
neighbor function is a polynomial of degree
one~\cite[Theorem~2 and Section~8.2]{BSCGT13}. All parameters are
generated in \(\poly(\log\abs F)\) field operations. Since \(k\) is
fixed and \(w_n\) and \(d\) are polynomial in \(\log T\), the extension
degree \(f=\log\abs F\) and \(c_N\) satisfy
\[
 f=\log T+O(\log\log T),\qquad c_N=O(\log T).
\]

\emph{Step 2: the verifier with single sub-verifier runs.} Let \(V_0\)
be the verifier of~\cite[Construction~8.5]{BCGT13} for
\(\mathrm{sACSP}(\mathrm{par})\), with one change. Each of the amplified
proximity verifiers \(V_{\rm aRS}\) and \(V_{\rm aVRS}\) in its Steps~2,
3 and~5 is replaced by a single run of the corresponding strong
proximity verifier \(V_{\rm RS}\) or \(V_{\rm VRS}\). All steps read
prefixes of one random string, as in~\cite[Algorithm~9]{BCGT13}. The
proof format is that of~\cite[Definition~8.4]{BCGT13}: five tables of
elements of \(F\), which we encode in binary with \(f\) bits per
element. We use the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Randomness and length.} By~\cite[Lemmas~C.1--C.5]{BCGT13},
one run of \(V_{\rm RS}\) or \(V_{\rm VRS}\) on a domain of dimension
\(f\) uses at most \(f+(\mu_{\rm rep}+2)\log(2f)\) random bits. Here \(\mu_{\rm rep}\) is
one of the fixed integer parameters of~\cite[Theorem~11.1]{BCGT13}. The
consistency test uses \(f\) bits. As the steps share their random
string, \(V_0\) uses
\[
 r_0\le f+(\mu_{\rm rep}+2)\log(2f)=\log T+O(\log\log T)
\]
random bits; compare~\cite[Lemma~C.8]{BCGT13}, where each term is
multiplied by the number of repetitions. The same lemmas bound the proof
length by \(2^ff^{O(1)}\) field elements. So the binary proof has
\(L_\pi\) bits, where
\[
 L_\pi\le2^ff^{O(1)},\qquad \log L_\pi=\log T+O(\log\log T).
\]
\item \emph{Time and queries.} \(V_0\) runs in time
\[
 \poly(n,\log T).
\]
We bound the parts of its work in turn.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Field operations.} The field \(F\) is represented by an
irreducible polynomial that Shoup's deterministic algorithm finds in
\(\poly(f)\) steps~\cite[Algorithm~17]{BCGT13}. So each field operation
takes \(\poly(f)\) steps.
\item \emph{Parameters.} The parameters of~\cite[Theorem~2]{BSCGT13}
include the constraint polynomial, an arithmetic circuit of size
\(O(\abs{M_n}+f)\) that the consistency test evaluates. They are
generated in \(\poly(\abs{M_n},f)\) field operations, where
\(\abs{M_n}=\poly(w_n)=\poly(f)\) is the size of the transition
function.
\item \emph{Sub-verifier runs.} A single run of \(V_{\rm RS}\) or
\(V_{\rm VRS}\) takes \(\poly(f)\) field operations. Its recursion
roughly halves the dimension of the current affine subspace at each
level and does \(\poly(f)\) field operations per
level~\cite[Algorithms~12--16]{BCGT13}.
\item \emph{The instance.} The only step whose cost depends on
\(\abs{\bar x}\) is the evaluation of the low-degree extension \(A_x\)
of the instance at the queried points (Step~5
of~\cite[Construction~8.5]{BCGT13}). By Lagrange interpolation over the
subspace \(I_{t,\log\abs{\bar x}}\) of size \(\abs{\bar x}\), each
evaluation takes \(O(\abs{\bar x}^2)\) field operations, and
\(\abs{\bar x}=O(n+\log T)\).
\end{itemize}
This is within a polynomial of the bound \((\abs{\bar x}+\abs{M_n}\poly(f))\poly(f)\)
for the verifier of~\cite[Theorem~8.1]{BCGT13}, given
by~\cite[Remark~8.2]{BCGT13} and the proof
of~\cite[Claim~5.1]{BCGT13}. That remark and that proof state their time
bounds on the basis of the algorithms in Appendix~B of~\cite{BCGT13}
rather than a separate analysis~\cite[footnote~18]{BCGT13}. The queries
of \(V_0\) are computed from the random string
alone~\cite[Lemma~12.23]{BCGT13}.
\item \emph{Completeness.} If the instance is in
\(\mathrm{sACSP}(\mathrm{par})\), the proof \(\pi\) of
\cite[Construction~8.3]{BCGT13} is accepted with probability one by the
verifier of~\cite[Theorem~8.1]{BCGT13}. Each amplified sub-verifier is
the conjunction of runs of the single verifier on disjoint blocks of its
random string~\cite[Lemmas~C.6 and~C.7, Algorithms~10 and~11]{BCGT13}.
So every single run accepts \(\pi\) on every random string, and \(V_0\)
accepts \(\pi\) with probability one.
\item \emph{Base soundness.} Suppose the instance is not in
\(\mathrm{sACSP}(\mathrm{par})\), and let \(\pi\) be any proof. The proof
of soundness in~\cite[Theorem~8.1]{BCGT13} distinguishes four cases. In
Cases~1--3, one of the tables tested in Steps~2, 3 and~5 is at relative
distance at least \(\delta_{\rm RS}\), \(1/8\) or \(\delta_c\),
respectively, from the code tested there. Here
\(\delta_{\rm RS}=\delta_c=(8\sum_i\deg N_{t,i})^{-1}=1/(8c_N)\),
because the neighbor functions have degree one. By strong soundness
\cite[Definition~4.15]{BCGT13} and~\cite[Theorem~11.1]{BCGT13}, a single
run of \(V_{\rm RS}\) or \(V_{\rm VRS}\) on a table at relative distance
at least \(\delta\) rejects with probability at least
\(\delta/(2+K_f)\). Here
\(K_f=(2^{\eta+1}+f^{\log c})/(1-2^{-\eta})\), and \(\eta\), \(c\) are
fixed constants of that theorem. In Case~4 the argument
of~\cite{BCGT13} shows that the consistency test of Step~4 rejects with
probability at least \(1/2\). The verifier \(V_0\) runs that test
unchanged, and the argument uses only that Cases~1--3 fail. Since
\(V_0\) rejects whenever one of its steps rejects, it rejects \(\pi\)
with probability at least
\[
 \sigma_0=\frac1{8c_N(2+K_f)}\ge\frac1{\poly(\log T)}.
\]
\end{enumerate}

\emph{Step 3: amplification.} Apply \cref{lem:patch-r} to \(V_0\) with
\(s=1/n\) and an \(\varepsilon\)-biased multiset \(S\) from
\cref{lem:small-bias}(i) with \(\varepsilon^2=\sigma_0/n\). Then
\[
 \abs S<4r_0^2n/\sigma_0=\poly(n,\log T).
\]
The resulting verifier \(V_1\) uses \(r_0\) random bits. It accepts the
proof of~(c) with probability one when the instance is in
\(\mathrm{sACSP}(\mathrm{par})\), and it accepts every proof with
probability at most \(1/n\) otherwise. It runs in \(\poly(n,\log T)\)
time.

\emph{Step 4: the oracle circuit.} Let
\[
 \ell=\max\{r_0,\ceil{\log L_\pi}\},\qquad\text{so that}\qquad
 \ell=\log T+O(\log\log T).
\]
For \(R\in\{0,1\}^\ell\), consider the computation of \(V_1\) on
\(\bar x\) with the random string formed by the first \(r_0\) bits of
\(R\). It runs in \(\poly(n,\log T)\) steps, and its proof bits are read
at addresses below \(L_\pi\), written with \(\ell\) bits. Convert this
computation into a circuit in the standard way, and replace each read of
a proof bit by an oracle gate on the \(\ell\) address bits. This gives
an oracle circuit of size \(\poly(n,\log T)\) that outputs the verdict
of \(V_1\) on the proof \(\pi_O\) whose bit at address \(a<L_\pi\) is
\(O(a)\). Let \(C^O_x\) be its negation. The algorithm \(A\) computes
\(T\), \(M_n\), \(\mathrm{par}\), \(S\) and \(C^O_x\) in
\(\poly(n,\log T)\) time, using a deterministic construction of the
field~\cite[Algorithm~17]{BCGT13}.

\emph{The properties of \(C^O_x\).} If \(V(x,y)\) accepts for some \(y\)
of length \(T\), Step~1 and property~(c) give a proof \(\pi\) that
\(V_1\) accepts on every random string. The oracle \(O\) with
\(O(a)=\pi_a\) for \(a<L_\pi\) and \(O(a)=0\) otherwise makes \(C^O_x\)
unsatisfiable. If \(V(x,y)\) rejects for every \(y\) of length \(T\),
the instance is not in \(\mathrm{sACSP}(\mathrm{par})\). So for every
oracle \(O\), \(V_1\) accepts \(\pi_O\) on at most a \(1/n\) fraction of
the strings in \(\{0,1\}^{r_0}\). Hence \(C^O_x\) has at least
\[
 2^\ell(1-1/n)
\]
satisfying assignments. Each oracle gate has \(\ell\) inputs, and
\(C^O_x\) has \(\ell\) inputs and size \(\poly(n,\log T)\).

Finally, \(\mathcal V_G\) and \(T_G\) satisfy the hypotheses by
\cref{lem:succinct-verifier}(i)--(ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Non-adaptivity]\label{rem:short-pcp-nonadaptive}
The circuit \(C^O_x\) of the proof is non-adaptive: the address bits of
every oracle gate are computed from the inputs \(R\) alone
(\cite[Lemma~12.23]{BCGT13} and \cref{lem:patch-r}(i)); the oracle
answers enter only the decision.
\end{remark}

It remains to prove \cref{lem:tm-ram}, a routine construction in two
parts. A random-access machine of~\cite[Definition~6.6]{BSCGT13}
simulates \(V\) step by step. An idle phase then makes the number of
configurations exactly \(T_n\), as the bounded-halting language
of~\cite[Definition~6.14]{BSCGT13} requires.

\emph{Idea of proof.} The machine \(M_n\) keeps the tapes of \(V\) in
its memory, interleaved, and the state of \(V\) in its program counter.
After reading its input, it simulates \(V\) step by step and unpacks
witness words on demand. Each block of its code pads its own length to
a multiple of \(8\) and adds that length to a step counter. Once the
verdict is known, a counted idle loop pads the run, so that the
\texttt{out} instruction comes at exactly step \(T_n-1\).

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:tm-ram}]
\emph{Layout.} Let \(V\) have \(k_V\) tapes, counting its input and
witness tapes. Memory cell \(k_Vj+i\) of \(M_n\) holds cell \(j\) of
tape \(i\) of \(V\). The tape symbols are coded as small integers, and
the blank as zero. Registers hold the head positions. The state of \(V\)
is kept in the program counter: the code has one block of instructions
for each state.

\emph{Reading the input.} First \(M_n\) reads \(\bar x\) from its input
tape in exactly \(\abs{\bar x}/w_n\) words of \(w_n\) bits. If one of
them is the end-of-tape symbol~\#, the verdict is reject. Otherwise, by
the consistency condition of~\cite[Definition~6.10]{BSCGT13}, the words
read form \(\bar x\) itself. The machine unpacks each word with
\(O(w_n)\) instructions, deletes the final \(10^j\), and stores \(x\) on
the input tape of \(V\). This takes \(O(\abs{\bar x})=O(n+w_n)\) steps.

\emph{Simulating \(V\).} One step of \(V\) is then simulated by
\(O(k_V)\) instructions. They load the symbols under the heads, branch
on them within the block of the current state, store the written
symbols, update the head registers, and jump to the block of the new
state. The witness \(w\) is read as a sequence of \(w_n\)-bit words, a
trailing partial word padded with zeros. It is loaded on demand. When the witness head of \(V\)
reaches a cell that has not been loaded, \(M_n\) reads the next word of
its witness tape and unpacks it into the next \(w_n\) witness cells.
Once that tape is exhausted, it uses zeros instead. This costs \(O(1)\)
steps per witness cell. Since \(V\) runs at most \(T(n)\) steps, it
reads only the first \(T(n)\) witness cells, which hold \(y_w\). The
simulation of \(V\) thus ends within \(O(n+w_n+T(n))=O(T(n)+w_n)\)
steps, and its addresses are below \(k_V(T(n)+w_n+1)\).

\emph{Counting.} The code of \(M_n\) consists of blocks: the
initialization, the unpacking of one input word, the loading of one
witness word, the simulation of one step of \(V\) in each state, and the
recording of the verdict. Every path through a block is padded with
\texttt{nop} instructions so that the number of steps it takes is a
multiple of~\(8\). The count includes the \texttt{pload} step
that~\cite[Section~6.4]{BSCGT13} attach to every \texttt{load}. On that
path the block adds this number to a counter register, which is zero at
the start~\cite[Definition~6.8]{BSCGT13}. Hence, whenever a block ends,
the counter equals the number of steps made so far, and this number is
a multiple of~\(8\). This at most multiplies the running time by a
constant.

\emph{Idling.} Once the verdict is recorded in a register, \(M_n\)
idles, as~\cite{BSCGT13} prescribe (``we can always pad the computation
with enough \texttt{nop}'s''). A fixed code cannot hold a number of
\texttt{nop}s that depends on the run, so the padding is a counted loop:
an idle block of exactly \(8\) instructions. In order, they are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{add} \(8\) to the counter;
\item \texttt{cmp} the counter with the constant \(T_n-8\);
\item five \texttt{nop}s;
\item \texttt{jne} back to the start of the idle block if the comparison
found the counter different from \(T_n-8\); as the counter never exceeds
\(T_n-8\), this means smaller.
\end{itemize}
The counter takes only multiples of \(8\). Since \(T_n\) is a power of
two and at least \(16\), \(T_n-8\) is also a multiple of \(8\). So if the
counter is at most \(T_n-16\) on entry, the loop ends after the pass at
whose end exactly \(T_n-8\) steps have been made.

\emph{The verdict.} Then \(M_n\) executes six \texttt{nop}s and one
\texttt{je} on the verdict register. The \texttt{je} jumps to an
instruction \((\texttt{out},\textit{reject})\) if the verdict is
rejection, and otherwise falls through to an instruction
\((\texttt{out},\textit{accept})\). The instruction \texttt{out} takes a
constant argument, so this branch is needed. After these seven steps
exactly \(T_n-1\) steps have been made, and the \texttt{out} instruction
with the verdict is executed at step \(T_n-1\).

\emph{Constants.} A suitable constant \(c_V\ge16\) ensures three things.
\begin{itemize}
\item The simulation and the recording of the verdict end within
\(T_n-16\) steps.
\item \(\abs{\bar x}\le2(n+1)+2w_n\le T_n\).
\item \(2^{w_n}\ge2^{c_V}T(n)\) is larger than \(T_n\) and every
address.
\end{itemize}
The code \(\mathsf C_n\) is a fixed code with the constant \(T_n-8\)
written into it. Each instruction acts on \(O(1)\) words of \(w_n\) bits
by addition, comparison or bitwise operations. These have circuits over
\(\mathbb F_2\) of size and degree polynomial in \(w_n\).
\end{proof}
